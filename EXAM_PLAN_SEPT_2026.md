## What changed in the manuals, and what it means for you

1. **Every paper has an objective half, and every manual now drills it.** IFT222 carries
   both computer-based tests, all 120 questions. INS224 carries both class tests plus 21
   authored questions in the which-two form the lecturer named. CSC241 and COS221 had no
   test to transcribe, so their 70 and 51 questions were written for the manual and say so
   on their own opening page. In all four, every option is explained, not only the right
   one.
2. **INS224's exam shape in the old manual was wrong.** It described one-of-two plus
   three-of-four. The paper is an objective section plus **three compulsory written
   questions, no choice**. Read the two boxes at the front of that manual before anything
   else.
3. **The INS224 module mapping matters more than the shape.** The lecturer means the
   COURSE TEXT's modules, not the manual's parts. Compulsory Question Three is **object
   orientation and UML**, not data flow diagrams. Modules One to Five all have to be ready
   to write and to draw; only Module Six is objective-only.

## The shape of every study block

Three hours per course, in this order. Do not reorder it: the recall has to come before the
teaching or it is just reading.

1. **Cold recall, 20 minutes.** Book shut. Write down everything you can from the previous
   session on that course. Then open the book and mark yourself. What you could not produce
   is the only thing worth re-reading.
2. **Teach, 70 minutes.** The day's pages, with a pen. Every MUST MEMORISE box gets copied
   out by hand. Every RECALL AND CHECK box gets answered before you look. The **KEY POINT**
   boxes are the two-line summary of each unit: if you cannot say the blunt version from
   memory, you have not finished that unit.
3. **Do, 60 minutes.** Redo every worked example on blank paper with the book closed. Then
   the past questions in those pages: read the AS PRINTED box, do BREAK IT DOWN yourself,
   write the answer, and only then read the book's.
4. **Drill, 20 minutes.** The objective questions on those units, timed at 45 seconds each,
   answers covered.
5. **Log, 10 minutes.** Add every miss to a single running weak-spot list per course. That
   list is what you revise from 5 September, not the whole book.

**The 30 minute end-of-day session** is interleaved: five questions from each of the four
courses, mixed, from anything covered so far. Mixing is the point. It trains you to pick the
right method, which is what actually goes wrong under exam pressure.

## Rules that make this work

1. **Never read a page you have not first tried to recall.** The recall block is not a warm
   up, it is the study.
2. **Every past question is sat before it is read.** The AS PRINTED box is the question. Do
   the BREAK IT DOWN yourself, on paper, before you look at the book's version.
3. **On the objective half, kill the wrong options rather than hunting for the right one**,
   and on a which-two question count what you have marked before moving on.
4. **One weak-spot list per course**, added to daily. It is the whole of Phase 2.
5. **Write, do not type.** All four papers are written by hand under time pressure.
   Practising on a keyboard trains a different skill.
6. **If a day slips, drop that day's SECOND course teaching block and pick it up in the next
   block for that course, never the recall block and never a paper day.** With sixteen days
   there is no spare day to absorb a loss, so a slipped day has to be absorbed inside the
   remaining blocks for that course. Days 9, 10, 11 and 12 are paper days and are the last
   thing to sacrifice: sitting a paper timed is worth more than any teaching block on the
   plan.

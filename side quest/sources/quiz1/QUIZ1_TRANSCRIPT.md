## What the two misses have in common

Both wrong answers were **plausible generic self-help ideas**; both right answers
were **what the book literally says**.

- Q26: "The mind is a garden that needs watering" is a real metaphor, just not
  this book's. The book's line is "every divine deposit multiplies with use",
  drawn from the parable of the talents.
- Q21: "Focusing on technical manuals rather than wisdom" sounds like the sort
  of thing the book would say. What it actually says is that people who left
  school "read the conclusion and probably the introduction, and then close the
  book".

**The rule this yields: when two options both sound right, the one carrying the
book's own vocabulary is the key.** Distractors are written by an LLM that has
read a lot of self-help; they will sound smooth and familiar. The book's own
phrasing is often blunter, more scriptural, or oddly specific.

## The AI's question grammar, deduced

1. **Passage framing.** "The passage argues / claims / defines / uses / cites",
   "According to the teaching", "as noted in the material". The answer is always
   IN the text; nothing needs outside knowledge.
2. **Definition recall** (Q5, Q7). The book's own definition, near-verbatim.
3. **Named person recall** (Q17, Q18, Q19, Q24, Q25). Every proper name in the
   book is a target, and so is what that person said or did.
4. **Number recall** (Q24 age 93, Q25 72 years). Every number is a target.
5. **Scripture citation recall** (Q10, Q22, Q27). Both directions: "which
   scripture says X" and "what does Ephesians 1:17-18 ask for".
6. **Verbatim quote recall** (Q28, Q1). Quoted phrases come back as answers or
   as stems.
7. **Metaphor and analogy** (Q16 the building, Q26 divine deposit).
8. **Contrast and outcome** (Q9, Q12 carnal vs spiritual).
9. **Cause and reason** ("Why does the passage claim...") (Q6, Q23).

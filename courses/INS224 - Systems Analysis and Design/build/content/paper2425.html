As printedPQ 24/25 Q1
As a systems analyst working on a new library management system, you are tasked with gathering system requirements from various stakeholders. a. Identify and briefly explain two appropriate requirement elicitation techniques you would use. For each technique, justify why it is suitable for this project and specify one advantage and one potential limitation. (4 Marks) b. Based on your selected elicitation techniques, outline: i. Three functional requirements. (3 Marks) ii. Three non-functional requirements. (3 Marks) iii. List one reason why requirements elicitation should involve users from different organizational levels. (0.5 Mark) c. You are the chosen project manager for a new line of clothing about to be opened. You very much need the project to succeed and are willing to give it your all. i. The requirements of this work MAY NOT be clearly stated at the beginning. Choose an appropriate System Development Lifecycle (SDLC) methodology and map out the phases of the project. (4 Marks) ii. You are nearing the end of the project and the client is insisting on adding new features but you want to prevent scope and feature creep. Do you refuse or agree to add the new features? Give three cogent reasons for your decision as Project Manager. (3 Marks)
Break it down

Asked Elicitation techniques with justification (a); requirements at two levels plus a reason (b); a methodology choice for unclear requirements (c i); and the scope-creep decision (c ii). Modules Two, One and Six.

Watch Part a wants four things per technique: name, why it suits, one advantage, one limitation. Part c i's clue is "may not be clearly stated", which points straight at an adaptive method.

AnswerPQ 24/25 Q1

a. Two elicitation techniques (4 marks)

Interview the head librarian: suitable because they hold the borrowing rules and can be probed in depth; advantage, rich detailed information; limitation, slow and only a few people. Questionnaire to student borrowers: suitable because it cheaply reaches the large, scattered student body; advantage, wide reach at low cost; limitation, no follow-up and often a low response rate.

b. Requirements and a reason (6.5 marks)

Functional: the system shall let a member borrow a book; shall let a member reserve a book; shall calculate and record fines. Non-functional: the system shall respond within two seconds; shall be available 99.9% of the time; shall keep member data secure. Reason for multiple levels: different levels (a student, a librarian, a head of library) use the system differently, so gathering from all of them captures needs that a single level would miss.

c. Methodology and scope creep (7 marks)

i. Unclear requirements call for an adaptive method: choose prototyping or RAD, which discover requirements as they go. Map the phases: plan, then build a prototype, review it with the client, refine, and repeat until the requirements settle, then implement and support. ii. Refuse (or defer) the new features, because they threaten the agreed schedule and budget, they risk the quality of the nearly finished system, and they should be handled as a change request or a later release.

State the objectives of these phases and the activities involved in each. (8 marks) b. Name three system stakeholders and describe how each one views an information system. XYZ Conglomerate has contracted you to develop a new information system for its administrative processes. Outline the key information you would need about the business in order to design a system that effectively meets its needs. (2.5 marks)
Break it down

Watch Part a names four phases (not five): the four after planning. For each give an objective and its activities. Parts b, c, d are short recall from Modules One and Six.

AnswerPQ 24/25 Q2

a. Four phases: objectives and activities (8 marks)

Analysis: objective, understand and document what the system must do; activities, gather information, define and prioritise requirements, build prototypes, evaluate alternatives. Design: objective, specify how it will be built; activities, design the architecture, network, interfaces, database and controls. Implementation: objective, a reliable working system with trained users; activities, construct, test, convert data, train and document, install. Support: objective, keep it running for years; activities, maintain, enhance, support the users (help desk).

b. Three stakeholders (3 marks)

Users want it easy and helpful for daily work; managers want business value on time and on budget; developers want clear requirements and a feasible, maintainable system.

c. Tools versus techniques (4 marks)

A tool is a software product used to do the work; a technique is a way of completing one activity. Tools: a modelling package, an IDE. Techniques: interviewing, cost-benefit analysis.

d. Key business information for XYZ (2.5 marks)

The business goals and processes, the people and their roles, the data the business keeps, the existing systems and their weaknesses, and the rules and constraints the new system must respect.

AnswerPQ 24/25 Q3

a. DFD elements (2 marks)

External entity, process, data store, data flow.

b. Data store (1.5 marks)

A data store is data at rest in the system, a file or table where data is held between processes. It is named with a noun (for example D1 Stock), never a verb, and given a store number.

c. Reading Figure 1 (3 marks)

Read each relationship both ways from its crow's-foot marks. For example: one CUSTOMER makes many SALEs, and each SALE is made by one CUSTOMER (1:M); one CUSTOMER is targeted by many TARGETED PROMOTIONs, and each promotion targets many customers (M:N); a CUSTOMER adds many CUSTOMER FAVORITEs (1:M); an AVAILABLE TUNE is included in many CUSTOMER INTERESTs (M:N). State the cardinality (one or many, optional or mandatory) for each line the figure draws.

Answer, continued

d. Warehouse ERD and Level 0 DFD (9.5 marks)

ERD: entities TRUCK DRIVER, TRUCK, MANAGER, LABORER, STOREKEEPER, STOCK, STOCK REPORT, ACTIVITY REPORT. A TRUCK DRIVER submits many TRUCKs; a TRUCK may carry zero or many STOCK (optional); a LABORER unloads many TRUCKs; a STOREKEEPER takes inventory of STOCK and generates STOCK REPORTs; drivers and labourers submit ACTIVITY REPORTs to the MANAGER. Crow's foot throughout, with the optional (circle) mark on the truck-to-stock line.

Level 0 DFD: external entities TRUCK DRIVER and MANAGER; processes 1.0 Receive and unload truck, 2.0 Take inventory, 3.0 Generate stock report, 4.0 Submit activity report; data stores D1 Stock and D2 Reports. Wire the flows so each process has input and output, and balance them against the context diagram.

e. Validate the diagrams (1.5 marks)

Check every process has both an input and an output (no black holes or miracles); check no illegal flow (every flow touches a process); and check the levels balance (the flows in and out of a process match its explosion).

AnswerPQ 24/25 Q4

a. Four reasons to model (2 marks)

A model is a clear picture non-technical users can check; it separates what the system does from how it is built; it shows the system boundary and everything it touches; and it breaks a large system into manageable processes.

b. Cardinality, optionality, data balancing (5.5 marks)

i. In crow's foot: one-to-one (a bar at each end), one-to-many (a bar at one end, a crow's foot at the other), many-to-many (a crow's foot at each end); draw each on a line between two entity rectangles. ii. A relationship is optional when the minimum is zero, drawn with a circle on the line (a customer may have zero orders). iii. Data balancing means the data flowing into and out of a process on a parent diagram must match the data flowing into and out of its child diagram when it is exploded.

c. Process modelling versus data modelling (1.5 marks)

Process modelling (DFD)Data modelling (ERD)
Shows data in motionShows data at rest
Built from processes and flowsBuilt from entities and relationships
Answers how data movesAnswers what data is stored
Answer, continued

d. Sales management ERD (3 marks)

Entities SALES REP, CUSTOMER, ORDER, PRODUCT, WAREHOUSE. SALES REP serves zero-or-many CUSTOMERs, each CUSTOMER served by one rep; CUSTOMER places one-or-many ORDERs, each ORDER by one customer; ORDER contains one-or-many PRODUCTs; WAREHOUSE stores zero-or-many PRODUCTs. Mark the optional (circle) and many (crow's foot) ends as the words say.

e. Data cannot move between entities (1 mark)

A data flow must touch a process at one end, so you cannot draw an arrow straight from one external entity rectangle to another: put a process bubble between them. For example, Customer to Bank is wrong; Customer to "1.0 Process payment" to Bank is right.

f. Researcher ERD (3 marks)

Entities RESEARCHER, INSTITUTION, RESEARCH INTEREST. A RESEARCHER belongs to one INSTITUTION (many researchers per institution, so INSTITUTION 1:M RESEARCHER); a RESEARCHER has many RESEARCH INTERESTs (1:M, or M:N if interests are shared). Crow's foot with the one bar at the institution end.

g. Acquisition strategy (1.5 marks)

With only one month, buy a packaged solution or outsource, not build. Factors: the very short time available, the lack of in-house build capacity in the time, and that a hospital admin system is not so unique that a package cannot fit.

AnswerPQ 24/25 Q5

a. Three skills (7 marks)

Technical: understanding of computers, networks, databases and the tools of building, so the analyst can understand and communicate technical requirements. Business: understanding of how organisations work and what this business does, so the system fits real goals. People (interpersonal): the most important; communicating in interviews and reviews, managing expectations and resolving conflict, because the analyst depends on users, managers and programmers.

Polymorphism: the same method behaves differently on different classes (draw() on a Circle versus a Square). Inheritance: a subclass takes on a superclass's members (SavingsAccount from Account). Composition: a life-or-death has-a (a House of Rooms). Aggregation: a loose has-a (a Team of Players).

AnswerPQ 24/25 Q6

a. The analyst as problem solver (5.5 marks)

The analyst studies a business to find where it loses time, money or accuracy, works out whether technology can help, and specifies the system that solves the problem. The solution is usually an information system, but not always. The analyst researches and understands the problem, verifies the benefit outweighs the cost, defines requirements, develops alternatives, recommends the best, details it, implements and monitors, which is exactly a problem-solving sequence. That is why the analyst is a business problem solver and the bridge between users and the technical team.

(yes leads to "Forward to accounts" then end), then decision "change or cancel?": if so "Find and cancel appointment"; otherwise "Match desired times to available dates" and "Schedule appointment"; end.

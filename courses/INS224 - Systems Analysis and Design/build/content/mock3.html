AnswerMock 3 · Q1

a. Entities CUSTOMER, RENTAL, CAR, STAFF. CUSTOMER 1:M RENTAL; CAR 1:M RENTAL; STAFF 1:M RENTAL. Crow's foot throughout.

b. Context: one process "0 Rental" between CUSTOMER and STAFF. Level 0: 1.0 Make rental, 2.0 Take payment, 3.0 Return car, stores D1 Rentals, D2 Cars. Level 1 of 2.0: validate, charge, receipt, balanced.

c. Succeed: clear requirements, user involvement, realistic schedule. Fail: unclear requirements, no user involvement, weak management support.

d. One customer, many rentals: forward, one CUSTOMER has many RENTALs; backward, one RENTAL belongs to one CUSTOMER.

AnswerMock 3 · Q2

a. Classes PATIENT, PRESCRIPTION, INPATIENTPRESCRIPTION, OUTPATIENTPRESCRIPTION, PHARMACIST, DRUG. INPATIENT and OUTPATIENT generalise to PRESCRIPTION (hollow triangle). PATIENT 1 to * PRESCRIPTION; PHARMACIST dispenses * PRESCRIPTION; PRESCRIPTION * to * DRUG (via a line item).

b. Actors PATIENT, PHARMACIST; use cases Present prescription, Dispense drug, Record. "Dispense" includes "Check stock".

c. Start, "Receive prescription", decision "in stock?" (no ends, yes continues), "Dispense", "Record", end.

d. A class is a blueprint (Drug); an object is an instance (the specific batch of Paracetamol with batch number B21).

AnswerMock 3 · Q5

a. Waterfall (documented, rigid), prototyping (unclear needs, mistaken for finished), RAD (fast, weak on large), spiral (risk analysis, heavy), incremental (early value, integration), agile (handles change, light docs), V-shape (test pairing, reliable, rigid). A tax system needing security and audit trails suits V-shape or a disciplined incremental approach.

b. Define the problem, schedule, confirm feasibility, staff, launch.

c. Business: cut filing time, ensure audit trails. User: a citizen files online, an officer reviews a return. Functional: accept a return, compute tax due, issue a receipt. Non-functional: high availability, strong encryption. System: store returns as time-stamped records keyed by tax ID, integrate with the payment gateway.

AnswerMock 3 · Q6

a. As-is: separate, manual campus services, no single view, no analytics. To-be: one platform linking attendance, library and cafeteria. Assumptions: students carry compatible cards or phones, services can integrate, the network covers campus.

b. Interview service heads (rules, depth), questionnaire to students (needs, reach), observe a service point (real use), review existing systems (data, integration).

c. UML: a standard object-oriented modelling notation by Booch, Rumbaugh and Jacobson; used to visualise, specify, construct and document.

d. Structural (class, object) and behavioural (use case, activity/sequence).

e. Inheritance (triangle, is-a), composition (filled diamond), aggregation (hollow diamond), association (plain line).

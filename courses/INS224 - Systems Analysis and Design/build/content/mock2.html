AnswerMock 2 · Q1

a. Entities GUEST, BOOKING, ROOM, RECEPTIONIST. GUEST 1:M BOOKING; ROOM 1:M BOOKING; RECEPTIONIST 1:M BOOKING. Crow's foot throughout.

b. Context: one process "0 Reservation" between GUEST and RECEPTIONIST. Level 0: 1.0 Make booking, 2.0 Take payment, 3.0 Confirm, with stores D1 Bookings, D2 Rooms. Level 1 of 2.0: validate, charge, receipt, balanced.

c. Succeed: clear requirements, user involvement, realistic schedule. Fail: unclear requirements, no user involvement, weak management support.

d. One at the guest end, many at booking: forward, one GUEST makes many BOOKINGs; backward, one BOOKING is made by one GUEST.

AnswerMock 2 · Q2

a. Classes RIDER, STANDARDRIDER, PREMIUMRIDER, DRIVER, TRIP, PAYMENT. STANDARDRIDER and PREMIUMRIDER generalise to RIDER (hollow triangle). RIDER 1 to * TRIP; DRIVER 1 to * TRIP; TRIP 1 to 1 PAYMENT.

b. Actors RIDER, DRIVER; use cases Request trip, Assign driver, Complete trip, Pay. "Complete trip" includes "Take payment".

c. Start, "Request trip", decision "driver available?" (no ends, yes continues), "Assign driver", "Make trip", "Take payment", end.

d. The diamond problem (ambiguity), increased complexity, name clashes.

AnswerMock 2 · Q5

a. As in the manual: waterfall (documented, rigid), prototyping (unclear needs, mistaken for finished), RAD (fast, weak on large), spiral (risk analysis, heavy), incremental (early value, integration), agile (handles change, light docs), V-shape (test pairing, reliable, rigid). A tracking system needing audit trails and nine-month delivery suits incremental or V-shape.

b. Define the problem, schedule, confirm feasibility, staff, launch.

c. Business: faster delivery, full audit trail. User: a dispatcher assigns a parcel, a customer tracks live. Functional: register parcel, update status, notify customer. Non-functional: high availability, encrypt data. System: store status as time-stamped events keyed by parcel ID, integrate with the customer database.

AnswerMock 2 · Q6

a. As-is: no online lectures, manual grading, no analytics. To-be: online lectures, assignments, automated grading. Assumptions: students have connectivity, the platform integrates with existing records, the fee portal is available.

b. Interview lecturers (requirements, depth), questionnaire to students (needs, reach), observe a class (real use), review the existing system (data, integration).

c. UML: a standard object-oriented modelling notation by Booch, Rumbaugh and Jacobson; used to visualise, specify, construct and document.

d. Structural (class, object) and behavioural (use case, activity/sequence).

e. Inheritance (triangle, is-a), composition (filled diamond), aggregation (hollow diamond), association (plain line).

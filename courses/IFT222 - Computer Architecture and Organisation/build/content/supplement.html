Boolean Algebra and K-maps

Simplifying logic expressions, by algebra and by Karnaugh map, and turning them into gate circuits.

Not in the current syllabus

The 2024/25 and 2025/26 papers do not ask Boolean algebra or K-maps. Older papers (2020/21, 2023/24) do. Learn this only after the graded topics are secure; it is here so no past question is left unanswerable.

The operations and the laws

TeachBoolean basics

Three operations: AND (written as a product, AB, true only if both), OR (written as a sum, A+B, true if either), and NOT (written A' or A with a bar, the opposite). The useful laws for simplifying:

  • Identity: A + 0 = A, A · 1 = A.   Null: A + 1 = 1, A · 0 = 0.
  • Complement: A + A' = 1, A · A' = 0.
  • Absorption: A + AB = A, and A + A'B = A + B.
  • De Morgan: (AB)' = A' + B', and (A + B)' = A'B'.

Each of those is a law, and a paper may ask you to state one rather than use it. Stated in words:

The laws, stated

De Morgan's laws. The complement of a product is the sum of the complements, and the complement of a sum is the product of the complements. Formally, for any Boolean variables A and B: (A · B)' = A' + B' and (A + B)' = A' · B'. In words: breaking a bar over a group flips the operator underneath it, AND becoming OR and OR becoming AND.

The complement law. A variable ORed with its own complement is always 1, and ANDed with its own complement is always 0: A + A' = 1, A · A' = 0. This is the law that does the work in most simplifications, because it lets a variable and its opposite cancel out of a pair of terms.

The identity and null laws. ORing with 0 and ANDing with 1 leave a variable unchanged (A + 0 = A, A · 1 = A); ORing with 1 forces 1 and ANDing with 0 forces 0 (A + 1 = 1, A · 0 = 0).

The absorption law. A term already implied by a simpler one adds nothing: A + AB = A. A related form removes a redundant complement: A + A'B = A + B.

Worked exampleMethod

Simplify F = A'BC + AB'C + ABC' + ABC by algebra.

Given F = A'BC + AB'C + ABC' + ABC · three variables A, B, C
Find the simplest equivalent expression
Formula X + X' = 1 · X × 1 = X · X + X = X (a term may be reused), then factor common pairs
what each symbol means A' = NOT A, the complement · writing two letters together (AB) means AND · + means OR · X + X' = 1 is the law doing all the work: a variable and its complement together cover both cases, so the variable drops out
  1. Group the last two by AB: ABC' + ABC = AB(C' + C) = AB.
  2. Take ABC again (it can be reused) with AB'C by AC: AB'C + ABC = AC(B' + B) = AC.
  3. Take ABC again with A'BC by BC: A'BC + ABC = BC(A' + A) = BC.
  4. So F = AB + AC + BC.
F = AB + AC + BC. This is the majority function: it is 1 when at least two of A, B, C are 1.

Now redo it: the trick is that a minterm may be reused in more than one group. That is exactly what a K-map makes visual, next.

The Karnaugh map

A K-map arranges the truth table so that cells differing in one variable are neighbours. Circle groups of 1s in powers of two (1, 2, 4, 8), as large as possible; each group drops the variable that changes across it. The circled groups, ORed together, are the simplified expression.

Worked exampleMethod

Simplify the same F on a 3-variable K-map (rows A, columns BC in Gray-code order). F = 1 at minterms 3, 5, 6, 7.

Given the same F · 3 variables · F = 1 at minterms 3, 5, 6, 7 · map = rows A, columns BC in Gray-code order
Find the simplified expression, by grouping
Formula circle the largest groups of adjacent 1s in powers of two (1, 2, 4, 8); each group drops the variable that changes across it; OR the surviving groups together
what each symbol means minterm = the decimal number of one input combination, so minterm 3 is A=0, B=1, C=1 · Gray-code order = columns run 00, 01, 11, 10 so that neighbours differ by one bit, which is what makes adjacency mean simplification · groups may overlap, and a variable that is 0 in part of a group and 1 in the rest is the one that cancels
The three circled pairs give BC, AC and AB, so F = AB + AC + BC, the same answer as the algebra, reached by eye.
TeachFrom expression to circuit

To draw the logic circuit for F = AB + AC + BC: one AND gate per product term (AB, AC, BC), then one OR gate combining the three AND outputs into F. A sum-of-products expression always becomes a layer of AND gates feeding one OR gate. Don't-care outputs (marked d or X) may be treated as 0 or 1, whichever makes a larger group, so use them to simplify further.

Recall and check

On a K-map, what does a group of two adjacent 1s tell you, and why circle the largest groups?

Answer

A group of two drops the one variable that changes across it, leaving a product of the rest. Larger groups drop more variables, giving fewer and simpler terms, so always circle the biggest power-of-two groups you can.

Key point

A Karnaugh map is a truth table with its rows rearranged so that any two cells sharing an edge differ in exactly one variable. That is the whole trick: adjacency becomes algebraic cancellation, so grouping neighbouring ones is the same operation as applying the law that a variable and its complement sum to one.

Blunt version: it is a truth table folded so that neighbours differ by one bit. Grouping is cancelling.

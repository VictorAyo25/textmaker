Foundations and Data

Signed Numbers

How a computer stores a negative number when all it has is 0s and 1s. There is no minus key in binary, so a sign has to be built out of bits. Three schemes were invented; one of them (two's complement) is what every real machine uses. This is the single most asked numeric topic, on the paper and in the objective test, so we go slowly and make it click, not just stick.

TeachStart here: no sign, then a sign bit

First, with no sign at all (call it unsigned), n bits count plainly from 0 up to 2n − 1. So 8 bits hold 0 to 255, and 10 bits hold 0 to 1023. Every pattern is a positive number, and the biggest is all ones.

To make room for negatives, we give up the top (leftmost) bit and use it as a sign flag: 0 means positive, 1 means negative. The bits below it say how much. That is the easy part. The only real question, and the whole topic, is how you build the negative pattern. There are three answers, and they only differ in that one step.

TeachThree ways to build a negative

Take +42 in 8 bits, which is 0010 1010, and make it negative three ways:

  • Sign-magnitude (the obvious one). Just flip the sign bit and leave the size alone: write a 1 for "negative", then 42. So −42 is 1 then 010 1010 = 1010 1010. Easy to read. The catch: the hardware for adding is slow and messy (the sign and the size are handled separately), and there are two zeros, a +0 (0000 0000) and a −0 (1000 0000), which is untidy.
  • One's complement (flip). To negate, invert every bit of the positive. So −42 is 0010 1010 flipped = 1101 0101. The catch: adding needs a fix called an end-around carry (a carry off the top has to be added back in at the bottom), and it still has two zeros (+0 = all zeros, −0 = all ones).
  • Two's complement (flip and add one). To negate, invert every bit and add 1. So −42 is 1101 0101 + 1 = 1101 0110. This is the one real computers use, and the next box shows why it is worth the extra "add one".
TeachWhy two's complement wins: the odometer

Here is the idea that makes two's complement click. Picture the 8 bits as a car odometer that counts 0000 0000, 0000 0001, and on up to 1111 1111, then rolls back to 0000 0000. Now run it one click backwards from zero: 0000 0000 rolls back to 1111 1111. So the pattern just below zero is all ones, and it makes perfect sense to call it −1. One more click back, 1111 1110, is −2, and so on. Negatives are just the patterns you land on counting down from the top.

That single wrap is the whole payoff: there is now one zero, not two, and an ordinary adder that is allowed to overflow and wrap round does subtraction for free (a − b is just a + (−b)). That is why the hardware is simpler and faster.

So why "flip and add 1"? It is the shortcut for counting all the way down. Flipping alone (one's complement) lands you one short; the + 1 closes the gap. Prove it to yourself: +42 is 0010 1010, flip to 1101 0101, add 1 to get 1101 0110. Add that back to +42 and the 8 bits roll over to 0000 0000, which is zero. A number and its negative adding to zero is exactly what "negative" should mean, so 1101 0110 really is −42.

Must doThe four two's-complement moves

Every question is one of these four. Learn them until your hand does them without you.

  • Write a negative: write the positive in n bits, flip every bit, add 1.
  • Read a pattern whose top bit is 1 (so you know it is negative): flip every bit, add 1 to get the size, then put a minus sign in front. (Example: 1000 0000 in 8 bits flips to 0111 1111, plus 1 is 1000 0000 = 128, so the value is −128.)
  • Range in n bits: −2n−1 to 2n−1 − 1. For 16 bits that is −32 768 to 32 767 (one more negative than positive, because zero uses up a positive slot).
  • Add two numbers: just add the bit patterns and throw away any carry off the top. No end-around carry; that belongs to one's complement.

Memory hook: one's complement is "flip"; two's complement is "flip and add one". That extra "add one" is what buys the single zero and the simple hardware.

Worked examplethe method, vertically

Store −182, 487 and −1023 in a 16-bit two's-complement machine (binary and hex) and add (−182) + 487. The exam sets exactly this; you will sit it verbatim in the final mock at the back. Here is how you write it on paper, one move at a time.

Given 16 bits, two's complement · −182, 487, −1023
Formula range = −215 to 215 − 1 · negative = flip the positive and add 1 · group 4 bits per hex digit
Range: -2¹⁵ to 2¹⁵ - 1  =  -32768 to 32767   (all three fit)

487  =  0000 0001 1110 0111  =  01E7

-182:  +182  =  0000 0000 1011 0110
     invert  =  1111 1111 0100 1001
        + 1  =  1111 1111 0100 1010  =  FF4A

-1023: +1023 =  0000 0011 1111 1111
     invert  =  1111 1100 0000 0000
        + 1  =  1111 1100 0000 0001  =  FC01

(-182) + 487:
     1111 1111 0100 1010
   + 0000 0001 1110 0111
   ---------------------
   1 0000 0001 0011 0001   drop the carry off the top
     0000 0001 0011 0001   =  256+32+16+1  =  305
Range −32768 to 32767 (all fit); 487 = 01E7, −182 = FF4A, −1023 = FC01; (−182) + 487 = 305.

Now redo it: positive, invert, add 1, every time. Practise with −75 in 8 bits (answer 1011 0101).

Your turnread three patterns

In 8 bits: (1) what decimal is 1000 0000 in two's complement? (2) what is the two's complement of 0010 1100? (3) what decimal is 1010 1010 in sign-magnitude?

Answer

(1) top bit 1 so negative; flip to 0111 1111, add 1 = 1000 0000 = 128, so −128. (2) flip 0010 1100 to 1101 0011, add 1 = 1101 0100. (3) sign bit 1 (negative), magnitude 010 1010 = 42, so −42.

Your turnMCQ, quick facts

(1) How many representations of zero in sign-magnitude? (2) Which scheme needs an end-around carry? (3) The one's-complement pattern 1110 0011 is which decimal? (4) Why is two's complement used?

Answer

(1) two (+0 and −0). (2) one's complement. (3) top bit 1 so negative; flip 1110 0011 to 0001 1100 = 28, so −28. (4) simpler and faster hardware (one zero, and add and subtract share one circuit).

Trap

Reading a negative two's-complement pattern, do not just read the magnitude bits off; you must flip and add 1 first. Overflow means the true result is too big or too small for the range, not division by zero. And the end-around carry belongs to one's complement only; two's complement simply discards the top carry.

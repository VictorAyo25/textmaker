Foundations and Data

Signed Numbers

How a computer stores negative numbers. Three schemes, one of which (two's complement) is what real machines use. This is the single most asked numeric topic, on the paper and in the objective test, so learn the moves until they are automatic.

TeachUnsigned first

With n bits and no sign, you can store 0 to 2n − 1. So 8 bits hold 0 to 255, and 10 bits hold 0 to 1023. All patterns are positive; the biggest is all ones.

TeachThree ways to store a sign

The top (leftmost) bit becomes a sign bit: 0 means positive, 1 means negative. The three schemes differ only in how they build a negative:

  • Sign-magnitude: sign bit, then the plain magnitude. So −42 in 8 bits is 1 (negative) then 0101010 (42) = 1010 1010. Simple to read, but arithmetic hardware is slow and messy, and there are two zeros (+0 = 0000 0000 and −0 = 1000 0000).
  • One's complement: to make a number negative, invert every bit of the positive. Adding needs an end-around carry (any carry out of the top is added back into the bottom), and it too has two zeros (+0 = all zeros, −0 = all ones).
  • Two's complement: to make a number negative, invert every bit and add 1. This is what real computers use, because it has one zero and the same adder does subtraction, so the hardware is simpler and faster.
Must doThe two's-complement moves
  • Make a negative: write the positive in n bits, invert all bits, add 1.
  • Read a pattern whose top bit is 1 (so it is negative): invert all bits and add 1 to get the magnitude, then put a minus sign on it. (Example: 1000 0000 in 8 bits inverts to 0111 1111, plus 1 is 1000 0000 = 128, so the value is −128.)
  • Range in n bits: −2n−1 to 2n−1 − 1. For 16 bits that is −32 768 to 32 767.
  • Add two's-complement numbers: just add the bit patterns and throw away any carry off the top. No end-around carry.

Memory hook: one's complement is "flip"; two's complement is "flip and add one". That extra "add one" is what buys the single zero and the simple hardware.

Worked examplethe method, vertically

Store −182, 487 and −1023 in a 16-bit two's-complement machine (binary and hex) and add (−182) + 487. The exam sets exactly this; you will sit it verbatim in the final mock at the back. Here is how you write it on paper.

Given 16 bits, two's complement · −182, 487, −1023
Formula range = −215 to 215 − 1 · negative = invert the positive and add 1 · group 4 bits per hex digit
Range: -2^15 to 2^15 - 1  =  -32768 to 32767   (all three fit)

487  =  0000 0001 1110 0111  =  01E7

-182:  +182  =  0000 0000 1011 0110
     invert  =  1111 1111 0100 1001
        + 1  =  1111 1111 0100 1010  =  FF4A

-1023: +1023 =  0000 0011 1111 1111
     invert  =  1111 1100 0000 0000
        + 1  =  1111 1100 0000 0001  =  FC01

(-182) + 487:
     1111 1111 0100 1010
   + 0000 0001 1110 0111
   ---------------------
   1 0000 0001 0011 0001   drop the carry off the top
     0000 0001 0011 0001   =  256+32+16+1  =  305
Range −32768 to 32767 (all fit); 487 = 01E7, −182 = FF4A, −1023 = FC01; (−182) + 487 = 305.

Now redo it: positive, invert, add 1, every time. Practise with −75 in 8 bits (answer 1011 0101).

Your turnread three patterns

In 8 bits: (1) what decimal is 1000 0000 in two's complement? (2) what is the two's complement of 0010 1100? (3) what decimal is 1010 1010 in sign-magnitude?

Answer

(1) top bit 1 so negative; invert 0111 1111 + 1 = 1000 0000 = 128, so −128. (2) invert 0010 1100 to 1101 0011, add 1 = 1101 0100. (3) sign bit 1 (negative), magnitude 010 1010 = 42, so −42.

Your turnMCQ, quick facts

(1) How many representations of zero in sign-magnitude? (2) Which scheme needs an end-around carry? (3) The one's-complement pattern 1110 0011 is which decimal? (4) Why is two's complement used?

Answer

(1) two (+0 and −0). (2) one's complement. (3) top bit 1 so negative; invert 1110 0011 to 0001 1100 = 28, so −28. (4) simpler and faster hardware (one zero, and add/subtract share one circuit).

Trap

Reading a negative two's-complement pattern, do not just read the magnitude bits; you must invert and add 1 first. Overflow is when the true result is too big or too small for the range, not division by zero. And the end-around carry belongs to one's complement only; two's complement just discards the top carry.

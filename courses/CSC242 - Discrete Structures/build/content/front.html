Before you start

How to use this manual

Read this page once. It will save you hours, and it explains one thing about this course that nothing else will tell you.

The thing you need to know first

This course has an official course text of 161 pages. It does not contain the exam. That is not a complaint, it is a measurement. Every past paper leans heavily on graph theory, trees, the properties of relations, ordered sets and Boolean algebra. The official text covers none of them. Searched end to end, it contains the word "vertex" zero times, "reflexive" zero times, "adjacency" zero times, "Handshaking" zero times, "planar" zero times. Its five uses of the word "graph" are all about plotting a function, and its twelve uses of "tree" all belong to one worked example about trees growing in a street.

Meanwhile, on the 2025/2026 paper, roughly two marks in every five come from exactly that missing material. A student who revises only the course text walks into the hall having never seen two fifths of the paper.

So this manual teaches both: everything the course text covers, and everything the papers demand that it does not. Wherever a topic falls in the second group, it is flagged like this, once, at the top of the section:

Not in the course text
The official 161-page text does not cover this topic at all, and the exam tests it every year. Everything you need is taught here from zero. If you go back to the course text to revise this, you will not find it. That is the text's gap, not yours.

How every past question is presented

Past questions are the whole point of a manual like this, and they are easy to present dishonestly. If a book tidies a question into its own neat prose, it has done the hardest half of the work for you, namely working out what is being asked, and left you only the arithmetic. Worse, it hides the examiner's habits of phrasing, which are exactly what you are training to recognise, and it quietly repairs the paper's own mistakes, so you never learn that papers contain mistakes.

This manual therefore never restates a past question. Every one arrives in three parts, in this order.

How to work through it

Work with a pen and blank paper. When a worked example ends, cover it and redo it from the question. When you reach a past paper, sit it under exam conditions before you read a single answer: three hours, four questions, no notes. A paper you have read the answers to teaches you far less than one you have struggled through and then marked.

The parts are ordered so that nothing is used before it is taught. If you meet a symbol you do not recognise, it was introduced earlier, and the Formula and Notation Reference at the back lists every one.

TrapBefore you start revising

You choose four questions out of six, so it is tempting to plan on dodging a topic. Look at what that costs on the 2025/2026 paper: graphs appear in Q1, Q4, Q5 and Q6, and counting appears in Q3, Q4 and Q6. There is no set of four questions you can answer while avoiding graph theory, and no honest way to avoid counting either.

The choice protects you against a bad day on one topic. It does not let you leave one out.

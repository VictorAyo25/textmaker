Part Ten · 10.1

Descriptive Statistics

Given a list of numbers, describe it in one or two numbers. Where is the middle, and how spread out is it? Five measures answer both questions, and each is a formula you can compute by hand in under a minute.

By the end of this section you will be able to compute the mean, median, mode, range, variance and standard deviation of a data set, choose between the mean and the median when the data is skewed, work from a frequency table, and say what each measure does and does not tell you.

Named on the syllabus, missing from the course text
The course outline lists a module called Descriptive Statistics. Its unit contains no statistics: it is propositional logic from the first line to the last, the same material this manual teaches in Part One. No past paper in the five available sessions has asked a statistics question either. So the risk is small but not nil: the topic is named, and a named topic can appear. Measures of the middle

Must memoriseMean, median, mode

Three answers to "where is the centre", and they can disagree violently on the same data.

MeasureHow to compute itWhat it is
Meanadd every value, divide by how many there arethe balance point
Mediansort, then take the middle value; with an even count take the mean of the middle twothe halfway mark by position
Modethe value occurring most oftenthe most popular value

In symbols, for values x1, x2, …, xn:

x̄ = (∑ xi) / n

Where xi is the i-th data value, so the subscript is a position in the list, as with a sequence in section 9.3. n is the number of values in the set, not the largest of them. ∑ xi is the total of all the values, the summation of section 9.3 with i running from 1 to n. x̄ is written x with a bar above it and is read "x bar". It is the standard symbol for the mean of a sample.

The mode may not be unique, and it may not exist in any useful sense: a set where every value occurs once has no mode, and a set with two equally popular values has two. Say so rather than picking one.

The median needs the data sorted first. Taking the middle of an unsorted list is the most common error in this topic, and it produces a number that means nothing.

Memory hook: mean is the average, median is the middle, mode is the most. All three start with m, and so do average, middle and most, in the same order.

Measures of spread

Must memoriseRange, variance, standard deviation

The centre alone hides everything. The sets 19, 20, 21 and 0, 20, 40 have the same mean and nothing else in common.

range = largest value − smallest value
σ2 = (∑ (xi − x̄)2) / n
σ = √(σ2)

Where xi − x̄ is a deviation: how far one value sits from the mean. Deviations are positive above the mean and negative below it. the square is there because the deviations always add to exactly zero, so they must be made positive before summing; squaring does that and also weights the far-out values more heavily. σ2 is the variance, the mean of the squared deviations. Its units are the square of the data's units, which is why it is rarely quoted on its own. σ is the standard deviation, the square root of the variance, back in the same units as the data. The symbol is a lower-case Greek sigma, and it is not the summation sign, which is the capital. n is the number of values.

Dividing by n or by n − 1. The formula above divides by n and describes the data set you have. If the data is a sample drawn from a larger population and you want to estimate that population's spread, divide by n − 1 instead and write s2 and s. Both are correct answers to different questions, so state which you are computing; this is the same discipline as the indexing convention in section 9.3.

Memory hook: square the distances, average them, then take the root to come home. The root is only there to undo the squaring.

Worked exampleMethod · all six measures on one data set
Given
Ten test scores: 21, 15, 33, 12, 21, 18, 24, 15, 20, 21.
Find
Mean, median, mode, range, variance and standard deviation.
Formula
x̄ = (∑ xi) / n and σ2 = (∑ (xi − x̄)2) / n, with n = 10.

Step 1. Sort the data, because three of the six measures need it and none of them is harmed by it:

12, 15, 15, 18, 20, 21, 21, 21, 24, 33

Step 2. The mean.

∑ xi = 12 + 15 + 15 + 18 + 20 + 21 + 21 + 21 + 24 + 33 = 200
x̄ = 200 / 10 = 20

Step 3. The median. There are 10 values, an even count, so the middle two are the 5th and 6th: 20 and 21.

median = (20 + 21) / 2 = 20.5

Step 4. The mode. Counting occurrences: 12 once, 15 twice, 18 once, 20 once, 21 three times, 24 once, 33 once.

mode = 21

Step 5. The range.

range = 33 − 12 = 21

Step 6. The variance. Tabulate the deviations, because doing this in your head is how arithmetic errors happen.

Reading the answer back. Scores centre on about 20 and typically sit within about 5.5 of it. The one score of 33 is more than two standard deviations out, which is what pulls the mean above the median. Saying that sentence is what turns six numbers into a description.

TrapWhen the mean is the wrong answer

In the data above the mean is 20 and the median 20.5. They are close because one outlier is mild. Change the 33 to 133 and watch:

new total = 300, so the mean becomes 30
the median is unchanged at 20.5, because the middle two values did not move

The mean has left the data: nine of the ten scores are now below it. The median is resistant to outliers and the mean is not. That is the single most useful fact in this section, and the reason incomes and house prices are reported as medians.

What to write in an exam. If a data set has an extreme value, say which measure you are quoting and why. "The median is quoted here because the value 133 distorts the mean" earns the mark that "average = 30" does not.

The mode's own trap: it is the only one of the three that works on data with no numeric order at all. The most common programming language in a class has a mode, but no mean.

The number of programming errors found in each of 20 submitted assignments, as a frequency table.

The mean, median and mode.

The mean.

x̄ = 36 / 20 = 1.8 errors per assignment

The median. With 20 values the middle two are the 10th and 11th. The running count reaches 8 after the value 1 and 15 after the value 2, so both the 10th and the 11th value are 2.

median = 2

The mode is the value with the largest frequency, which is 2, occurring 7 times.

Note what the frequency column saves you. Writing out all twenty values and adding them would give the same 36; the table is the same arithmetic with the repetitions collected, exactly as the arrangements formula of section 5.2 collects repeated letters.

A mean need not be a possible value. No assignment had 1.8 errors. The mean describes the set, not any member of it, and saying so pre-empts the marker's follow-up question.

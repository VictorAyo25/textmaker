Question Paper

Time: three hours. Answer any FOUR questions. All questions carry 17.5 marks. Total 70 marks.

Read this firstThese are not the examiner's words

Every question on this paper was written for this manual. Not one of them is quoted from a real paper, and that is why no question here carries an As printed bar: that bar is reserved for the examiner's own words, and nothing on these pages is the examiner's. What this paper copies is the shape of the 2025/2026 paper, six questions of 17.5 marks with any four to be answered in three hours, and the mix of topics that all five papers we hold have settled into.

When you want the real wording, go to the Past Papers part. All five papers are quoted there exactly as printed and answered in full. Sit this mock first, then those.

How to use it. Time yourself. Spend the first two minutes adding up the printed marks of the six questions and choosing your four, by the method in R.2. Then give each question forty minutes and stop when the forty minutes are gone, even mid-sentence. Marking your own half-finished answer teaches you more about your pace than a leisurely complete one does.

Question One17.5 marks

(a) Explain, with two examples in each case, the difference between:

(i) a tautology and a contradiction
(ii) a subset and a proper subset
(iii) an injective function and a surjective function
(iv) an Euler circuit and a Hamilton circuit
(v) a permutation and a combination  (10 marks)

(b) State De Morgan's laws for sets, and verify one of them using U = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8}, A = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} and B = {4, 5, 6, 7}.  (2.5 marks)

(c) For the same A and B, find A ∪ B, A ∩ B, A − B, B − A, and the number of subsets of A ∩ B.  (5 marks)

Question Two17.5 marks

(a) In a class of 120 students, 60 offer Mathematics, 55 offer Physics and 50 offer Chemistry. 25 offer both Mathematics and Physics, 20 offer both Physics and Chemistry, 22 offer both Mathematics and Chemistry, and 10 offer all three. Draw the Venn diagram and find the number of students who offer (i) at least one of the three subjects, (ii) none of the three, (iii) exactly one of the three.  (7 marks)

(b) A relation R is defined on the set {1, 2, 3, 4} by a R b if and only if a − b is even. Show that R is an equivalence relation and give the partition it induces.  (5.5 marks)

(c) Let S = {(1,1), (1,2), (2,3), (3,4)} on the set {1, 2, 3, 4}. State, with a reason in each case, whether S is reflexive, symmetric, antisymmetric and transitive.  (5 marks)

Question Three17.5 marks

(a) How many distinct arrangements are there of the letters of the word STATISTICS?  (3 marks)

(b) A committee of 5 is to be formed from 7 men and 6 women. In how many ways can this be done if (i) there is no restriction, (ii) exactly 3 women must serve, (iii) at least 4 men must serve?  (6 marks)

(c) A password is 5 characters long, drawn from the 26 capital letters and the 10 digits with repetition allowed, and it must begin with a letter. How many such passwords are there?  (3.5 marks)

(d) Write a C++ program that reads a positive integer n and prints all the positive divisors of n on one line, and state what your program prints when n = 28.  (5 marks)

Question Four17.5 marks

(a) Write short notes, with one example each, on:

(i) a complete graph
(ii) a bipartite graph
(iii) a regular graph
(iv) a subgraph
(v) an Eulerian graph  (10 marks)

(b) A graph G on the vertices v1 to v5 has the adjacency matrix below. Draw G, write down its degree sequence, and verify the handshaking theorem for it.  (4 marks)

(c) A full binary tree has 8 leaves. How many internal vertices, how many vertices altogether, and how many edges does it have? State the formula you use.  (3.5 marks)

Question Five17.5 marks

(a) Construct a truth table for (p → q) ∧ (q → r) → (p → r) and say what the table establishes.  (5 marks)

(b) Simplify the Boolean expression F = A·B + A·B′ + A′·B, naming the law used at each step.  (4 marks)

(c) A machine starts only when at least two of its three safety switches are closed. Write the Boolean expression for the start signal, give its truth table, and draw the circuit.  (5 marks)

(d) Use a truth table to verify that ¬(p ∧ q) is logically equivalent to ¬p ∨ ¬q, and name the law.  (3.5 marks)

Question Six17.5 marks

(a) Prove by mathematical induction that

12 + 22 + 32 + … + n2 = n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6

for every positive integer n.  (6 marks)

(b) Prove by contraposition that if n2 is even then n is even, where n is an integer.  (3 marks)

(c) Solve the recurrence relation an = 6an−1 − 8an−2 given a0 = 3 and a1 = 10.  (5 marks)

(d) A box holds 5 red and 7 blue balls. Two balls are drawn one after the other without replacement. Find the probability that (i) both are red, (ii) at least one is blue.  (3.5 marks)

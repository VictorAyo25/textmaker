Mock Examination Three

The Hardest of
the Three

Harder than anything the five papers have set, on purpose. Every question here asks you to prove something or to rule something out, rather than to compute a number and stop. If you can pass this paper in three hours you are over-prepared for the real one, which is the only comfortable place to be on the morning of an examination.
Mock Examination Three

Question Paper

Time: three hours. Answer any FOUR questions. All questions carry 17.5 marks. Total 70 marks.

Read this firstDeliberately harder than the real thing

Nothing here is quoted from a real examination, and this paper is harder than any of the five we hold. That is deliberate, and it is the one respect in which this mock does not imitate the real paper. Do not read a poor score on it as a prediction.

What it does imitate is the marking. Every part asks for a justification, so a correct number with nothing under it is worth close to nothing, exactly as on the real paper when the phrase "with a detailed explanation" or "justify your answer" appears. Four of the five genuine papers use one of those phrases somewhere.

If you are short of time, sit Questions One, Four and Six. Those three carry the proof techniques, and proof is the part of this syllabus that the official course text covers worst.

Question One17.5 marks

(a) Show, without using a truth table and naming the law used at each step, that (p ∧ q) ∨ (p ∧ ¬q) is logically equivalent to p.  (4 marks)

(b) Determine whether (p → q) ∧ (¬p → r) → (q ∨ r) is a tautology.  (5 marks)

(c) Express in symbols, and then negate, the statement "Every student in the class has solved at least one problem". Say in plain words what the negation asserts.  (4 marks)

(d) Test the validity of the following argument, justifying your answer: "All computer scientists are logical. Some logical people are musicians. Therefore some computer scientists are musicians."  (4.5 marks)

Question Two17.5 marks

(a) A relation R is defined on the set of all integers by a R b if and only if a − b is divisible by 5. Prove that R is an equivalence relation and describe its equivalence classes.  (6 marks)

(b) The function f from the integers to the integers is defined by f(n) = 2n + 1. Determine whether f is injective and whether it is surjective. If f is not a bijection, restrict its codomain so that it becomes one.  (4 marks)

(c) Let R = {(1,1), (1,3), (2,2), (3,1), (3,3), (4,4)} on {1, 2, 3, 4}. Write down its matrix, read the five properties off the matrix, and give the partition induced if R is an equivalence relation.  (4 marks)

(d) Prove that if f and g are both injective then g ∘ f is injective.  (3.5 marks)

Question Three17.5 marks

(a) How many of the integers from 1 to 1000 inclusive are divisible by 3 or by 5 but not by 15?  (5 marks)

(b) How many distinct arrangements are there of the letters of the word MISSISSIPPI, and in how many of them do all four S's stand together?  (5 marks)

(c) A password is 8 characters long, drawn from the 26 small letters, the 26 capital letters and the 10 digits with repetition allowed, and it must contain at least one digit. How many such passwords are there?  (4 marks)

(d) Show that among any 13 people two must have been born in the same month, and find the least number of people that guarantees three share a birth month.  (3.5 marks)

Question Four17.5 marks

(a) Prove that in any undirected graph the number of vertices of odd degree is even.  (4 marks)

(b) A tree has two vertices of degree 4, three vertices of degree 3, and every one of its remaining vertices of degree 1. How many vertices has the tree altogether?  (5 marks)

(c) Show that no simple graph can have the degree sequence (5, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1).  (3.5 marks)

(d) Use Euler's formula to prove that every simple connected planar graph with at least three vertices has a vertex of degree at most 5.  (5 marks)

Question Five17.5 marks

(a) Simplify F = A′BC + AB′C + ABC′ + ABC to a minimal sum of products, naming the law used at each step.  (5 marks)

(b) Draw the logic circuit for your simplified expression and say in words what function it computes.  (3 marks)

(c) A function of three inputs is 1 exactly when an odd number of its inputs are 1. Write down its sum of products form from the truth table, and show that no two of its four product terms can be combined, so that the expression is already as short as a sum of products can be.  (4.5 marks)

(d) Write a C++ program that reads three inputs, each 0 or 1, and prints the value of the majority function of part (a). State what it prints for the input 1 0 1.  (5 marks)

Question Six17.5 marks

(a) Prove by strong induction that every integer n ≥ 2 can be written as a product of prime numbers.  (5 marks)

(b) Solve the recurrence relation an = 2an−1 + 3 given a0 = 1.  (4.5 marks)

(c) A bag holds 4 white, 5 black and 6 green balls. Three are drawn together without replacement. Find the probability that all three are of the same colour.  (4 marks)

(d) Prove by contradiction that there is no largest prime number.  (4 marks)

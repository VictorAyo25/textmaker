=== NOTES ON THIS PAPER (defects, gaps and unresolved readings) ===

1. QUESTIONS 1 AND 2 ARE MISSING FROM THE SOURCE. The scan opens at Question 3.
   Page 1 shows only the line "CSC 242: DISCRETE STRUCTURE". Do not invent them.
   The manual must say plainly that this paper is held only from Q3 onward.

2. QUESTION 4 HAS NO NUMBER AND ITS LAST PART HAS NO LETTER. After Q3(d) the
   page runs straight into "(a) Explain in details ... the types of undirected
   graph." and "(b) A Christian missionary ...", which are plainly the parts of
   Question 4, but no "4." is printed. The third part, "Highlight, with a
   relevant example each, three application domains of Tree models in
   computing." carries its marks but no "(c)".

3. "(1 marks)" is printed three times in Q3(a), and "(2 marks)" throughout
   Q3(d): singular value, plural noun. Q5 does the same. Left as printed.

4. THE DIRECTED GRAPH IN Q5(c) IS ONLY PARTLY RESOLVABLE. The three multi-arc
   bundles (a-b, a-d, b-c, d-c) are clear because their arrowheads are
   unambiguous. The two crossing diagonals in the middle are not: at the
   available resolution the arrowheads on them can be read either as two arcs
   a to c plus one arc b to d, or as one each way. The manual must state the
   reading it tabulates, show the method (which is what the 4 marks are for),
   and tell the reader to re-read the arrowheads on their own copy. Do not
   present an uncertain reading as certain.

5. Q5(b) is ambiguous as English: "He can travel directly between two cities
   through the same route" is doing the work of "every pair of cities is joined
   by one route". Part (i) asks whether each of the five cities can connect to
   exactly three others, which is a Handshaking question: five vertices each of
   degree 3 gives a degree sum of 15, which is odd, so no such graph exists.

6. Q3(c) is word-for-word the same question as 2024/2025 Q2 b(iii): compose
   f(x) = x² + 1 with g(x) = x + 2. This paper adds "State if f ∘ g = g ∘ f."

7. Q3(d) is nearly the same relation-classification question as 2024/2025 Q4(b),
   with the same R1 to R6, but this paper adds part (i) on the divides relation
   and asks Transitive last rather than repeating reflexive.

8. Marks on the parts we hold: Q3 1+1+1+7+3.5+2+2+2+2+2 = 23.5;
   Q4 9+10+4.5 = 23.5; Q5 3+3+2+1+1+2+2+6+3.5 = 23.5. So this paper's questions
   are worth 23.5 each, not 17.5: a different rubric from 2024/2025 and
   2025/2026. Worth telling the reader, because it changes the time budget.

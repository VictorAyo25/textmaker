=== NOTES ON THIS PAPER (defects and unresolved readings) ===

1. MARK TEXT IS CLOSED UP on this paper: "5marks", "6½marks", "12marks",
   "7½marks", "8½marks". Two exceptions print a space: "(10 marks)" in 2(c) and
   "(5 marks)" twice in 5(d). Quote each exactly as it appears.

2. Q5(b) IS BADLY GARBLED AND THE NUMBERS DO NOT WORK. It prints "There of 25
   students who major in Computer, while 13 in Maths. If 8 students major in
   both". "There of" is meant to be "There are". More seriously, if 25 major in
   Computer Science and 13 in Maths with 8 in both, inclusion and exclusion give
   25 + 13 − 8 = 30 for the whole class, which is fine; but the sentence can
   also be read as 25 being the class total. The manual quotes it as printed,
   flags the ambiguity, and answers the standard reading (30).

3. THE DIRECTED GRAPH IN Q1(b) IS NOT RELIABLY READABLE at the resolution we
   hold. It is the same style of figure as the 2023/2024 Q5(c) digraph, drawn
   small with soft arrowheads. The manual must teach the method (tabulate each
   vertex, its arcs, its in-degree and its out-degree, then check the two totals
   agree with the arc count) and say plainly that the reader should read the
   arrowheads off their own copy. Never present an unreadable figure as read.

4. Q1(a)(iii) "{x | x is a perfect square and x < 100}" does not say x is a
   positive integer, so strictly it also admits 0 and non-integer squares. The
   intended answer is {0, 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81} or the same without
   0. Flag it; do not silently pick one.

5. Q4(a) "Briefly explain the Handshaking Theorem?" is a command punctuated as a
   question. Q1(a) and Q2(c) also mix "explain" with a question mark.

6. Q5(d)(i) prints the connective as a circled minus, ⊖. In context it must mean
   exclusive or, normally written ⊕. Quote the glyph as printed and say what it
   has to mean for the question to be answerable.

7. Q5(d)(ii) is exactly the same proposition as 2024/2025 Q1 b(ii):
   (q → ¬p) ↔ (p ↔ q). One truth table serves both papers.

8. Q2(b) names four buildings (SENATE Building, CUCRID, CLR, CSIS NOC), so the
   count of simple links is C(4,2) = 6. The 2020/2021 version of this question
   names five buildings and its answer is C(5,2) = 10. Do not blur the two.

9. This paper is 2 credit units and 2 hours, "attempt any THREE". The 2024/2025
   and 2025/2026 papers are 3 credit units and 3 hours, "any FOUR". The rubric
   changed; the topics did not.

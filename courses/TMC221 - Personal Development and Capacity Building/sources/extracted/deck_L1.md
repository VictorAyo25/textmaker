## S1 — Title
Goal Setting and Personal Accomplishment. Proven frameworks for turning Ambition into Achievement.

## S2 — CONTEXT: "Same Starting Line, Completely Different Finish"
- In 2008, two roommates each wrote about their futures.
- First wrote: "I want to be successful."
- Second wrote a precise plan: a certification, an NGO role, and ₦500,000 saved, all by a specific date.
- By 2012, the second had done all three. The first was still writing the same sentence.
- The difference was not intelligence, family background, or luck. It was **clarity**.
- Quote (Ben Stein): "The indispensable first step to getting what you want is this: decide what you want."

## S4 — WHAT A GOAL IS: "Not Every Aspiration Is a Goal" (the four levels)
| Level | Example | The problem |
|---|---|---|
| Wish | "I want to be rich" | No direction or deadline |
| Dream | "I want to own a business someday" | Indefinite timeframe |
| Intention | "I plan to start saving this year" | No commitment or measure |
| Goal | "I will save ₦20,000 monthly for 6 months from Jan 1st" | Specific, measurable, time-bound |
- Most people live in the Wish and Dream layers; they feel purposeful without requiring anything of themselves.
- A real goal is a **declaration with evidence**: what will happen, when, and how you will know.
- **Key shift:** Move from "I want" to "I will, by [date], measured by [evidence]."

## S5 — THREE TYPES OF GOALS: "Only One Actually Changes You" (Outcome, Process, Identity, ranked by lasting power)
- **Outcome Goals** — What you want to achieve. "Win the department prize." Motivating but entirely outside your daily control. Useful for direction. Dangerous alone.
- **Process Goals** — What you commit to doing. "Study four hours every weekday." Fully within your control and builds directly toward outcomes. The daily engine of achievement.
- **Identity Goals** — Who you are becoming. "I am the kind of person who shows up consistently." When the goal becomes your identity, behaviour follows without negotiation. The student who *is* disciplined outlasts the one who *wants* to be.

## S6 — EXERCISE: "What Type Is Your Goal?" (5 minutes; work individually)
1. Write down one current goal you are actively pursuing (or avoiding).
2. Identify its type: Outcome, Process, or Identity?
3. Rewrite it as an Identity Goal: "I am becoming the kind of person who..."
4. Notice how the rewrite feels — does it land differently?

## S7 — FRAMEWORKS: "Four Frameworks That Actually Work" (each solves a different failure mode)
| # | Framework | The problem it solves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | SMART+ER | Goals that lack structure and emotional connection |
| 2 | OKRs | Ambition with no measurable evidence of progress |
| 3 | 12-Week Year | Annual planning that destroys urgency |
| 4 | Implementation Intentions | Motivation without a trigger to act |
- Use these as a **toolkit**, not as a menu to pick one from.

## S8 — FRAMEWORK 1: SMART+ER ("Works But it Has a Fatal Weakness"; add two letters)
- **S** — Specific: Exactly what will you do or produce?
- **M** — Measurable: What number proves you succeeded?
- **A** — Achievable: Is this a genuine but realistic stretch?
- **R** — Relevant: Does this serve your bigger purpose?
- **T** — Time-bound: What is the exact deadline?
- **+E** — Exciting: Would you pursue this without anyone watching?
- **+R** — Reviewed: When exactly will you check progress?
- Why the upgrade matters: A SMART goal can be abandoned by week 2 because of no emotional connection. E = internal fuel. R builds in accountability. A goal without a review schedule is a wish with an expiry date.

## S9 — FRAMEWORK 2: OKRs ("Ambition Meets Evidence"; used by Google since 1999)
An OKR has two parts:
1. **Objective** — one bold, inspiring, *qualitative* statement. Answers "Where am I going?" Ambitious enough to feel slightly uncomfortable.
2. **Key Results (2 to 4)** — specific, measurable outcomes that *prove* the Objective was achieved. Answers "How will I know I got there?"
- Critical distinction: a Key Result is **not an activity**. "Attend networking events" = activity (wrong). "Secure 3 mentors by end of quarter" = Key Result (right).
- Activities describe what you do. Key Results describe what changes.

## S10 — OKR IN ACTION: "Adaeze's OKR, From Vague to Verified" (300-Level Mass Comm student)
- Objective: Establish myself as a credible emerging voice in digital media before the end of this academic year.
  - KR 1: Publish 12 posts (min. 200 views each) by Apr 30.
  - KR 2: Complete one freelance project with external published output by June 30.
  - KR 3: Secure 2 active industry mentors, 1 conversation/month, by March 31.
  - KR 4: Grow LinkedIn to 500 connections at 3% engagement by May 31.
- End-of-Quarter Score = **0.76 (Strong)**:
  - 12 posts → 0.75 (published 9)
  - Internship → 1.0
  - 2 Mentors → 0.5 (found 1)
  - LinkedIn → 0.8 (reached 412)
- Scoring replaces vague guilt with a structured conversation about what changes next quarter.

## S11 — FRAMEWORK 3: 12-Week Year (compress execution into 12-week cycles; four fresh starts per year)
The five elements of a 12-Week cycle:
1. **Vision** — your long-term *why* that doesn't change weekly.
2. **12-Week Goal** — one to three specific goals for this cycle only.
3. **Weekly Plan** — specific scheduled actions per week; written, not mental.
4. **Weekly Scorecard** — % of planned actions completed. Target: **85%+**.
5. **Weekly Review** — what worked? what changes next week?
- When you have 12 months to achieve a goal, your brain behaves as though there is **always more time**. Urgency is low in Jan. Relevance fades in Jun. Panic arrives in Oct, too late for a full recovery.
- **Brian Moran's Solution:** treat every 12 weeks as a Full Year.

## S12 — FRAMEWORK 4: Implementation Intentions ("Pre-Decide So Your Future Self Doesn't Negotiate")
- Psychologist **Peter Gollwitzer** found that people who specified *when* and *where* they would act followed through **two to three times more often** than those who only stated strong intentions.
- The formula: "When [specific cue] occurs, I will [specific behaviour]."
- The cue must be **concrete**: a time, place, or completed action. Not "when I feel motivated."
- **71%** completion rate when people specified exactly when and where they would act (vs. **32%** with intention only).

## S13 — IMPLEMENTATION INTENTIONS: "How to use Implementation Intentions" (6 steps)
1. Name the goal behaviour: one specific action.
2. Identify your failure mode: when and why it typically doesn't happen.
3. Find a reliable daily cue: Time, Place, or Existing routine.
4. Write the if-then plan: When [cue], I will [action].
5. Pre-empt the obstacle: If [TEMPTATION], then I will [COUNTER-ACTION].
6. Chain it to an existing habit for automatic triggering.

## S14 — EXERCISE: "Build One Implementation Intention" (5 minutes)
For your most important current goal:
1. What is the specific behaviour the goal requires — one concrete action?
2. What is your most predictable failure mode — when and why do you typically not do it?
3. Write your Implementation Intention: "When ___, I will ___."
4. Write your Obstacle Plan: "If ___ happens, then I will ___."
5. Connect it to an existing habit: "After I [habit] → I will [goal action]."

## S15 — PSYCHOLOGY OF ACHIEVEMENT: "Three Reasons Goals Fail — None of Them Is Laziness"
1. **Planning Fallacy** — we overestimate what we can do short-term and underestimate long-term capacity. **40%** — Fix: add a 40% time buffer. Work backwards from the deadline.
2. **Motivation Decay** — motivation peaks at the start and end. Weeks 3 to 8 (the dangerous middle) is when most people quit. Fix: design your system for the middle, not the launch.
3. **Willpower Depletion** — self-control tires with use. Relying on willpower alone is a losing strategy. Fix: design your environment so the right behaviour needs least resistance.

## S16 — SIX ACHIEVEMENT STRATEGIES: "Sustain Goals When Motivation Fades" (Environment and Systems, not Willpower and Inspiration)
1. **Habit Stacking** — attach a new goal behaviour to an existing daily habit.
2. **Temptation Bundling** — pair a hard task with something you enjoy. Research: **+51%** exercise when bundled with audiobooks.
3. **The 2-Minute Rule** — commit to just 2 minutes of a hard task. The goal is to *start*, not to finish.
4. **Progress Principle** — small wins are the biggest driver of motivation (Harvard, Amabile). Keep a daily progress journal.
5. **Accountability** — a structured accountability partner raises goal completion to **95%** (ASTD).
6. **WOOP** — Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan. Mental contrasting outperforms positive visualisation.

## S17 — WOOP IN PRACTICE: "Visualise the Win, Then Plan the Obstacle" (4-step method)
1. **Wish** — state your most important goal clearly and specifically.
2. **Outcome** — visualise the best possible result vividly. How does it feel when you get there?
3. **Obstacle** — name the biggest *internal* obstacle honestly. What has stopped you before?
4. **Plan** — write the if-then response: "If [obstacle], then I will [specific action]."
- Example: Wish: Finish my research proposal this month. Outcome: Supervisor approves it; I feel proud and ahead of schedule. Obstacle: I get distracted by social media when working alone. Plan: When I sit to write, I switch my phone to airplane mode first.

## S18 — TOTAL MAN DIMENSION: "Winning in One Area While Losing in Another Is Not Success" (whole-life excellence)
The Total Man framework covers every domain:
- **Spiritual** — Formation, calling, and purpose.
- **Mental** — Academic excellence and intellectual growth.
- **Physical** — Health, discipline, and bodily stewardship.
- **Social** — Relationships, community, and service.
- **Financial** — Stewardship and wealth-building discipline.
Three questions for aligned goal setting:
1. Does this goal serve my **purpose**, not just my preference?
2. Am I building in **every dimension**, or winning in one while crumbling in others?
3. Is this goal aligned with **who God has called me to be**?

## S19 — GOAL ARCHITECTURE: "The 4-Layer Goal Stack — Vision to Action This Week" (nested commitments)
| Layer | Timeframe | The question |
|---|---|---|
| Life Vision | 10 to 20 years | Who am I called to be? |
| Strategic Goals | 1 to 3 years | What must I build toward that vision? |
| Quarterly Targets | 12 weeks | What do I commit to right now? |
| Weekly Actions | 7 days | What do I actually do this week? |
- Example (200-Level Student): Vision: Lead political reform in West Africa. Strategic: First class + MPA or LAW scholarship by 2030. Quarterly: Raise CGPA 0.2 points this semester. Weekly: Notes done Fri, study group Sat 10am.
- Each layer feeds the one above it. Weekly actions are not random; they are the bricks that build the vision.

## S20 — EXERCISE: "Build Your 4-Layer Goal Stack" (8 minutes; top-down)
1. **Life Vision:** Who are you called to be? What legacy will you leave? Write 2 sentences — not a job title, but a person description.
2. **Strategic Goal:** What must you build or achieve in the next 3 years toward that vision?
3. **Quarterly Target:** One specific, measurable commitment for the next 12 weeks.
4. **Weekly Action:** One concrete action this week — write the day, time, and location.
5. **Pair share:** Read only your Weekly Action to the person beside you. Exchange contacts. Agree to check in before next lecture.

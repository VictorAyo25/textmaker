Stage W: Winning Wisdom (Oyedepo)

Teach: wisdom is the foundationL2 S4
The central claim: "Winning wisdom is the supernatural ability to bring solutions to matters too difficult for the ordinary human mind." (Dr. David Oyedepo, drawing on James 1:5.) The greatest answers are received by the prepared thinker, not manufactured; wisdom is not the reward of more effort but the fruit of divine connection. Before you develop your strategy, go to your source.
Teach: what wisdom is and is notL2 S5
Wisdom is not thinking harder, researching more, or being more disciplined. Wisdom is uncovering God's approach to every issue of life, knowing which way to go before the path is fully visible, and above all the application of knowledge, not its mere accumulation.
"The application of knowledge is what is called wisdom. Until a man amasses adequate knowledge, there will be nothing for him to use." Dr. Oyedepo
Teach: the Knowledge-Wisdom LadderL2 S6
Three rungs, and most people never reach the top.
RungWhat it isWhere people stop
1. InformationGathering facts, data, experienceWhere most begin
2. KnowledgeOrganising and understanding informationMany stop here
3. WisdomApplying knowledge to produce resultsMost never reach this

Key insight: knowledge gives you power; wisdom gives you strength. The leap from knowledge to wisdom needs more than study, it requires seeking.

Stage I: Inner Architecture (Leaf)

Teach: you are the architectL2 S7
The core discovery: every thought you think physically changes the structure of your brain (Romans 12:2). The brain is not fixed; it is a living, dynamic architecture you are constantly building or dismantling. You are not a product of your neurology, you are its architect. The critical link: Oyedepo says wisdom must be received; Leaf shows the mechanism, a mind prepared to think well is a mind positioned to receive wisdom.
Teach: the 21-day SWITCH ProtocolL2 S8
The SWITCH Protocol is Dr. Leaf's scientifically grounded 21-day brain detox: not motivational self-talk, but a rewiring protocol that literally builds new neural structures. Its six steps:
StepAction
GatherBecome aware of the toxic thought
Focused ReflectionExamine its root and emotional charge
WriteExternalise it in a journal
RevisitReview patterns and connections, understand context
Active ReachChoose and rehearse the replacement thought
CelebrateAcknowledge growth, reinforce the new pathway
Must MemoriseL2 S8
SWITCH is a 21-day protocol that builds new neural structures through deliberate practice and repetition. Its six steps in order: Gather, Focused Reflection, Write, Revisit, Active Reach, Celebrate. It is not positive self-talk.
Memory hook: Gather, Reflect, Write, Revisit, Reach, Celebrate. Rewire, do not just cheer.

Module Two · Unit 2

Spirit and Execution

The last two stages of the W.I.S.E. Model: the attitude that fuels action (S, Peale), and the tools that turn insight into solutions and ideas (E, de Bono and Young).

Teach: Spirit of Possibility (Peale)L2 S9
The central claim: "What you consistently believe about your situation determines what you will experience." (Norman Vincent Peale.) Peale's role in the model: Oyedepo establishes the source, Leaf prepares the receiver, and Peale fuels the engine. A mind rebuilt by Leaf but filled with doubt will not generate the confident, exploratory energy creative problem-solving demands.

Stage E: Lateral Thinking (de Bono)

Teach: escape the patternL2 S12
De Bono's insight: most problems persist not from lack of intelligence, but from over-commitment to an existing pattern of thinking.
Vertical ThinkingLateral Thinking
Builds on what is knownChallenges what is known
Seeks the one correct answerExplores many possible answers
Efficient within a patternEscapes patterns entirely

The solutions too difficult for the ordinary mind cannot be reached by digging deeper in the same rut.

Teach: four lateral-thinking toolsL2 S13
  1. Random Entry: introduce a random word and force a connection to the problem; the arbitrary link opens untravelled pathways.
  2. The Provocative Operation (Po): make a deliberate, absurd statement about the problem to disrupt pattern-lock ("Po: our product has no users"), then follow the implications.
  3. Challenge Assumptions: surface every unexamined assumption and ask, "Why must this be this way?"
  4. The Six Thinking Hats: White (facts), Red (emotion), Black (caution), Yellow (optimism), Green (creativity), Blue (process).

Stage E: A Technique for Producing Ideas (Young)

Teach: ideas are new combinationsL2 S14
Young's central claim: the production of ideas is not a gift, it is a process; anyone who follows it faithfully will produce ideas. Two foundational principles: an idea is nothing more than a new combination of existing elements; and forming new combinations depends on the capacity to see relationships between things that appear unrelated.
Teach: Young's five-step processL2 S15
StepAction
1. Gather Raw MaterialCollect specific and general knowledge voraciously
2. Digest the MaterialTurn it over in your mind; let connections form
3. Drop the ProblemRelease it entirely; let the subconscious work
4. The Eureka MomentCapture the idea the instant it arrives
5. Shape and DevelopRefine through critical analysis and collaboration
Must MemoriseL2 S15
Young's five steps: Gather, Digest, Drop (incubate), Eureka, Develop. An idea is a new combination of existing elements, found by seeing relationships.
Memory hook: Gather, Digest, Drop, Eureka, Develop. Feed it, forget it, catch it, fix it.
Fill in the GapL2 S14, S15

Answer new combination; drop.
Teach: de Bono and Young togetherL2 S16
The complete creative cycle: Young provides the five-step architecture of idea production; de Bono supplies the tools, especially in steps 1 and 2, to break cognitive locks and broaden thinking; and in step 5 Young relies on de Bono's vertical thinking to refine the raw idea. de Bono teaches you to think differently; Young teaches you to produce ideas systematically.
Teach: what all five authors agree onL2 S17
Across five decades and five disciplines, the same five convictions:
  1. The mind is primary: inner life matters far more than external circumstances.
  2. Patterns of thought can be changed: intelligence and potential are developed, not fixed.
  3. Insight is prepared for, not waited for: breakthroughs reward readiness.
  4. Discomfort is part of the process: growth passes through resistance the undisciplined mind avoids.
  5. Wisdom, thinking, and creativity are not separate domains: they are one integrated capacity.

Teach: the full picture and the five textsL2 S18, S19
The W.I.S.E. Model is not a sequence to complete once; it is a cycle to inhabit continuously: W (seek the source), I (prepare the receiver), S (fuel the process), E (channel wisdom into solutions). The five core texts, for reference: Oyedepo, Winning Wisdom (1996); Leaf, Switch On Your Brain (2013); Peale, The Power of Positive Thinking (1952); de Bono, Lateral Thinking (1970); Young, A Technique for Producing Ideas (1939).

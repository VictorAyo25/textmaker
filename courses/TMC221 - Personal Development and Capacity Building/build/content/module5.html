Discouragement and the Grit to Outlast It

First, what discouragement actually is, then the finding that the enemy is never the problem but your response to it, then the one trait that predicts achievement, and finally the image the whole lecture turns on: the 50th blow.

Teach: what discouragement isL5 S2
Discouragement is the gradual withdrawal of the courage to continue. Read the definition slowly, because every word is doing work. It is gradual, not sudden, which is why the lecture calls it the slow leak: nobody quits in one dramatic moment, they leak courage until there is none left. And what leaks out is courage, not ability. You do not lose the capacity to continue, you lose the willingness.

The science of grit

Teach: what actually predicts successL5 S4
Angela Duckworth studied West Point cadets, spelling bee champions, and top entrepreneurs, asking what separated those who finished from those who did not. Three obvious candidates failed the test: IQ, Talent, and Social class. One survived: Grit, described as the only consistent predictor across all high achievers. Her definition is a formula worth memorising exactly: Grit = Passion + Long-term Perseverance. Passion alone is enthusiasm; perseverance alone is drudgery; grit is the two held together over years. "Our potential is one thing. What we do with it is quite another." (Angela Duckworth, Grit.)
Must MemoriseL5 S4
Grit = Passion + Long-term Perseverance, and grit is the only consistent predictor of success across all high achievers. Not IQ, not talent, not social class. Studied in West Point cadets, spelling bee champions, and top entrepreneurs.
Memory hook: two words make grit, Passion plus Perseverance, and the second one is long-term. Three rivals struck out: IQ, Talent, Class.

The 50th blow

Teach: the Sledgehammer PrincipleL5 S5
The Sledgehammer Principle (Dr Oyedepo): a man swings at a wall, blow after blow, and nothing happens. Blow 10 = Nothing. Blow 30 = Nothing. Blow 49 = Nothing. Blow 50 = the wall collapses. Then comes the point the whole lecture rests on: it was not the 50th blow that brought it down, it was the cumulative force of ALL the blows before it. Which means the invisible blows were never wasted: the results you cannot see today are being built by the work you are doing now. So do not stop at blow 49. This is the same wall from Lecture 4's Kit 7, Tarrying, and Lecture 5 supplies the numbers.
Must MemoriseL5 S5
Blow 10, nothing. Blow 30, nothing. Blow 49, nothing. Blow 50, the wall collapses. And the collapse belongs to all the blows, not to the last one.
Memory hook: the 50th gets the credit, the 49 did the work. Don't stop at 49.
TrapL5 S5
The tempting answer is "the 50th blow was the decisive one, so the earlier blows mattered less." The slide says the exact opposite in bold: it was not the 50th blow that brought it down, it was the cumulative force of ALL the blows before it. If an option credits the final blow, it is the distractor.

Pillar 1: vision in practice, the Precision Test

Teach: run with precision, not just passionL5 S10
"If you don't have a target, you'll keep following whatever is popular." (Dr. Oyedepo.) That is the same warning as Lecture 4's Kit 3, and here it gets a four-part test. The Precision Test, a goal must be:
  1. Specific: not "do better," but "achieve 4.90 GPA"
  2. Measurable: you can track it
  3. Time-bound: it has a deadline
  4. Written: if it is not written, it is a wish

The action the slide asks for: write your top 3 goals for the next 12 months. That is your target. Now run.

Compare with SMART from Lecture 1. The Precision Test keeps Specific, Measurable and Time-bound, drops Achievable and Relevant, and adds one SMART never had: Written. And "if it is not written, it is a wish" lands exactly on Lecture 1's bottom rung, the Wish.

Four Strategies and the 7-Step Plan

Sustaining the fire takes four named strategies, then the course closes with a seven-step plan and one arithmetic fact about compounding that makes the whole thing worth doing.

Teach: Strategy 1, RESISTL5 S14
RESIST: build endurance like a soldier (2 Timothy 2:3). Four Truths About Resistance:
  1. Discouragement attacks hardest in the middle of the journey.
  2. Every champion has marks of failure in their past record.
  3. Opposition is not a sign to stop, it is proof you are moving.
  4. The crown is on the other side of the endurance.

"Winners never quit, and quitters never win." (Oral Robert, as the slide prints the name.) The strategy in one line: rise again, every time. That is the strategy.

Truth one is the practically useful one: if the attack is heaviest in the middle, then feeling worst halfway through is evidence you are halfway through, not evidence you were wrong to start.

Teach: Strategy 4, SPEAK YOUR DREAML5 S17
SPEAK YOUR DREAM: a closed mouth is a closed future.

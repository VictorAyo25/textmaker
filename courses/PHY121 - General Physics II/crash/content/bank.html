Test 1 Q1work done on a charge

1. Find the work done on a charge of +6.0 μC placed in vacuum as it moves through a distance of 50 cm.

a. 2 x 10-6 J

b. 1.08 x 10-6 J

c. 0.5 x 10-6 J

d. 0.65 x 10-6 J

e. 3 x 10-6 J

Answer 3 x 10-6 J

Work done moving a charge along a field is W = qEd. Converting first: q = 6.0 x 10-6 C and d = 50 cm = 0.50 m. With the unit field the examiner intends, E = 1 Vm-1, W = 6.0 x 10-6 x 1 x 0.50 = 3 x 10-6 J. The stem does not print a field strength, so the marked answer only follows if E is taken as 1 Vm-1; the arithmetic that earns the mark is q x d.

2 x 10-6 J Wrong. This is q x d with the distance left as 1/3 m rather than 0.50 m.

1.08 x 10-6 J Wrong. No route to this value from a 6.0 μC charge over 0.50 m.

0.5 x 10-6 J Wrong, and it is the trap: this is the DISTANCE in metres multiplied by 10-6, not the work.

0.65 x 10-6 J Wrong. No route to this value from these numbers.

3 x 10-6 J Correct. W = qEd = 6.0 x 10-6 x 1 x 0.50 = 3 x 10-6 J.

Test 1 Q2combining capacitors

2. Determine the total capacitance of the capacitors given in Figure 1

a. 5.1 μF

b. 3.43 μF

c. 0.8 μF

d. 8.8 μF

e. 14.0 μF

Answer 8.8 μF

Read the picture before reaching for a formula. C1 and C2 sit one above the other in the SAME branch, so they are in series; that branch and C3 span the same two terminals, so the pair is in parallel with C3. Series first: 1/Cs = 1/1 + 1/5 = 6/5, so Cs = 5/6 = 0.833 μF. Then parallel adds: C = 0.833 + 8 = 8.83, which is 8.8 μF. The clue that parallel comes last is that the answer must exceed 8, since adding a capacitor in parallel can only increase the total.

5.1 μF Wrong. No arrangement of 1, 5 and 8 gives 5.1.

3.43 μF Wrong. This is all three in series: 1/(1/1 + 1/5 + 1/8) = 0.784, or a mis-stated version of it. Series can never exceed the smallest capacitor, so it cannot be right here.

0.8 μF Wrong, and it is the near miss: 0.833 μF is C1 and C2 in series, the FIRST step only, with C3 forgotten.

8.8 μF Correct. C1 in series with C2 gives 5/6 = 0.833 μF, and in parallel with C3 that is 0.833 + 8 = 8.8 μF.

14.0 μF Wrong. This is all three added as though every one were in parallel: 1 + 5 + 8 = 14.

Test 1 Q3holding a charge stationary

3. Calculate the electric field in which a charged oil drops of mass 8 mg and charged +9 μC will be held stationary.

a. 5.3 Vm-1

b. 4.35 Vm-1

c. 8.89 Vm-1

d. 7.26 Vm-1

e. 6.62 Vm-1

Answer 8.89 Vm-1

Stationary means the electric force balances the weight, so qE = mg and E = mg/q. Given m = 8 mg = 8 x 10-6 kg and q = 9 x 10-6 C, E = (8 x 10-6 x 10) / (9 x 10-6) = 80 x 10-6 / 9 x 10-6 = 8.89 Vm-1. Note the examiner is using g = 10 ms-2; with g = 9.8 the answer would be 8.71, which is not offered.

5.3 Vm-1 Wrong. No route to 5.3 from these numbers.

4.35 Vm-1 Wrong. This is roughly half the correct value, which is what you get from using 4 mg or double the charge.

8.89 Vm-1 Correct. E = mg/q = (8 x 10-6 x 10) / (9 x 10-6) = 8.89 Vm-1.

7.26 Vm-1 Wrong. No route to 7.26 from these numbers.

6.62 Vm-1 Wrong. No route to 6.62 from these numbers.

Test 1 Q4absolute potential at two distances

4. What is the absolute potential at each of the following distances from a charge of +4 μC in air: r = 5 cm and r = 20 cm?

a. 750 KV & 180 kV

b. 150 KV & 75 KV

c. 720 KV & 180 KV

d. 250 KV & 50 KV

e. 500 KV & 150 KV

Answer 720 KV & 180 KV

Absolute potential is V = kQ/r, which falls as 1/r, not as 1/r2. With kQ = 9 x 109 x 4 x 10-6 = 36000: at r = 0.05 m, V = 36000/0.05 = 720000 V = 720 kV; at r = 0.20 m, V = 36000/0.20 = 180000 V = 180 kV. The distance quadruples, so the potential falls to a quarter, which is the check to run on the pair.

750 KV & 180 kV Wrong on the first value. The second is right, so this catches anyone who computes only one and matches on it.

150 KV & 75 KV Wrong. This pair falls by half, not by a quarter, so it cannot come from a 1/r law over a fourfold change in distance.

720 KV & 180 KV Correct. V = kQ/r gives 720 kV at 5 cm and 180 kV at 20 cm.

250 KV & 50 KV Wrong. This pair falls to a fifth and neither value follows from kQ = 36000.

500 KV & 150 KV Wrong. Neither value follows from kQ/r with these numbers.

Test 1 Q5electric flux through a surface

5. A uniform electric field E = 200 N/C passes through a surface of area 2 m2 at 0°. Find Flux.

a. 450 400 N/m2/C

b. 300 N/m2/C

c. 400 N/m2/C

d. 120 N/m2/C

e. 100 N/m2/C

Answer 400 N/m2/C

Flux is the field times the area times the cosine of the angle between the field and the surface NORMAL: phi = EA cos(theta). At 0 degrees the field is straight through, cos 0 = 1, so phi = 200 x 2 x 1 = 400. The angle being zero is what makes this a one-line question; at 90 degrees the flux would be zero.

450 400 N/m2/C Wrong, and the printed option runs two numbers together. Neither follows from 200 x 2.

300 N/m2/C Wrong. This would need an area of 1.5 m2.

400 N/m2/C Correct. phi = EA cos 0 = 200 x 2 x 1 = 400.

120 N/m2/C Wrong. This is 200 x 2 x 0.3, which is no cosine of any angle offered.

100 N/m2/C Wrong. This halves the field instead of doubling it by the area.

Test 1 Q6parallel plate capacitor with a dielectric

6. The area of each plate of a parallel plate capacitor is 4 x 10-2 m2. If the thickness of the dielectric placed in between the plates is 10-3 m and the relative permittivity of the dielectric is 7, then the capacitance of the capacitor is?

a. 0.2478 x 10-9 F

b. 2.478 x 109 F

c. 4.77 F

d. 2.478 x 10-9 F

e. 0.2478 x 109 F

Answer 2.478 x 10-9 F

C = (epsilon0 x epsilonr x A) / d. Substituting: (8.854 x 10-12 x 7 x 4 x 10-2) / 10-3. Numerator: 8.854 x 10-12 x 7 = 6.198 x 10-11, times 4 x 10-2 = 2.479 x 10-12. Dividing by 10-3 gives 2.478 x 10-9 F, that is about 2.5 nF. A capacitance in nanofarads is the sanity check: a positive power of ten here would be an impossibly large capacitor.

0.2478 x 10-9 F Wrong by a factor of ten, which is what you get from mishandling the 10-2 area or the 10-3 thickness.

2.478 x 109 F Wrong sign on the exponent. 2.478 x 109 F is larger than any capacitor ever built.

4.77 F Wrong. This drops the permittivity of free space entirely.

2.478 x 10-9 F Correct. C = epsilon0 epsilonr A / d = 2.478 x 10-9 F.

0.2478 x 109 F Wrong on both the digits and the sign of the exponent.

Test 1 Q8net field at a midpoint

8. Two charges Q1 = +3.0 x 10-6 C and Q2 = -3.0x10-6 C, are placed 0.10 m apart in air. Find the net electric field at the midpoint between the charges.

a. 1.99 x107 N/C

b. 1.08 x 107 N/C

c. 2.16 x 107 N/C

d. 3.0 x 107 N/C

e. 1.07 x 107 N/C

Answer 2.16 x 107 N/C

The midpoint is 0.05 m from each charge. Each contributes E = kQ/r2 = 9 x 109 x 3 x 10-6 / (0.05)2 = 27000 / 0.0025 = 1.08 x 107 N/C. The field points AWAY from the positive charge and TOWARDS the negative one, so at the midpoint both contributions point the same way and they add: 2 x 1.08 x 107 = 2.16 x 107 N/C.

1.99 x107 N/C Wrong. No route to 1.99 from these numbers.

1.08 x 107 N/C Wrong. This is the field from ONE charge only, and it is the trap: the two contributions add rather than cancel because the charges are opposite in sign.

2.16 x 107 N/C Correct. Two equal contributions of 1.08 x 107 N/C pointing the same way, so 2.16 x 107 N/C.

3.0 x 107 N/C Wrong. This would need a separation smaller than the one given.

1.07 x 107 N/C Wrong, and it is a near miss on the single-charge value, which is itself only half the answer.

12. Given a circuit where R1, R2 and R3 are in series with a voltage source of 48 V, such that R1 = 7 Ω, R2 = 6 Ω and R3 = 3 Ω, calculate the current that flows through R3?

a. 3 A

b. 6 A

c. 4 A

Answer 3 A

In series the resistances add and the SAME current flows through every component, so the answer for R3 is the answer for the whole loop. R = 7 + 6 + 3 = 16 ohms, and I = V/R = 48/16 = 3 A. Naming R3 specifically is the trap: it invites you to divide 48 by 3.

3 A Correct. R total = 16 ohms, so I = 48/16 = 3 A, and that same current flows through R3.

4 A Wrong. This is 48/12, that is the sum of two of the three resistors.

12 A Wrong, and it is the trap: 48/3 uses R3 alone, ignoring that current is set by the WHOLE series loop.

5 A Wrong. No route to 5 A from 48 V and 16 ohms.

Test 2 Q3emf from external and internal resistance

3. In a circuit, the external resistor has a value of 5 Ω. The internal resistance of the cell is 0.5 Ω. If the current flowing is 1 A. Calculate the e.m.f of the cell.

a. 5.5 V

b. 4.5 V

c. 3.5 V

d. 6.5 V

e. 7.5 V

Answer 5.5 V

The emf drives the current through BOTH the external resistor and the cell's own internal resistance, so E = I(R + r) = 1 x (5 + 0.5) = 5.5 V. Equivalently E = IR + Ir, that is 5 V delivered to the circuit plus 0.5 V lost inside the cell. The emf is always LARGER than the terminal p.d., which is the check.

5.5 V Correct. E = I(R + r) = 1 x 5.5 = 5.5 V.

4.5 V Wrong, and it SUBTRACTS the internal resistance instead of adding it, which would make the emf smaller than the terminal p.d.

3.5 V Wrong. No route to 3.5 V from these numbers.

6.5 V Wrong. This would need an internal resistance of 1.5 ohms.

7.5 V Wrong. This would need an internal resistance of 2.5 ohms.

Test 2 Q4emf from the terminal p.d.

4. A cell of unknown e.m.f E, and internal resistance 2 Ω is connected to a 5 Ω resistor. If the terminal p.d. is 1 volt, calculate the value of "E" if a current of "I" flows through the cell.

a. 1.4 V

b. 1.2 V

c. 1.3 V

d. 1 V

Two steps. First the current, from the terminal p.d. across the external resistor: I = V/R = 1/5 = 0.2 A. Then the emf, which must also push that current through the internal resistance: E = I(R + r) = 0.2 x (5 + 2) = 0.2 x 7 = 1.4 V. Equivalently E = V + Ir = 1 + 0.2 x 2 = 1.4 V.

1.4 V Correct. I = 1/5 = 0.2 A, then E = I(R + r) = 0.2 x 7 = 1.4 V.

1.2 V Wrong. This adds only half the internal drop, as if r were 1 ohm.

1.3 V Wrong. No consistent route gives 1.3 V here.

1 V Wrong, and it is the trap: 1 V is the TERMINAL p.d. given in the stem, not the emf. The emf must exceed it by the internal drop.

1.5 V Wrong. This would need a current of 0.25 A, that is a 4 ohm external resistor.

Test 2 Q5when Ampere's law helps

5. Ampère's law is most useful for conductors with high ________.

Fill the blank. Accepted: symmetry

Answer symmetry

Symmetry. Ampere's law is always true, but it is only USEFUL when you can choose a loop on which the field has the same magnitude everywhere and points along the loop, so that the integral collapses to B times the length. That needs symmetry: a long straight wire, a solenoid, a toroid. Without it the law still holds but cannot be solved for B, which is exactly the same reason Gauss's law is only useful for spherical, cylindrical or planar charge distributions.

Test 2 Q6transformer efficiency

6. A 240 V step-down transformer is used to light up eight 12 V, 24 W lamps and draws a current of 1 A in the primary. Calculate its efficiency.

a. 80 %

b. 60 %

c. 40 %

d. 20 %

e. 0 %

Answer 80 %

Efficiency is output power over input power. Output: eight lamps at 24 W each is 8 x 24 = 192 W. Input: the primary side, P = VI = 240 x 1 = 240 W. So efficiency = 192/240 = 0.8, that is 80 per cent. The 12 V rating of each lamp is not needed, because the wattage already states the power each one consumes.

80 % Correct. Output 8 x 24 = 192 W over input 240 x 1 = 240 W, giving 80 per cent.

40 % Wrong. This would need an output of 96 W, that is four lamps.

20 % Wrong. This uses one lamp only: 24/240 wrongly scaled.

0 % Wrong. A transformer lighting eight lamps is plainly transferring power, so zero cannot be right.

Test 2 Q8radius of a circular path in a field

8. An electron of mass 9.1 × 10−31 kg and charge 1.6 × 10−19 enters a magnetic field of 0.03 T perpendicularly with velocity 4 × 106 m/s. Find the radius of its circular path.

a. 7.58 × 10-4 m

b. 7.58 × 10-6 m

c. 7.58 × 10-2 m

d. 7.58 × 10-8 m

e. 7.58 × 10-1 m

Answer 7.58 × 10-4 m

A charge moving perpendicular to a field goes in a circle, with the magnetic force supplying the centripetal force: qvB = mv2/r, so r = mv/(qB). Substituting: r = (9.1 x 10-31 x 4 x 106) / (1.6 x 10-19 x 0.03) = 3.64 x 10-24 / 4.8 x 10-21 = 7.58 x 10-4 m. Every option shares the digits 7.58, so the ONLY thing being tested is the power of ten.

7.58 × 10-4 m Correct. r = mv/(qB) = 3.64 x 10-24 / 4.8 x 10-21 = 7.58 x 10-4 m.

7.58 × 10-6 m Wrong exponent, off by 100. Check the subtraction: -24 minus -21 is -3, then the leading 3.64/4.8 being less than one shifts it to -4.

7.58 × 10-2 m Wrong exponent, off by 100 the other way.

7.58 × 10-8 m Wrong exponent, off by 10000.

Test 2 Q9current in a solenoid

9. A solenoid of length 0.4 m has 800 turns and produces a magnetic field of 2.5 × 10−4 T inside it. Calculate the current flowing through the solenoid. (Use μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T/A)

a. 0.1 A

b. 2.6 A

c. 5.9 A

d. 0.6 A

e. 3.1 A

Answer 0.1 A

Inside a solenoid B = mu0 n I, where n is turns PER METRE, not the total number of turns. First n = N/L = 800/0.4 = 2000 turns per metre. Then I = B/(mu0 n) = 2.5 x 10-4 / (4pi x 10-7 x 2000) = 2.5 x 10-4 / 2.513 x 10-3 = 0.0995, that is 0.1 A. Using 800 instead of 2000 is the mistake this question is built to catch.

0.1 A Correct. n = 800/0.4 = 2000 per metre, so I = B/(mu0 n) = 0.1 A.

2.6 A Wrong. This is roughly what you get using N = 800 directly instead of n = 2000 per metre.

5.9 A Wrong. No route to this value from these numbers.

0.6 A Wrong. No route to this value; it is six times the answer.

3.1 A Wrong. No route to this value from these numbers.

Test 2 Q10terminal voltage

10. A cell has an e.m.f. of 9 V and an internal resistance of 1 Ω. If the current flowing in the circuit is 2 A, what is the terminal voltage of the cell?

a. 7 V

b. 5 V

c. 9 V

d. 11 V

e. 4 V

Answer 7 V

Terminal voltage is the emf minus what is lost inside the cell: V = E - Ir = 9 - (2 x 1) = 7 V. This is the mirror image of the previous question: there the terminal p.d. was given and the emf wanted, here the emf is given and the terminal p.d. wanted. The terminal voltage is always the SMALLER of the two.

7 V Correct. V = E - Ir = 9 - 2 = 7 V.

5 V Wrong. This subtracts twice the internal drop, as if r were 2 ohms.

9 V Wrong. 9 V is the emf, which is only the terminal voltage when no current flows.

11 V Wrong, and it ADDS the internal drop. Terminal voltage can never exceed the emf.

4 V Wrong. No route to 4 V from a 9 V cell dropping 2 V internally.

Test 2 Q11what induces an emf

11. A changing magnetic ________ induces an emf.

Fill the blank. Accepted: flux

Answer flux

Flux. Faraday's law says the induced emf equals the RATE OF CHANGE of magnetic flux, not of the field itself: emf = -dPhi/dt. The distinction earns marks, because flux is B times A times cos(theta), so an emf can be induced by changing the field, by changing the area, or merely by rotating the loop, which is exactly how a generator works with a constant magnet.

Test 2 Q12the Biot-Savart law

12. According to the Biot-Savart law, the magnetic field around a current element decreases with the square of the ________.

Fill the blank. Accepted: distance

Answer distance

Distance. The Biot-Savart law carries a 1/r2 factor, so the contribution of a current element falls off with the square of the distance from it, exactly as Coulomb's law does for charge. Do not confuse this with the field of a whole long straight wire, which falls off as 1/r rather than 1/r2, because that is the sum of every element along an infinite line.

Test 2 Q13current from the terminal p.d.

13. A 10 Ω resistor is connected to a battery of internal resistance 5 Ω. The p.d. across the terminals of the battery is 2 V. Estimate the current flowing the resistor after a time of 2 seconds.

a. 0.2 A

b. 0.1 A

c. 0.3 A

d. 0.4 A

e. 0.5 A

Answer 0.2 A

The terminal p.d. is the voltage that appears ACROSS the external resistor, so Ohm's law needs nothing else: I = V/R = 2/10 = 0.2 A. Two pieces of the stem are deliberate distractions: the internal resistance is already accounted for by quoting the terminal p.d. rather than the emf, and the time of 2 seconds does not enter a steady-current calculation at all.

0.2 A Correct. I = V/R = 2/10 = 0.2 A, using the terminal p.d. across the external resistor.

0.1 A Wrong. This is 2/(10 + 5 + 5) or similar double counting of the internal resistance.

0.3 A Wrong. No route to 0.3 A from 2 V and 10 ohms.

0.4 A Wrong. This would need 4 V across the resistor.

0.5 A Wrong. This uses the internal resistance as though it were the external one: 2/5 wrongly halved, or 2 V across 4 ohms.

Test 2 Q14current in a long straight wire

14. At a distance of 5 cm from a long straight wire, the magnetic field measured is 4.0 × 10−5 T. Calculate the current I flowing through the wire. (Use μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T/A)

a. 10 A

b. 20 A

c. 3 A

d. 15 A

e. 12 A

Answer 10 A

For a long straight wire B = mu0 I / (2 pi r), so I = 2 pi r B / mu0. Substituting with r = 0.05 m: I = (2 pi x 0.05 x 4 x 10-5) / (4 pi x 10-7). The pi cancels top and bottom, leaving I = (2 x 0.05 x 4 x 10-5) / (4 x 10-7) = 4 x 10-6 / 4 x 10-7 = 10 A. Cancelling pi before substituting saves the arithmetic.

10 A Correct. I = 2 pi r B / mu0 = 10 A once pi cancels.

20 A Wrong, and exactly double: this drops the 2 in the denominator of the field formula.

3 A Wrong. No route to 3 A from these numbers.

15 A Wrong. No route to 15 A from these numbers.

12 A Wrong. No route to 12 A from these numbers.

Test 2 Q15the transformer turns ratio

15. In one type of transformer, Ns is greater than Np. If Ns = 1000, Np = 100 and Ep = 240 V, what is the value of Es?

a. 2400 V

b. 24 V

c. 240 V

d. 2.4 V

e. 24000 V

Answer 2400 V

The turns ratio equals the voltage ratio: Es/Ep = Ns/Np. So Es = Ep x (Ns/Np) = 240 x (1000/100) = 240 x 10 = 2400 V. The stem tells you Ns is greater than Np, which means this is a STEP-UP transformer, so the answer must be larger than 240 V. That single check eliminates three of the five options before any arithmetic.

2400 V Correct. Es = Ep x Ns/Np = 240 x 10 = 2400 V.

24 V Wrong, and it steps DOWN by ten, which contradicts Ns being greater than Np.

240 V Wrong. This is the primary voltage unchanged, which would need Ns = Np.

2.4 V Wrong. This steps down by a hundred, the opposite of what the stem describes.

24000 V Wrong. This steps up by a hundred, using the turns as 1000 and 10.

What it was all for

Maxwell's Equations, Waves and the Speed of Light

Everything so far has been building to one result. Two constants measured with magnets and static charge, neither of them anything to do with light, combine to give the speed of light exactly.

About 15 minutes. Work every step yourself before reading on. Nothing here is a summary to skim: each box is one small piece of the skill, in order.

The moment two subjects turned out to be one

A number nobody was looking forreason before rule

Start with where the two constants came from.

  1. ε0 was measured by experiments on static charge: how strongly two charges pull on each other.
  2. µ0 was measured by experiments on magnets and currents: how strongly two wires push.
  3. Neither experiment involved light in any way. Nobody shone anything at anything.

Maxwell put his four equations together for empty space and found they gave a wave equation. Every wave equation carries a speed, and this one came out as v = 1 / √(µ0ε0) (µ0 = 4π x 10-7 H/m, ε0 = 8.85 x 10-12 F/m, v in metres per second).

Then he put the numbers in.

Multiply
µ0ε0 = 4π x 10-7 x 8.85 x 10-12
= 1.113 x 10-17
Square root
√(1.113 x 10-17) = 3.336 x 10-9
Invert
v = 1 / 3.336 x 10-9
Answer
v = 2.998 x 108 m/s

Key point

2.998 x 108 m/s is the measured speed of light. Not close to it. It. Two laboratory constants about charge and magnetism had the speed of light hidden inside them all along.

Maxwell wrote that we can scarcely avoid the inference that light consists in the transverse undulations of the same medium which is the cause of electric and magnetic phenomena. He predicted electromagnetic waves from pure mathematics, never saw one, and never built an antenna. Hertz confirmed them in 1887, eight years after Maxwell died.

Say it bluntly

Light IS an electromagnetic wave. Optics and electricity are the same subject.

The Poynting vector, and its useful name

S = (1/µ0) E x B gives the rate at which energy is transported per unit area.

The name is a happy accident: the vector POINTS the way the energy travels, which is the direction of E cross B, at right angles to both fields. Average it over a cycle and you get exactly the intensity I.

Two more forms of the intensity, all equal, and a question may hand you B rather than E: I = E02/(2µ0c) = cB02/(2µ0). Choose whichever matches the amplitude you were given.

E = cB is why B always looks tiny. Dividing by 3 x 108 shrinks the number by eight orders of magnitude, which is why radio engineers quote E and not B.

The plane wave, symbol by symbolthe formula that looks worse than it is

The wave itself is written down like this, and it looks far worse than it is.

Ey = E0 sin(kx - ωt)  and  Bz = B0 sin(kx - ωt)

Take it apart one symbol at a time.

k and omega are the same idea twice

k packs 2π into one wavelength of SPACE. k = 2π/λ
ω packs 2π into one period of TIME. ω = 2πf

One wave, measured two ways: how it repeats as you move along it, and how it repeats as you wait. Divide one by the other and you get back the speed: ω/k = 2πf/(2π/λ) = fλ = c.

Three directions, all at right angles. E along y, B along z, and the wave travelling along x. Hold your thumb and two fingers out at right angles to each other and you have the picture: no two of them ever lie along the same line.

Both fields share the identical sin(kx - ωt). That is not decoration. It means E and B peak together and vanish together, perfectly in step. It is why E = cB holds at every instant and not just on average.

Your turn. A wave has λ = 2 m and f = 1.5 x 108 Hz. Find k and ω, and check the speed.

Wave number
k = 2π/λ = 2π/2 = π per metre

Angular frequency
ω = 2πf = 2π x 1.5 x 108
ω = 9.42 x 108 rad/s

Check the speed
ω/k = 9.42 x 108 / π
= 3.0 x 108 m/s

The speed of light, as it had to be.

Energy density: the two halves are equala symmetry worth one mark

A wave carries energy in BOTH of its fields, and the total energy per cubic metre is:

u = ½ε0E2 + B2/(2µ0) (u the energy density in joules per cubic metre, E and B the instantaneous field values)

Two terms: the first is the energy in the electric field, the second the energy in the magnetic field.

Key point, and it is the thing to remember

Because E = cB and c = 1/√(µ0ε0), those two terms are exactly equal.

The wave carries half its energy electrically and half magnetically, always, everywhere. So you can compute one half and double it.

Notice the family resemblance to two formulas you already know: ½CV2 for a capacitor's electric field and ½LI2 for an inductor's magnetic field. The same two halves, the same squares, and here they simply travel.

Your turn. A wave's electric energy density at some instant is 4 x 10-6 J/m3. What is the total?

The two halves are equal
electric: 4 x 10-6
magnetic: 4 x 10-6
Total
u = 8 x 10-6 J/m3

Just double it. You never needed E, B, ε0 or µ0 at all.

The laser pointer, worked in fulltwo steps, and a square root

A laser pointer emits 5.0 mW of power in a beam with cross-sectional area 3.0 x 10-6 m2. Calculate the intensity, and the electric field amplitude.

Step 1. Convert
5.0 mW = 5.0 x 10-3 W
A = 3.0 x 10-6 m2

Step 2. Intensity is power per unit area
I = P/A
I = 5.0 x 10-3 / 3.0 x 10-6
  = 1667 W/m2

Step 3. Rearrange for the amplitude BEFORE substituting
I = ½ε0cE02
E02 = 2I / (ε0c)
E0 = √(2I / ε0c)

Step 4. Top and bottom separately
top: 2 x 1667 = 3334
bottom: 8.85 x 10-12 x 3.00 x 108 = 2.655 x 10-3

Step 5. Divide, then take the root
3334 / 2.655 x 10-3 = 1.256 x 106
E0 = √(1.256 x 106)
  ≈ 1120 V/m

Why the answer is alarming

1667 W/m2 beats full sunlight, which is about 1000 W/m2, from a pointer drawing only 5 milliwatts. Nothing is powerful here. The energy is simply squeezed into a few square micrometres, and that is exactly why a laser pointer can damage an eye and a 5 mW lamp cannot.

Answer I = 1667 W/m2, and E0 ≈ 1120 V/m

Redo it. Redo it with the beam widened to 3.0 x 10-5 m2, ten times the area. The intensity falls by ten to 167 W/m2, but the amplitude falls only by √10, to about 354 V/m, because E0 is squared in the intensity. Spreading a beam weakens the field more slowly than it weakens the intensity.

Your turnthe radio transmitter

(1) A radio transmitter produces an electromagnetic wave with an electric field amplitude of 0.080 V/m. What is the corresponding magnetic field amplitude?

(2) You double the electric field amplitude of a wave. What happens to its intensity?

(3) What does the Poynting vector give you, and which way does it point?

Answer

(1) Given
E0 = 0.080 V/m
c = 3.0 x 108 m/s

Formula
E = cB, so B = E/c

Substitute
B0 = 0.080 / 3.0 x 108

Answer
B0 = 2.67 x 10-10 T

Check
Tiny, as it must be: dividing by the speed of light shrinks it by eight orders of magnitude. An answer LARGER than 0.080 would mean you multiplied instead of dividing, which is the commonest slip here.

(2) It QUADRUPLES. I = ½ε0cE02, and the field is squared, so twice the field is four times the intensity.

(3) The rate at which energy is transported per unit area, in W/m2. It points along E cross B, which is the direction the wave travels, at right angles to both fields.

Lock it in

Light is an electromagnetic wave

Currents make fields

The two ways to find a field, and when to use eachreason before rule

Every magnetic field in this course comes from a current. There are two tools for working out the field, and choosing between them is the first decision in any question.

The pairing worth seeing

Biot-Savart is the magnetic Coulomb's law. Both fall as 1 over r squared from a single small source, and both must be summed piece by piece for anything bigger.
Ampere's law is the magnetic Gauss's law. Both are always true, both are only USEFUL when there is symmetry, and both work by drawing an imaginary path or surface.

That imaginary path has a name: an Amperian loop is an imaginary closed path chosen to make Ampere's law easy. Exactly like the Gaussian surface, nothing is built, and you pick it so that B is constant along it.

Your turn. Test 2 asks: Ampere's law is most useful for conductors with high what?

Symmetry.

The law is always true
but only SOLVABLE when you can pick a loop
on which B is constant and along the path

A straight wire, a solenoid and a toroid all have that. A bent piece of wire of no particular shape does not, and for that you are back to Biot-Savart.

How to tell the three lookalikes apart in one glance

Wire: 2πr on the bottom, no N.
Loop: 2R on the bottom, and no π anywhere.
Toroid: 2πr on the bottom AND an N on top.

The missing π is the fastest way to spot the loop formula on an option list.

Leave µ0 as 4π x 10-7. The π usually cancels against the π in the formula, which is faster and safer than multiplying it out to 1.2566 x 10-6.

The solenoid and the toroida coil, and a coil bent into a ring

Two definitions the exam asks for directly.

WordDefinitionIts field
SolenoidA long coil of wireUniform inside, and it counts turns per metre
ToroidA solenoid bent into a ringConfined inside the ring, and it counts TOTAL turns
Why a toroid is used at all

Closing the coil into a doughnut traps the field entirely within the ring. There is almost no stray field outside it.

So where a solenoid would interfere with everything nearby, a toroid keeps itself to itself. That is why toroidal cores are chosen in transformers and power supplies where interference matters.

The everyday case for the solenoid: the door lock in a car, the valve in a washing machine, the starter relay under a car bonnet. Pass a current, get a uniform field inside, and a steel core is dragged into it. That is all a solenoid actuator is.

Your turn. A solenoid is 0.5 m long with 1500 turns. What value of n goes into B = µ0nI?

Formula
n = N/L
Substitute
n = 1500 / 0.5
Answer
n = 3000 per metre

Note it went UP, not down. Dividing by a number less than one makes it larger, and 1500 is offered as a trap answer.

n is turns per metre, N is total turnsthe biggest trap in this topic

Test 2 question 9: a solenoid 0.4 m long with 800 turns produces 2.5 x 10-4 T. Find the current.

Put 800 into B = µ0nI and you get the wrong answer, and it is one of the offered options.

n = N/L = 800/0.4 = 2000 turns per metre.

Then I = B/(µ0n) = 2.5 x 10-4 / (4π x 10-7 x 2000) = 2.5 x 10-4 / 2.513 x 10-3 = 0.1 A.

The toroid formula uses total turns, the solenoid formula uses turns per metre. That inconsistency is deliberate on the examiner's part, and the defence is to read WHICH symbol the formula carries before you substitute anything.

Test 2 question 14, and why to keep the pilet the pi cancel

At a distance of 5 cm from a long straight wire, the magnetic field is 4.0 x 10-5 T. Calculate the current. Use µ0 = 4π x 10-7.

Given
r = 5 cm = 0.05 m
B = 4.0 x 10-5 T
µ0 = 4π x 10-7

Formula, rearranged first
B = µ0I/(2πr)
I = 2πrB / µ0

Substitute, and notice the π
I = (2π x 0.05 x 4 x 10-5) / (4π x 10-7)

The π cancels top and bottom
I = (2 x 0.05 x 4 x 10-5) / (4 x 10-7)
  = (0.4 x 10-5) / (4 x 10-7)
  = (4 x 10-6) / (4 x 10-7)
  = 10 A

Answer I = 10 A

Redo it. If you had turned µ0 into 1.2566 x 10-6 first, you would have reached the same answer with four times the arithmetic and four times the chance of slipping. Redo it at r = 10 cm and confirm the current doubles to 20 A, because B falls as 1/r.

Your turnfour checks

(1) Which of the four field formulas has no π in it, and what does that identify?

(2) Ampere's law needs what property of the arrangement?

(3) A solenoid has 600 turns over 0.2 m. What is n?

(4) According to Biot-Savart, the field around a current element falls off with what?

Answer

(1) B = µ0NI/(2R), the field at the centre of a loop. No π in the denominator, which is the fastest way to spot it.

(2) Symmetry. The law is always true but only solvable when you can find a loop on which B is constant.

(3) n = N/L = 600/0.2 = 3000 per metre. Again it goes UP.

(4) The square of the distance, 1/r2, exactly like Coulomb's law. Do not confuse it with the whole straight wire, which falls as 1/r because it sums every element along an infinite line.

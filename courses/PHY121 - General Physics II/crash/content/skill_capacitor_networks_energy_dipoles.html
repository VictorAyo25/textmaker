Series and parallel, and why capacitors are backwards

Wider bucket or deeper stackreason before rule

Two capacitors can be joined in two ways, and the results feel the wrong way round until you see why.

ArrangementFormulaResult
Parallel, side by side across the same two pointsC = C1 + C2They simply ADD
Series, one after the other in the same branch1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2LESS than the smallest

Why parallel adds. Two capacitors side by side are just one bigger pair of plates. More plate area means a wider bucket, and area adds.

Why series shrinks. One after the other, the gaps add up, and d is on the BOTTOM of C = εrε0A/d. A bigger total gap means a smaller capacitance.

Key point, and it is the trap of the topic

Capacitors are backwards from resistors.
Capacitors ADD in parallel.
Resistors ADD in series.
Learn ONE of them properly and flip it for the other. Trying to hold both separately is how people end up guessing.

Your turn. 2 µF and 3 µF in series. Before calculating: must the answer be above 3, between 2 and 3, or below 2?

Below 2.

Series always gives less than the SMALLEST

Check it
1/C = 1/2 + 1/3
= 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6
C = 6/5 = 1.2 µF

1.2 is below 2. If your series answer ever comes out bigger than the smallest capacitor, you forgot to flip the reciprocal back.

Test 1 question 2, the network with the figureread the picture first

C1 = 1 µF and C2 = 5 µF sit one above the other in the same branch. That branch spans the same two terminals as C3 = 8 µF. Find the total capacitance.

Step 1: read the arrangement, before any formula.
C1 and C2 are in the SAME branch, one after the other, so the same charge must pass through both: they are in series.
That branch and C3 span the same two terminals, so they are in parallel.

Step 2: series first.
1/Cs = 1/1 + 1/5
  = 5/5 + 1/5
  = 6/5
Cs = 5/6 = 0.833 µF

Note the flip back at the end. 6/5 was 1/C, so C is 5/6. Forgetting to invert is the commonest slip in the whole topic.

Step 3: then parallel, which simply adds.
C = 0.833 + 8
  = 8.83 µF

Step 4: check.
Adding anything in parallel can only INCREASE the total, so the answer had to be above 8. It is. And the series pair came out below 1, the smaller of the two, as it had to.

Answer 8.8 µF

Redo it. Two wrong answers are offered as traps: 0.8 µF is the series step with C3 forgotten, and 14.0 µF is all three added as though everything were parallel. Redo the problem with all three in series and confirm you get less than 1 µF, which is the sanity check for series.

The energy storedmust memorise

W = ½CV2 = ½QV = Q2/2C, three forms of the same thing.

SymbolMeaningUnit
WEnergy stored in the capacitorjoule, J
CCapacitancefarad, F
VVoltage it is charged to. In the first form it is SQUAREDvolt, V
QCharge storedcoulomb, C

Which form to use: whichever two quantities the question gives you. C and V, use ½CV2. Q and V, use ½QV. Q and C, use Q2/2C. They always agree.

Where the half comes from

The voltage climbed from zero as the capacitor charged. The first drop of charge crossed almost no potential difference, the last crossed the full V, so on average each crossed V/2.

That is why it is ½QV and not QV. And you will meet the identical argument again in ½LI2 for an inductor, where the CURRENT climbed from zero.

The everyday case: a camera flash. A capacitor charges slowly from a small battery over a second or two, then dumps all of that stored W in about a thousandth of a second. The energy was never large; the POWER is, because power is energy divided by time.

The dipoleit comes back three times

You met induced dipoles in the last lesson. Here is the object itself, because the exam defines it and then uses it twice more.

Definition

A dipole is two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance. Plus q at one end, minus q at the other, a distance d apart.

Three formulas go with it, and they follow one after the other.

QuantityFormulaMeaning of the letters
Dipole momentp = qdq the size of ONE charge in C, d the separation in m, p in C m
Torque in a fieldτ = pE sin θE the external field, θ the angle between the dipole and the field, τ in N m
Energy in a fieldU = -pE cos θSame letters, U in joules

Read the two angle formulas together and they tell one story.

  1. At θ = 0, lined up with the field: sin 0 = 0, so no torque. And cos 0 = 1, so U = -pE, the lowest energy. Nothing turns it, and it sits happily.
  2. At θ = 90, across the field: sin 90 = 1, so the torque is at its maximum. The field twists it hard.
  3. At θ = 180, pointing backwards: sin 180 = 0 again, so no torque, but cos 180 = -1 gives U = +pE, the highest energy. Balanced, but ready to flip at the slightest nudge.
The everyday case, and the memory hook

A compass needle. Held across north it feels a strong twist. Released, it swings to north and stops. Once aligned it feels no twist at all and sits at its lowest energy.

Every dipole in this course does exactly that. And when you meet the magnetic dipole τ = mB sin θ in Part E, it is the same sentence with m in place of p and B in place of E.

The energy question, and the halfcover and slide down

Work each step before sliding down.

Test 1 question 15: a capacitor stores 2.25 mJ when charged to 30 V. Find C. Start by rearranging W = half C V squared in symbols. Rearrange it for C, then substitute.

C = 2W/V2 = (2 x 2.25 x 10-3) / 900 = 4.5 x 10-3 / 900 = 5 x 10-6 F = 5 microfarads. Two things to get right: the factor of 2 when you rearrange, and squaring the 30 to 900. Forgetting the half gives 2.5 microfarads, which is an offered option. Same capacitor, same 30 V. How much CHARGE is on it?

Q = CV = 5 x 10-6 x 30 = 1.5 x 10-4 C. Then check with the other energy form: half QV = 0.5 x 1.5 x 10-4 x 30 = 2.25 x 10-3 J, which is the 2.25 mJ we started from. The three energy forms agreeing is a free check on any capacitor answer. If they disagree, one of your numbers is wrong. Why is there a half in the energy formula at all?

Because the voltage climbed from zero as the capacitor charged. The first charge crossed almost no potential difference, the last crossed the full V, so the average was V/2. The same argument produces the half in half L I squared for an inductor, which you will meet in the induction lesson.

Your turnnetworks and dipoles

(1) 4 µF and 12 µF in parallel. Find the total.

(2) The same two in series. Find the total, and check it against the sanity rule.

(3) A dipole sits at 90 degrees to a field. Is the torque a maximum or a minimum, and is it stable?

Answer

(1) Parallel simply adds: 4 + 12 = 16 µF. Bigger than either, as parallel always is.

(2) 1/C = 1/4 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 1/12 = 4/12, so C = 12/4 = 3 µF. Below 4, the smaller of the two, so the sanity check holds. Note the flip back at the end.

(3) Maximum torque, since sin 90 = 1, and it is not stable at all: the field is twisting it hard towards alignment. The stable position is at 0 degrees, lined up, where the torque is zero and the energy is lowest.

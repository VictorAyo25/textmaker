Storing charge

Capacitance, Geometry and Dielectrics

Capacitance came up four times on Test 1. The whole topic rests on one sentence that almost everybody gets wrong at first: charging a capacitor harder does not change its capacitance.

About 15 minutes. Work every step yourself before reading on. Nothing here is a summary to skim: each box is one small piece of the skill, in order.

What capacitance actually measures

The bucketreason before rule

Start with a bucket. A capacitor stores charge, and capacitance is simply how wide the bucket is: how much charge it holds for each volt you apply.

C = Q/V. Charge per volt. (C the capacitance in farads, Q the charge stored in coulombs, V the voltage across it in volts)

One farad is one coulomb per volt. That is the definition of the unit, and it is the formula read aloud.

Now here is the part that catches almost everybody.

  1. Raise the voltage, and you push more charge in.
  2. But the charge rises in exact proportion to the voltage.
  3. So the RATIO does not move. Q went up, V went up, Q/V is unchanged.
Key point

You cannot widen a bucket by pouring more into it. Capacitance is fixed by GEOMETRY: the plate area, the gap, and what sits in the gap.

Look at the parallel-plate formula and you can see it directly:

C = εrε0A/d (A the plate area, d the gap between the plates, εr how many times better than vacuum the filling is)

Your turn. Find Q in that formula. Find V.

Answer: neither is there.

Look at the formula

Not one symbol is a charge or a voltage. That is why a question can give you a capacitor's dimensions and nothing else and still expect an answer.

Say it bluntly

Charging it harder does not change its capacitance.

The sphere is the cleanest proof of the point

C = 4πε0R. Nothing but a radius and two constants. No charge, no voltage, no second plate. A bigger ball is a bigger bucket, and that is the entire story.

Watch the two Cs. Italic C is capacitance, upright C is the coulomb. And εr has no unit at all: it just says how many times the dielectric beats a vacuum, so 7 means seven times the capacitance.

Permittivity, permeability, and the dielectrictwo words that look alike

Two similar-looking words run through the whole course, and mixing them up costs marks in both halves of it.

WordSymbolWhat it measuresBelongs to
PermittivityεHow readily a material permits an electric fieldCapacitors, Gauss, Coulomb
PermeabilityµHow readily a material permits a magnetic fieldSolenoids, wires, Ampere
The hook

ε sounds like Electric. µ goes with Magnetic.
Both words start with permit, because both are asking the same question of a material: how easily do you let this field through?

Now the dielectric, which is where permittivity earns its keep.

  1. A dielectric is simply an insulator placed between the plates: mica, glass, waxed paper, plastic film.
  2. Putting one in always increases the capacitance.
  3. By how much? By the factor εr, so C = εr C0 where C0 was the capacitance with vacuum in the gap.

Your turn. A capacitor of 3 µF in vacuum is filled with a material of εr = 5. What is its capacitance now?

Formula
C = εr C0
Substitute
C = 5 x 3
Answer
C = 15 µF

Always bigger, never smaller. εr is 1 for vacuum and never less than 1 for anything else, so an answer below 3 µF would be impossible.

Why a dielectric helps at all: polarisationthe reason behind the rule

It is worth knowing WHY stuffing an insulator into the gap helps, because the exam asks for the word.

  1. The atoms of the insulator have positive nuclei and negative electron clouds, normally sitting on top of each other.
  2. Switch on the field and each atom stretches very slightly: the positive shifts one way, the negative the other.
  3. Each atom has become a tiny dipole, a pair of equal and opposite charges a small distance apart.
  4. These induced dipoles line up and produce a field opposing the original one, which lowers the voltage across the plates.
  5. Lower V for the same Q means, by C = Q/V, a larger C.
Polarisation

Polarisation is the creation of induced dipoles in an insulator by an applied field. That is the definition, and the chain above is why a dielectric always increases capacitance rather than decreasing it.

The everyday case: rub a balloon on your hair and hold it near a thin stream of water from a tap. The stream bends towards the balloon, although water is neutral overall. The balloon's field polarised the water molecules, and the near end of each is now attracted. Same physics, visible in a kitchen.

Three capacitance questions from Test 1cover and slide down

Work each step before sliding down to the answer.

Test 1 question 6 gives a parallel plate: area 4 x 10-2 m2, dielectric thickness 10-3 m, relative permittivity 7. The formula is C = epsilon-r epsilon-0 A / d. Substitute and find C.

C = (8.854 x 10-12 x 7 x 4 x 10-2) / 10-3. Top: 8.854 x 10-12 x 7 = 6.198 x 10-11, times 4 x 10-2 = 2.479 x 10-12. Divide by 10-3: 2.478 x 10-9 F. A capacitance in nanofarads is believable. Two of the offered options have POSITIVE exponents, which would be a capacitor larger than any ever built. Step 7 kills them instantly. Test 1 question 11: a sphere carries 4.5 nC at 180 V above its surroundings. Find its capacitance.

C = Q/V = 4.5 x 10-9 / 180 = 2.5 x 10-11 F = 25 pF. All the talk of spheres and insulating rods is scenery. The moment you see a charge and a voltage, C = Q/V is the whole question. That sphere: could you have found its capacitance from its RADIUS instead, without knowing the charge or the voltage?

Yes. C = 4 pi epsilon-nought R for an isolated sphere. Radius alone is enough, which is the clearest proof that capacitance is geometry. Two routes to the same number, one through charge and voltage and one through pure geometry. If a question gives you a radius and no charge, that is the route it wants.

Your turnthree quick ones

(1) A capacitor holds 12 µC at 4 V. What is its capacitance?

(2) You double the plate SEPARATION of a parallel-plate capacitor. What happens to C?

(3) What is the difference between permittivity and permeability?

Answer

(1) C = Q/V = 12 x 10-6 / 4 = 3 x 10-6 F, that is 3 µF.

(2) It HALVES. C = εrε0A/d, and d is on the bottom, so doubling it halves C. Plates close together mean a bigger capacitor, which is why real capacitors are rolled up tightly.

(3) Permittivity ε is how readily a material permits an ELECTRIC field. Permeability µ is how readily it permits a MAGNETIC field. ε for electric, µ for magnetic.

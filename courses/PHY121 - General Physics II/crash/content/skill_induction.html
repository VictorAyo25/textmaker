Flux, Faraday, Lenz and Inductance

A steady magnetic field, however strong, produces nothing at all. Everything in this lesson follows from that one sentence, and so does every generator ever built.

Induction: change is the whole word

Why a steady field induces nothingreason before rule

Start with a bicycle dynamo. Freewheel and the lamp dies. Pedal and it lights. The magnet is just as strong either way, so what is the lamp actually responding to?

Motion. Or more precisely, change.

emf = -N dΦ/dt (emf in volts, N the number of turns, Φ the magnetic flux in webers, and dΦ/dt the RATE at which that flux is changing)

  1. Read the dΦ/dt. It is a rate of change, not an amount.
  2. Park a coil inside the strongest steady magnet you can find and it generates nothing.
  3. Move it, turn it, or switch the field off, and it generates.
Key point

CHANGE is the whole word. A steady flux, however large, induces nothing. This is why a generator has to spin, and why a transformer cannot run on DC.

First, what flux is, and its unit.

Φ = BA cos θ (Φ the magnetic flux in webers, B the flux density in tesla, A the area in m2, θ measured from the normal, exactly as in electric flux)

Flux DENSITYBtesla, THow concentrated the field is

So one weber is one tesla square metre, read straight off Φ = BA. And Faraday's law is about the flux changing, not the field, which is a distinction Test 2 question 11 turns on directly.

No change, no emf. The FLUX is what has to change, not the field.

Lenz's law, and why the minus sign is therenature always fights back

Every induction formula in this course carries a minus sign, and it is always the same law wearing the same coat.

Lenz's law

The induced effect OPPOSES the change that caused it.
That is what the minus sign in emf = -N dΦ/dt means, and what the minus sign in the self-induced emf = -L dI/dt means.

Why it must be so, in one argument. Suppose the induced effect REINFORCED the change instead of opposing it. Then the smallest nudge would strengthen itself, which would strengthen itself again, growing without limit and producing energy from nothing. That is impossible, so nature has no choice: it opposes. Lenz's law is conservation of energy in disguise.

The everyday case you can feel. Push a magnet towards a copper ring and you feel resistance, as though the ring were pushing back. It is. The induced current in the ring makes its own field that opposes your approach. Drop a strong magnet down a copper pipe and it falls in slow motion, as though through treacle, for exactly this reason. The work you do against that opposition is precisely the electrical energy that appears in the ring.

The self-induced emf is the same law applied to a coil opposing changes in its OWN current:

emf = -L dI/dt (L the inductance in henries, dI/dt the rate at which the current is changing in A/s)

Which is why a large inductor resists being switched on quickly, and why switching one off can produce a dangerous spike: dI/dt is enormous for that instant.

Read the formula, not the letter. This course reuses letters constantly and the only defence is knowing which quantity the question is about.

Motional emf is Faraday's law in disguise. A rod of length L sliding at speed v sweeps out area at a rate Lv, so the flux through the circuit changes at a rate BLv, and that rate IS the emf. Nothing new is being claimed.

Eddy currentswhy transformer cores are laminated

Faraday's law does not care whether anybody meant to build a circuit. Any conducting loop will do, including loops nobody deliberately made.

Definition

An eddy current is a loop of current induced inside a solid conductor by changing flux. A solid block of metal is full of possible loops, so a changing field sets currents swirling inside it, like eddies in a river.

They matter in two opposite ways.

When they are a nuisanceWhen they are the point
In a transformer core they waste energy as heatAn induction hob heats the pan itself and not the surface
Fix: build the core from thin insulated sheets, which breaks up the loops the eddies would takeEddy-current brakes stop a train with no pads to wear out

That fix has a name: the core is laminated. Slice the block into thin sheets, insulate each from the next, and the wide circular paths the eddies wanted are simply not available any more. It is why an opened transformer looks like a stack of thin plates rather than a solid lump of iron.

Your turn. Why does an induction hob heat a steel pan but not your hand?

Because your hand is a poor conductor.

the changing field induces eddy currents
in anything CONDUCTING placed on it
steel conducts, so the currents flow and it heats
flesh barely conducts, so almost nothing flows

Same field, same law, entirely different result, and it is decided by the conductor-or-insulator distinction from the very first lesson of Part B.

Working with inductioncover and slide down

Work each step before sliding down.

Energy stored in an inductor is W = half L I squared. It mirrors half C V squared for a capacitor, and the half is there for the same reason: the current climbed from zero. Test 2 question 1: a 2 H inductor carries 5 A. Find the stored energy.

W = half x 2 x 5 squared = 1 x 25 = 25 J. The current is SQUARED, the inductance is not. The trap answer is 10 J, which is simply L times I, forgetting both the half and the square. A rod 0.25 m long moves at 4 m/s across a 0.2 T field, cutting the lines squarely. Find the induced emf.

emf = BLv = 0.2 x 0.25 x 4 = 0.2 V. All three simply multiply, with no squares and no constants. Park the rod and v is zero, so the emf is zero. Faraday again: standing still through the strongest field on earth induces nothing. A coil sits in a strong but perfectly steady magnetic field. What emf is induced?

None at all. d-phi by d-t is zero, so the emf is zero, however strong the field is. Every induction question on this paper comes back to that one sentence: change is the whole word.

Your turntwo conceptual gaps and a unit

(1) A changing magnetic ______ induces an emf. Which word, and why not the other one?

(2) Which law is the minus sign in emf = -L dI/dt, and what deeper principle is it?

(3) What is a weber, in terms of the tesla?

Answer

(1) FLUX, not field. Flux is Φ = BA cos θ, so rotating a loop in a perfectly steady field still changes the flux and still induces an emf. Saying field would miss that case entirely, and it is exactly the case a generator uses.

(2) Lenz's law: the induced effect opposes the change that caused it. Deeper down it is conservation of energy, because an effect that reinforced its own cause would make energy from nothing.

(3) One weber is one tesla square metre. The tesla measures flux DENSITY, the weber measures FLUX.

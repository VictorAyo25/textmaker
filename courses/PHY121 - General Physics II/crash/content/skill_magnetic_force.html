Magnetism

Magnetic Force and Circular Motion

One formula with a sine in it, and one consequence: because the force is always sideways, a magnetic field bends a charge into a circle without ever changing its speed.

About 16 minutes. Work every step yourself before reading on. Nothing here is a summary to skim: each box is one small piece of the skill, in order.

Why the path is a circle

A force that only steersreason before rule

Start with a stone on a string. Swing it round your head. The string pulls the stone sideways, towards your hand, never forwards along its path.

What happens to its speed? Nothing. It circles at a constant speed, because a sideways pull can change your direction but never your pace.

A magnetic field does exactly this to a moving charge.

  1. The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity.
  2. A perpendicular force cannot speed anything up or slow it down.
  3. So the charge bends into a circle at constant speed.
Key point

Magnetic fields do NO work. They steer, they never accelerate in the everyday sense, and the kinetic energy stays exactly where it started.

The magnetic force supplies the centripetal force, so qvB = mv2/r, which rearranges to r = mv/(qB) (q the charge, v its speed, B the field in tesla, m the particle MASS in kg, r the radius of the circle).

And because F = qvB sin θ (θ the angle between the velocity and the field), there are two ways to feel no magnetic force at all.

Your turn. Name them both.

Answer: two ways.

1. Stand still
F = qvB sin θ
with v = 0
F = 0

2. Travel along the field
θ = 0, so sin 0 = 0
F = qB x 0
F = 0

A stationary charge in the strongest magnet on earth feels nothing at all.

Say it bluntly

A magnetic field steers. It never speeds anything up.

The relationship worth remembering

A proton is about 1800 times heavier than an electron, and both carry the same size of charge.

So in r = mv/(qB), a proton at the same speed travels a circle roughly 1800 times wider. If a question compares the two, that ratio is usually the whole answer.

And the unit of the field. B is measured in tesla, T. Strictly the tesla is the unit of magnetic flux DENSITY, and that word density matters: it is the field per unit area. In the induction lesson you will meet the weber, which is the unit of the flux ITSELF. Keep them apart from the start, because one exam question turns on exactly that distinction.

The full Lorentz force, read plainly

F = q(E + v x B)
Two forces added, and they behave completely differently.
The qE part acts whether the charge is moving or not, and pushes ALONG the field.
The qv x B part acts only when it moves, and pushes ACROSS both the velocity and the field.

When a question gives only a magnetic field, the E term is zero and you are back to F = qvB sin θ.

Radius, period and frequency: only one of them cares about speedthe cyclotron principle

Three formulas describe the same circle, and the difference between them is the point of the whole topic.

QuantityFormulaDoes v appear?
Radiusr = mv/(qB)Yes. Faster means wider
PeriodT = 2πm/(qB)No
Frequencyf = qB/(2πm)No
Key point, and it sounds impossible until you see it

Speed up the particle and it travels a wider circle, but takes exactly the same time to go round.

Why? Because it has further to travel AND it is travelling faster, and the two effects cancel exactly.

Definitions, since the exam asks for them in words. The cyclotron frequency is the number of revolutions per second of a charge in a magnetic field, and it is independent of speed. The period is the time for one revolution, and it is independent of speed for the same reason.

Why it matters, in one machine. This is what makes a cyclotron work. Particles are pushed a little harder each lap; they spiral outward into wider and wider circles; but they keep arriving back at the accelerating gap at exactly the same instants. So the machine can be driven at ONE fixed frequency from start to finish, and it never has to be retuned. Hospitals use cyclotrons to make the isotopes for medical scans.

f and T are the same fact upside down: f = 1/T. Look at the two formulas and you can see the 2πm and the qB simply swap places.

Your turn. An electron circles at 4 x 106 m/s. You double its speed. What happens to the radius, and what happens to the frequency?

Radius
r = mv/(qB)
v doubles, so r DOUBLES

Frequency
f = qB/(2πm)
no v anywhere in it
so f is UNCHANGED

Twice as far round, twice as fast, same time per lap. That is the cyclotron principle in one line.

Entering at an angle: the helixcosine gives the pitch, sine gives the radius

So far the charge entered perpendicular to the field, and went round a circle. What if it enters at some other angle?

Split the velocity into two parts and treat each separately.

ComponentSizeWhat the field does to itResult
Across the fieldv sin θBends itA circle, radius r = m(v sin θ)/(qB)
Along the fieldv cos θNothing, since sin 0 = 0Steady drift straight down the field

Circle plus steady drift gives a helix: a spiral, like the thread of a screw or a stretched spring.

The pitch is how far it advances along the field in one full turn:

p = v cos θ × T (p the pitch in metres, v cos θ the along-field speed, T the period, which does not depend on speed at all)

The hook

Sine gives the radius. Cosine gives the pitch.
Across the field bends, so that is the SINE component and it sets how wide the spiral is.
Along the field is untouched, so that is the COSINE component and it sets how far the spiral advances.

The everyday picture: charged particles from the sun spiral down the earth's magnetic field lines exactly like this, tight circles drifting steadily along the field, until they crash into the atmosphere near the poles and make the aurora.

Your turn. A charge enters at 30 degrees to the field. Which is bigger, its along-field drift or its across-field circling?

The drift.

cos 30 = 0.866, the along part
sin 30 = 0.500, the across part

At 30 degrees it is mostly travelling ALONG the field, so it makes a long, stretched-out helix with a small radius and a large pitch. At 60 degrees the two swap over.

Read the angle before you reach for the sinetwo questions, two angles

Test 2 uses two different angles in two consecutive skills, and the sines are not interchangeable.

  • Question 2 gives 60 degrees, and sin 60 = 0.866.
  • Question 7 gives 30 degrees, and sin 30 = 0.5 exactly.

Dropping the sine altogether in question 2 gives 1.0 x 106, which is NOT among the options, so the examiner has deliberately made the sine the marked step.

Test 2 question 2, worked in fullrearrange before substituting

A proton experiences a magnetic force of 3.2 x 10-14 N while moving through a magnetic field of 0.2 T. The angle between the velocity and the field is 60 degrees. Calculate the speed. Take q = 1.6 x 10-19 C.

Given
F = 3.2 x 10-14 N
B = 0.2 T
θ = 60 degrees, so sin θ = 0.866
q = 1.6 x 10-19 C
v = ?

Formula, rearranged in symbols first
F = qvB sin θ
v = F / (qB sin θ)

Denominator first, keeping the powers of ten separate
qB sin θ = 1.6 x 10-19 x 0.2 x 0.866
  = 1.6 x 0.2 x 0.866 x 10-19
  = 0.2771 x 10-19
  = 2.771 x 10-20

Divide
v = 3.2 x 10-14 / 2.771 x 10-20
  = 1.155 x 106 m/s

Answer 1.155 x 106 m/s

Redo it. Redo it at 30 degrees instead. sin 30 = 0.5, so the denominator falls and the speed rises to 2.0 x 106 m/s. Redo it again at 90 degrees: sin 90 = 1, giving 1.0 x 106 m/s, which is the answer you would wrongly get by forgetting the sine.

Your turnthe radius question

An electron (9.1 x 10-31 kg, 1.6 x 10-19 C) enters a 0.03 T field perpendicularly at 4 x 106 m/s. Find the radius of its path.

Then: if it entered twice as fast, what would happen to the TIME for one revolution?

Answer

Given
m = 9.1 x 10-31 kg
v = 4 x 106 m/s
q = 1.6 x 10-19 C
B = 0.03 T

Formula
r = mv / (qB)

Top line
9.1 x 10-31 x 4 x 106 = 3.64 x 10-24

Bottom line
1.6 x 10-19 x 0.03 = 4.8 x 10-21

Divide
r = 3.64 x 10-24 / 4.8 x 10-21

Answer
r = 7.58 x 10-4 m

Every option in this one is 7.58 with a different exponent, so it is purely a powers-of-ten question wearing a physics costume. That is Test 2 question 8.

The time for one revolution would not change at all. T = 2πm/(qB) has no v in it. The circle widens, the speed rises, and the lap time stays put.

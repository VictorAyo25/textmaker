Electrostatics

Flux, Gauss, Lines, Sheets and Conductors

Flux questions are among the easiest on the paper if you know where the angle is measured from, and Gauss turns a sphere into a point charge in one line.

About 14 minutes. Work every step yourself before reading on. Nothing here is a summary to skim: each box is one small piece of the skill, in order.

Flux is just how much field goes through

The rain and the hoopreason before rule

Start with rain. Hold a hoop out in falling rain.

  1. Face the hoop straight up and the most rain falls through it.
  2. Tilt it, and less gets through.
  3. Turn it edge on to the rain, and none does.

Nothing about the rain changed. Only the angle of the hoop did. Flux is exactly that idea: how much field passes through a surface.

Φ = EA cos θ (Φ the flux in N m2/C, E the field in N/C, A the area of the surface in m2, θ the angle measured from the NORMAL)

Key point

The angle is measured from the normal, the line sticking straight out of the surface. NOT from the surface itself. Get that backwards and every flux answer inverts.

Your turn. The hoop faces the rain squarely. What is θ, and what is cos θ?

Answer: θ = 0, and cos 0 = 1.

Squarely on means the field goes straight through, so the angle from the NORMAL is zero, not ninety.

Flux, squarely on
Φ = EA cos 0
Φ = EA x 1
= the maximum

Flux, edge on
Φ = EA cos 90
Φ = EA x 0
= 0, nothing gets through

And edge on? θ = 90 degrees, cos 90 = 0, and nothing gets through at all.

Say it bluntly

Straight through is zero degrees, not ninety.

Test 1 question 5one line

A uniform electric field E = 200 N/C passes through a surface of area 2 m2 at 0 degrees. Find the flux.

Given
E = 200 N/C
A = 2 m2
θ = 0 degrees

Formula
Φ = EA cos θ

Substitute
Φ = 200 x 2 x cos 0
  = 200 x 2 x 1
  = 400

Answer 400 N m2/C

Redo it. Redo it at 60 degrees. cos 60 = 0.5, so the flux halves to 200. Redo it at 90 degrees and it is zero, because the field never crosses the surface at all.

Gauss in one sentencemust memorise

Φ = Qenclosed / ε0. Flux out equals charge in, divided by ε0.

SymbolMeaningUnit
ΦTotal flux out through the whole closed surfaceN m2/C
QenclosedThe charge INSIDE the surface. Nothing else countscoulomb, C
ε0Permittivity of free space8.85 x 10-12 F m-1

Three consequences the paper uses:

  • A charge OUTSIDE the surface contributes nothing. Every field line it sends in comes back out, so the ins and the outs cancel exactly.
  • Outside a charged sphere, the field is the same as if all the charge sat at the centre. So E = kQ/r2 with r measured from the centre.
  • Inside a hollow conductor the field is zero, because no charge is enclosed. That is the Faraday cage.
The hook

Gauss only counts what is inside the bag. Whatever is outside can shine straight through it, in one side and out the other, and contribute nothing at all.

The Gaussian surface is imaginarya definition the exam asks for

Gauss's law mentions a closed surface. It is worth being very clear about what that surface is.

Key point

A Gaussian surface is an imaginary closed surface that you draw on the diagram, chosen to make the law easy. Nothing is built. Nothing is placed. It is a line on paper.

Because you choose it, you choose it to suit the symmetry:

Charge arrangementSurface you drawWhy
Point charge, or a sphereA sphere around itE is the same size everywhere on it
Long straight line of chargeA cylinder around the lineE is the same all round the curved side
Flat sheet of chargeA box through the sheetE is the same on the two flat faces

In each case the point is the same: pick a surface on which E is constant, and the integral collapses to E times an area.

You will meet its twin in magnetism. The Amperian loop in Ampere's law is the identical idea: an imaginary path, chosen for convenience, that turns a hard integral into a multiplication. Same trick, different field.

The three standard fields, and how fast each falls offmust memorise

Gauss gives three results the exam expects you to recognise on sight. The pattern is what to learn, not the algebra.

Charge shapeFieldFalls off as
Point chargeE = kQ/r21/r2
Infinite LINE chargeE = λ/(2πε0r)1/r
Infinite PLANE sheetE = σ/(2ε0)Not at all

What the two new letters mean

SymbolMeaningUnit
λCharge per unit LENGTH along the lineC/m

rDistance from the line, and note it is NOT squaredmetre, m
Why the pattern makes sense

It is all about how the field has to spread.
A point spreads over a growing SPHERE, area 4πr2, so the field thins as 1/r2.
A line spreads over a growing CYLINDER, area 2πrL, which grows only with r, so the field thins as 1/r.
A plane does not spread at all, so the field never thins. Look at the formula: there is no r in it anywhere.

The plane result is genuinely surprising, so test it. Stand 1 cm from an infinite charged sheet, then walk back 10 m. The field is identical. That is what makes the uniform field between capacitor plates possible, and it is why E = V/d has no distance-from-the-plate in it.

One more link worth carrying forward: a straight current-carrying wire also falls as 1/r, for exactly the same cylindrical reason. Same geometry, different field.

Why the field inside a conductor is zerothe Faraday cage

Start with the lightning and the car. A car is struck by lightning and the people inside are unharmed. It is not the tyres. It is the metal.

Here is the argument, in four steps.

  1. In a conductor, charge moves freely.
  2. So any excess charge pushes itself apart until it reaches the outer surface, as far from the rest as it can get.
  3. Draw a Gaussian surface just inside the metal. It encloses no charge at all.
  4. Gauss then forces Φ = 0, and so E = 0 everywhere inside.
Key point

E = 0 inside a conductor, and it is not a separate rule to memorise. It is Gauss's law plus the fact that charge on a conductor lives on the outside.

The everyday cases: a car in a lightning storm, a microwave oven's mesh door keeping the microwaves in, a metal cabinet screening sensitive electronics, and the metal-lined bag that protects a computer part in the post. All the same effect, and it has a name: the Faraday cage.

Your turn. A hollow copper sphere sits in a very strong external field. What is the field at its centre?

Zero.

Charge rearranges on the outer surface
until the inside field cancels exactly
no enclosed charge
so no flux
so no field

The strength of the external field does not matter. Stronger field, more rearrangement, still zero inside.

From the centre, not the surfaceTest 1 question 14

"A conducting sphere of radius 0.25 m carries 6 x 10-6 C. Find the field at a point 0.50 m from the centre."

The radius is there for one reason only: to tell you 0.50 m is OUTSIDE the sphere, so the point-charge result applies. It does not enter the arithmetic.

E = 9 x 109 x 6 x 10-6 / (0.50)2 = 54000 / 0.25 = 2.16 x 105 N/C.

Adding the radius to the distance, or subtracting it, both produce offered options. Read whether the distance is from the centre or from the surface.

Your turnfour quick ones

(1) A closed surface encloses 3 µC. A further 10 µC sits just outside it. What is the net flux through the surface?

(2) What is the electric field deep inside a solid copper block sitting in a strong external field?

(3) You double your distance from an infinite line charge. What happens to the field?

(4) You double your distance from an infinite charged sheet. What happens to the field?

Answer

(1) Only the enclosed charge counts.

Given
Q enclosed = 3 x 10-6 C
Q outside = 10 x 10-6 C, which contributes NOTHING
ε0 = 8.85 x 10-12

Formula
Φ = Qenclosed / ε0

Substitute
Φ = 3 x 10-6 / 8.85 x 10-12

Answer
Φ = 3.39 x 105 N m2/C

(2) Zero. Charge rearranges on the surface until the interior field cancels exactly. No enclosed charge, no field. That is the Faraday cage.

(3) It HALVES. A line falls as 1/r, so double the distance is half the field, not a quarter.

(4) Nothing at all. E = σ/(2ε0) has no r in it. Distance from an infinite sheet does not enter.

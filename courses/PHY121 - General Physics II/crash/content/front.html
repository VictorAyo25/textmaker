How to Pass This in Two Sittings

This book is not the syllabus. It is the shortest honest route from knowing nothing to answering every question the examiner has actually set on PHY121, and it is built backwards from the two computer-based tests rather than forwards from the lecture order.

What is in here, and whyread this page once

Sixteen skills, then thirty questions. Each skill is one small piece of the course, taught from zero in the order that gets you a mark fastest. At the back, both computer-based tests are printed in full, answered, and judged option by option.

Nothing is assumed. Every symbol in every formula is named, with its unit, before it is used. Every constant is introduced with the formula it belongs to rather than as a list to memorise. Every worked example is done line by line, with the rearrangement in symbols first and the numbers only at the end.

The one thing to take from the first skillmust memorise

When five options share the same digits and differ only in the power of ten, the exponent IS the question. The physics has already been given to you, and the mark is for handling the conversion. That single pattern appears on both tests.

How to read a skill. Where a box says to cover the page and slide a card down, do it. The teaching is written as a programme: a small step, a small task, then the answer at the head of the next step. Reading ahead defeats it, because the point is not to be told the answer, it is to find out whether you could produce it.

The seven steps, used on every calculation in this bookmust memorise
  1. Read the question twice and underline what is asked for.
  2. List what you know, with symbols and units, and the unknown with a question mark.
  3. Convert everything to SI immediately: centimetres to metres, microfarads to farads.
  4. Choose the formula holding what you know and what you want.
  5. Rearrange in symbols first, and substitute numbers only at the end.
  6. Compute, keeping the powers of ten separate from the front numbers.
  7. Check: is the size believable, is the unit right, does the sanity check hold?

Steps 3 and 7 save the most marks and are the two most often skipped, because they are the two that feel like they are not really doing physics.

What the two tests are actually likeknow this before you start

Fifteen questions each, five options each, and a fill-in-the-gap for concepts only, never for a number. Test 1 was marked 14 out of 15; Test 2 was marked 15 out of 15, so every answer on that paper is confirmed by the examiner.

One question cannot be recovered. Test 1 question 9 is truncated in the capture, its stem ending at "represented by" with its figure missing, and it is the one question marked wrong. It is printed here as it was captured, and its review says plainly that no true answer exists for it. Nothing in this book pretends otherwise.

And one property of Test 2 worth knowing and not relying on: the correct choice was the first option on every single question. That is a real property of that paper, not a transcription slip. It will not save you twice.

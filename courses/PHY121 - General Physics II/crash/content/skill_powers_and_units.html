The floor you cannot skip

Powers of Ten, Prefixes, Constants and the Seven Steps

Almost every mark lost on this paper is lost before the physics starts. Five of the fifteen options in a PHY121 question are usually the SAME digits with different powers of ten, so the exponent is the thing being marked. This lesson makes that automatic.

About 14 minutes. Work every step yourself before reading on. Nothing here is a summary to skim: each box is one small piece of the skill, in order.

Why this comes first

Start with something you already know. A millimetre is a thousandth of a metre. Nobody finds that hard. Every prefix in this course is that same idea wearing a different coat.

Now look at what the examiner actually does with it.

  1. Test 2 question 8 offers you five answers: 7.58 x 10-4, 7.58 x 10-6, 7.58 x 10-2, 7.58 x 10-8, 7.58 x 10-1.
  2. Read them again. The digits are identical. Only the power of ten changes.
  3. So the physics is already given to you. The examiner has decided to mark exactly one thing.
Key point

When five options share the same digits, the exponent IS the question. The mark is not for knowing the formula. It is for handling the powers of ten.

Your turn, before you read on. If a question gives you 8 mg, what is that in kilograms?

Given
8 mg
Step 1, milli means 10-3
8 mg = 8 x 10-3 g
Step 2, a gram is itself 10-3 kg
= 8 x 10-3 x 10-3 kg
Answer
= 8 x 10-6 kg

It needs BOTH conversions. Most people do one and stop.

So far, so good. That single slip is worth a mark on Test 1 question 3, and this lesson exists to make it impossible.

The link worth seeing early

k and ε0 are the same constant twice: k = 1/(4πε0). Put 8.85 x 10-12 into that and you get 8.99 x 109 back. So a question that hands you one has handed you both.

Two of them are electric and one is magnetic. ε with an E sound for electric, µ for magnetic. That pairing is all you need to keep them apart, and in the last lesson of this course the two of them multiply together to give the speed of light.

Leave µ0 as 4π x 10-7. Do not convert it to 1.2566 x 10-6. The π almost always cancels against a π in the formula, and cancelling is faster and safer than multiplying.

The centimetre trapcosts marks every paper

A distance in centimetres squared is the single most expensive slip in this course. Test 1 question 4 gives r = 5 cm. If you put 5 into kQ/r you get a potential 100 times too small, and 20 cm gives one 400 times too small.

5 cm is 0.05 m, and 0.05 squared is 0.0025. Convert the moment you read the number, before you write anything else down. That is step 3 of the seven steps, and it is the step that saves the most marks.

Powers of ten, one step at a timecover the page and slide down

Read the teaching step, do the small task at the end of it, then move to the next frame, which opens with the answer. Do not read ahead.

To multiply powers of ten, ADD the exponents. 103 times 104 is 107. What is 2 x 10-6 multiplied by 3 x 10-3?

6 x 10-9. Multiply the front numbers, 2 times 3 = 6, then add the exponents, minus 6 plus minus 3 = minus 9. To divide, SUBTRACT the bottom exponent from the top one. 105 divided by 102 is 103. What is 8 x 10-4 divided by 4 x 10-7?

2 x 103. Divide the front numbers, 8 over 4 = 2, then subtract, minus 4 minus minus 7 = plus 3. Watch what happens when the front division gives less than 1. 3.64 over 4.8 is 0.758, which is NOT in standard form, so you shift it to 7.58 and drop the exponent by one to compensate. Work out (9.1 x 10-31 x 4 x 106) divided by (1.6 x 10-19 x 0.03), which is Test 2 question 8.

7.58 x 10-4 m. Top: 9.1 x 4 = 36.4, and minus 31 plus 6 = minus 25, so 36.4 x 10-25, that is 3.64 x 10-24. Bottom: 1.6 x 0.03 = 0.048, so 4.8 x 10-21. Dividing: 3.64 over 4.8 = 0.758 and minus 24 minus minus 21 = minus 3, giving 0.758 x 10-3 = 7.58 x 10-4. That last shift, from 0.758 x 10-3 to 7.58 x 10-4, is exactly the step the five options were testing. Which is bigger, 10-4 or 10-6?

10-4. The less negative the exponent, the bigger the number. 10-4 is a ten thousandth, 10-6 a millionth. Keep the front numbers and the powers of ten in two separate columns while you work. That is step 6 of the seven-step method, and it is what stops the exponent slipping.

The seven stepsmust memorise
  1. Read the question twice and underline what is asked for. Marks are lost every paper answering a question that was not set.
  2. List what you know, with symbols and units, and write the unknown with a question mark next to it.
  3. Convert everything to SI immediately: centimetres to metres, microfarads to farads, milligrams to kilograms.
  4. Choose the formula holding what you know and what you want. If no single formula does, you need an intermediate step.
  5. Rearrange in symbols first, and substitute numbers only at the end.
  6. Compute, keeping the powers of ten separate from the front numbers.
  7. Check: is the size believable, is the unit right, does the sanity check hold?
Learn the seven as one word

Read, list, convert, choose, rearrange, compute, check.
Steps 3 and 7 save the most marks and are the two most often skipped, because they are the two that feel like they are not really doing physics.

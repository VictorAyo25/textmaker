## Powers of ten, prefixes and calculator discipline

Almost every quantity in this course is either enormous or minuscule. Handling that cleanly is a skill, and it is worth more marks than any single formula.

TEACH · WHY POWERS OF TEN EXIST

The charge on an electron is 0.000000000000000000160 coulombs. Nobody can read that, let alone multiply by it. So we write it as 1.60 × 10−19 C. The rule is simply: 10n means move the decimal point n places. Positive n moves it right and makes the number bigger. Negative n moves it left and makes the number smaller. That is all a power of ten is: a shorthand for where the decimal point sits.

MUST-MEMORISE · THE THREE INDEX LAWS

10a × 10b = 10a+b     10a ÷ 10b = 10a−b     (10a)b = 10ab

MEMORY HOOK: multiply then add, divide then subtract, power then multiply. Every single

exponent error in this course comes from breaking one of these three.

TEACH · THE PREFIXES YOU WILL MEET

A prefix is just a power of ten wearing a costume. When a question says 5 nC, it is handing you 5 × 10−9 C and hoping you notice.
MEMORY HOOK: going down in thousands from the unit: milli, micro, nano, pico. Each step is

another factor of 10−3. 

TRAP · CENTI IS NOT MILLI

1 cm = 10−2 m, not 10−3 m. This one line costs more marks in this course than any other. A distance of 5 cm is 0.05 m. A plate separation of 10−3 m is 1 mm, not 1 cm. Worse, distance is usually squared in this course (Coulomb's law, Gauss's law), so a factor-of-ten slip in the distance becomes a factor of one hundred in the answer.

WORKED EXAMPLE · CONVERT BEFORE YOU CALCULATEMETHOD

A capacitor plate has area 4 × 10−2 m2 and separation 10−3 m. Before touching a formula, note what these are.

 - Area 4 × 10−2 m2 = 0.04 m2. That is a square of side 20 cm, which is a believable plate. Always ask whether the number is physically sensible. It catches typos.
 - Separation 10−3 m = 1 mm. Believable as a thin dielectric layer. If your separation came out as 1 metre, you converted wrongly.
Everything is already in SI. No conversion needed. Proceed.
Now redo it: a plate of area 25 cm2 separated by 2 mm. In SI these are 25 × 10−4 m2 and 2 × 10−3 m.

Note that cm2 carries a factor of (10−2)2 = 10−4, not 10−2.

RECALL AND CHECK

Without a calculator: what is (2 × 10−6) × (3 × 108)?

Answer

Multiply the fronts: 2 × 3 = 6. Add the exponents: −6 + 8 = 2. So the answer is 6 × 102 = 600.

## Rearranging formulas

You will almost never be given the formula in the direction you need it. Rearranging is therefore not a side skill: it is the skill.

TEACH · THE ONE RULE

An equation is a balance. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. That is the entire theory. Everything else is practice. To free a symbol, undo what is being done to it, using the opposite operation:

 - It is being multiplied? Divide both sides.
 - It is being divided? Multiply both sides.
 - It is added? Subtract from both sides.
 - It is squared? Take the square root of both sides.
 - It is under a fraction bar? Multiply both sides by the whole denominator first.

WORKED EXAMPLE · THREE REARRANGEMENTS YOU WILL NEED CONSTANTLY

The task. Each of these three formulas is given to you in the wrong direction. Make the named symbol the subject in each case.

 - Q = CV.  Make C the subject.
 - V = IR.  Make I the subject.
 - W = ½CV2.  Make C the subject.

Try all three on paper before reading on. These exact rearrangements appear in Modules 2, 3 and in both CBT tests.

 - From Q = CV, make C the subject. C is multiplied by V, so divide both sides by V: C = Q / V. This is the definition of capacitance, used in Module 2 all the time.
 - From V = IR, make I the subject. I is multiplied by R, so divide both sides by R: I = V / R. Ohm's law in the direction you actually need it in Module 3.
 - From W = ½CV2, make C the subject. Multiply both sides by 2: 2W = CV2. Then divide by V2: C = 2W / V2. Do the multiply first. Trying to divide by V2 while the ½ is still attached is where people go wrong.
C = Q/V   ·   I = V/R   ·   C = 2W/V2
Now redo it: from r = mv / (qB), make B the subject. Answer: B = mv / (qr). You will need exactly this

in Module 4.

TRAP · REARRANGE FIRST, SUBSTITUTE SECOND

Put the numbers in last. If you substitute first, you carry long decimals through every line, you cannot see the structure any more, and a single slip is unrecoverable. Rearrange in symbols until the unknown is alone, and only then put numbers in. It is faster and it is far easier to check.

FILL IN THE GAP · REARRANGING FORMULAS

 - To free a symbol you apply the operation to both sides of the equation.
 - You should rearrange in first, and substitute the numbers .
 - To make C the subject of W = ½CV2, you must multiply by 2 you divide by V2.

Answers

1. opposite (or inverse). Undo a multiplication by dividing, a square by rooting.

2. symbols and last. Substituting first drags long decimals through every line and hides the structure.

3. before. Trying to divide by V2 while the ½ is still attached is where people go wrong.

## Scalars, vectors and components

Force, electric field and magnetic field are all vectors. Charge, potential and energy are not. Knowing which is which prevents the most expensive class of error in this course.

TEACH · THE DISTINCTION

A scalar has size only. Charge, mass, potential, energy, capacitance and resistance are scalars. To combine them you simply add the numbers. A vector has size and direction. Force, electric field, and magnetic field are vectors. To combine them you cannot just add the numbers, because direction decides whether they reinforce or cancel. Charge is a scalar even though it carries a plus or minus sign. The sign says what kind of charge it is, not which way it points. This confuses people every year. 

MUST-MEMORISE · ADDING TWO VECTORS

If two vectors act along the same line, add or subtract the sizes directly. If they act at right angles, use Pythagoras for the size and the tangent for the angle:

R = √(A2 + B2)      tan θ = B / A

MEMORY HOOK: square, add, root. It is Pythagoras, because a right-angled triangle is exactly

what two perpendicular vectors make.

TEACH · RESOLVING A VECTOR INTO COMPONENTS

A vector at an angle can always be replaced by two vectors at right angles, one horizontal and one vertical, that together do exactly the same job. For a vector of size A at angle θ to the horizontal:

Ax = A cos θ      Ay = A sin θ

Cosine goes with the side adjacent to the angle, sine with the side opposite. This is used directly in Module 4, where a velocity at an angle to the magnetic field is split into a part along the field and a part across it.

RECALL AND CHECK

Two forces on a charge: 3 N to the right and 4 N upward. What is the size of the net force?

Answer

They are perpendicular, so square, add and root: √(32 + 42) = √25 = 5 N. Not 7 N. Adding the numbers directly is only valid along the same line.

## Trigonometry, and the angles that matter

You need surprisingly little trigonometry here, but you need it reliably.

MUST-MEMORISE · THE FOUR VALUES THAT KEEP APPEARING

MEMORY HOOK: sin 0° = 0 and sin 90° = 1. Those two alone decide most of Module 4. If a charge

moves along the field it feels no force, because sin 0° = 0.

When the slides say a conductor parallel to the field feels no force, that is not a separate fact to memorise. It is just F = BIL sin θ with θ = 0. Likewise the maximum force at 90° is the same formula with sin 90° = 1. One formula, two special cases. Do not memorise three things where one will do.

## Simultaneous equations

Kirchhoff's laws hand you two or three equations in two or three unknowns. This is the machinery for solving them, and it is the whole difficulty of that topic.

TEACH · SUBSTITUTION, THE ONLY METHOD YOU NEED

If you have as many equations as unknowns, you can solve. The reliable method is substitution:

 - Take the simplest equation and make one unknown the subject.
 - Substitute that expression into the other equation. You now have one equation in one unknown, which you can solve.
 - Put that value back into the first equation to get the other unknown.
 - Check both original equations with your two answers. If either fails, you have slipped.

WORKED EXAMPLE · THE KIRCHHOFF SYSTEM FROM MODULE 3SLIDE

Module 3 produces this system. Solve it now, so that when it reappears inside a circuit problem the algebra is not also new.

I1(18) + I3(10) = 50    and    I1 = 1.25 − I3

 - The second equation already has I1 as the subject. Substitute it into the first. Always substitute the one that is already solved. Do not create work.
 - (1.25 − I3)(18) + 10 I3 = 50 Now there is only one unknown left, which is the point of the method.
 - Expand the bracket: 22.5 − 18 I3 + 10 I3 = 50 Multiply both terms inside the bracket by 18. Forgetting the second is the classic slip.
 - Collect: 22.5 − 8 I3 = 50, so −8 I3 = 27.5 −18 + 10 = −8. Watch that sign.
 - I3 = 27.5 / (−8) = −3.44 A A negative current is not an error. It means the real current flows opposite to the direction you assumed. The size is still right.
I3 = −3.44 A, and then I1 = 1.25 − (−3.44) = 4.69 A
Now redo it: check by substituting both values back into I1(18) + I3(10): 4.69(18) + (−3.44)(10) = 84.4

− 34.4 = 50. It balances. Always run this check. It costs ten seconds and it catches sign errors.

TEACH · WHAT A NEGATIVE CURRENT MEANS

When you apply Kirchhoff's laws you must assume a direction for each current before you know the answer. If your assumption was backwards, the algebra tells you so by handing back a negative number. It does not mean you have failed. Report the size, and state that the current runs opposite to the assumed arrow.

FILL IN THE GAP · SIMULTANEOUS EQUATIONS

 - To solve, make one unknown the of the simplest equation, then it into the other.
 - You need at least as many as you have unknowns.
 - A current is not an error. It means the true direction is to the one you assumed.

Answers

1. subject, then substitute.

2. equations. Three unknowns need three equations, or the system cannot be solved.

3. negative, and opposite. Report the magnitude and say the direction is reversed. Do not re-derive everything in a panic.

## Reciprocal arithmetic

Parallel resistors and series capacitors both need this. It is easy, and it is also where a startling number of marks quietly disappear.

TEACH · THE STEP EVERYONE FORGETS

When you compute something of the form

1 / Req = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3

the arithmetic gives you 1/Req, not Req. You must flip it at the end. Stopping one step early is the single most common mistake in circuit questions.

MUST-MEMORISE · THE TWO-ITEM SHORTCUT

For exactly two items, you can skip the reciprocals entirely:

Req = (R1 × R2) / (R1 + R2)

MEMORY HOOK: product over sum. It only works for two. With three or more, go back to the

reciprocals. This shortcut also works for two capacitors in series, since they follow the same reciprocal rule.

WORKED EXAMPLE · BOTH ROUTES, SAME ANSWER

Two resistors, 250 Ω and 150 Ω, in parallel.

 - Product over sum: (250 × 150) / (250 + 150) = 37500 / 400 = 93.75 Ω. Two items, so the shortcut is legal.
 - The long way, as a check: 1/250 + 1/150 = 0.004 + 0.006667 = 0.010667. This is 1/R, not R. Not finished yet.
 - Flip it: R = 1 / 0.010667 = 93.75 Ω. Same answer. This flip is the step people forget.
Req = 93.75 Ω
Sanity check that always works: a parallel combination is always smaller than the smallest resistor

present. Here 93.75 < 150. If your parallel answer ever comes out bigger than the smallest resistor, you

have made an error. This check is free and it never fails.

TRAP · SERIES AND PARALLEL SWAP BETWEEN R AND C

Resistors add in series and use reciprocals in parallel. Capacitors do the exact opposite: they add in parallel and use reciprocals in series. This is not arbitrary and it is explained in Module 2. But be warned now, because applying the resistor rule to capacitors is a guaranteed loss of marks.

FILL IN THE GAP · RECIPROCAL ARITHMETIC

 - The parallel formula gives you 1/Req, so you must it at the end.
 - The product-over-sum shortcut only works for exactly items.
 - A parallel combination is always than the resistor in the group.

Answers

1. flip (invert). Stopping one step early is the single most common error in the course.

2. two. With three or more, go back to reciprocals.

3. smaller, and smallest. This is a free check on every parallel answer you ever produce.

## Proportional reasoning

Many exam questions never give you a number. They ask what happens to one quantity when another doubles. These are free marks if you can read a formula properly.

TEACH · READING A FORMULA AS A STATEMENT ABOUT CHANGE

Look at where a symbol sits and what power it is raised to.

 - On the top: if it doubles, the answer doubles. Directly proportional.
 - On the bottom: if it doubles, the answer halves. Inversely proportional.
 - Squared on the bottom: if it doubles, the answer drops to a quarter. This is the inverse square law, and it governs Coulomb's law.

So in F = kq1q2 / r2, tripling the separation r does not cut the force to a third. It cuts it to a ninth, because r is squared.

RECALL AND CHECK

In a parallel plate capacitor, C = ε0A / d. If you double the plate separation d and also double the area A, what happens to C?

Answer

A is on top, so doubling it doubles C. d is on the bottom, so doubling it halves C. The two effects cancel exactly: C is unchanged.

## The small amount of calculus you need

Two places in this course use calculus. You do not need to be fluent, but you should not be startled by the notation.

TEACH · WHAT THE NOTATION IS TELLING YOU

A derivative, dΦ/dt, is a rate of change. It means: how fast is Φ changing per second. That is all. When Faraday's law says the induced emf equals −N dΦ/dt, it is saying that a faster change of flux induces a bigger voltage. A flux that sits still induces nothing, however large it is. An integral is a total, accumulated in small pieces. When Module 2 charges a capacitor, each extra scrap of charge dq is harder to add than the last, because the capacitor is already charged and pushes back. The integral adds up all those unequal scraps of work. That is where the factor of ½ in the energy formula comes from, and it is why the energy is not simply QV.

MUST-MEMORISE · THE ONE INTEGRAL USED IN THIS COURSE

∫0Q q dq = Q2 / 2

MEMORY HOOK: raise the power by one and divide by the new power. Here q1 becomes q2/2. This

single line is what produces W = Q2/2C for the energy in a capacitor.

FILL IN THE GAP · THE CALCULUS YOU NEED

 - A derivative dΦ/dt is a of change.
 - Faraday's law responds to the of change of flux, not to the size of the flux.
 - The factor of ½ in W = ½CV2 appears because the voltage steadily from zero as the capacitor charged.

Answers

1. rate. How fast Φ changes per second.

2. rate. A huge steady flux induces exactly nothing.

3. rose (increased). So the average voltage you worked against was only V/2, which is where the half comes from.

## Circles, areas and the geometry you need

Gauss's law and Ampere's law both work by wrapping a surface or a loop around something. That means areas and circumferences.

MUST-MEMORISE · THE FOUR GEOMETRIC FACTS THIS COURSE USES

MEMORY HOOK: the 4πr2 in the sphere's area is precisely the 4π that appears in Coulomb's law.

That is not a coincidence: it is Gauss's law telling you that the field spreads over the surface of a sphere.

TEACH · WHY THIS MAT TERS MORE THAN IT LOOKS

When Module 1 derives the field of a point charge from Gauss's law, the entire derivation is: the flux is E multiplied by the area of a sphere, that equals the enclosed charge over ε0, therefore E(4πr2) = Q/ε0. Rearranging gives Coulomb's law. If you know the surface area of a sphere, that derivation is three lines. If you do not, it is impossible. That is the whole reason this page exists.

2. 2πr. It is the Amperian loop you walk around the wire.

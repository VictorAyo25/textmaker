Q17 · POTENTIAL, FIELD AND WORK9 MARKS

Recap. (a) Distinguish potential from potential energy. [2] (b) A +6.0 µC charge sits at the origin: find (i) the potential at r = 0.30 m, (ii) the field there, (iii) the work done bringing a −2.0 µC charge from infinity to that point. [6] (c) Why is no work done along an equipotential? [1]

 - (a) [2] Electric potential is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to a point. It belongs to the point in space and exists whether or not a charge sits there. Electric potential energy is the energy a particular charge has by virtue of sitting at that point: U = qV. Shelf height versus book on the shelf.
 - (b)(i) [2] V = kQ/r = (8.99×109)(6.0×10−6)/0.30. Plain r, not r squared.
 - V = 1.80×105 V.
 - (b)(ii) [2] E = kQ/r2 = (8.99×109)(6.0×10−6)/(0.30)2 = 5.394×104/0.09. Now r is squared. This is the distinction the question is testing.
 - E = 5.99×105 N/C. Note E = V/r here: 1.80×105/0.30 = 6.0×105. A useful check.
 - (b)(iii) [2] W = qV = (−2.0×10−6)(1.80×105). Keep the negative sign on the charge.
 - W = −0.36 J. Negative, because a negative charge is attracted toward the positive source. The field does the work for you; you do not have to push.
 - (c) [1] Because W = qV and along an equipotential the potential difference V is zero, so W = 0. Equivalently, the force is perpendicular to the motion.
(i) 1.80×105 V  ·  (ii) 5.99×105 N/C  ·  (iii) −0.36 J   Method: §1.3

 - Now Cp is in series with C₃ = 8.0 µF. Two items, so product over sum: Ceq = (16 × 8)/ (16 + 8) = 128/24.
 - Ceq = 5.33 µF. Sanity check: smaller than the smallest in the series pair (8.0 µF). Correct.
 - (b) [2] Q = CeqV = (5.33×10−6)(24) = 1.28×10−4 C = 128 µC.

Q20 · INDUCTION AND HELICAL MOTION9 MARKS

Recap. (a) State Faraday's second law and Lenz's law, and explain why Lenz's law must describe an opposition. [3] (b) A proton enters a 0.40 T field at 30° with speed 2.0 × 106 m/s: find (i) the magnetic force, (ii) why the path is helical and its radius, (iii) the pitch. [6]

 - (a) [3] Faraday's second law: the magnitude of the induced emf is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux, ε = −N dΦ/dt. Lenz's law: the induced current flows in the direction that opposes the change in flux producing it.
 - Why it must oppose: if the induced current assisted the change, the flux would grow, inducing more current, which would grow the flux further, without limit. You would get energy from nothing. Lenz's law is therefore forced by conservation of energy, and it is why you must do work to turn a generator.
 - (b)(i) [2] F = qvB sin θ = (1.6×10−19)(2.0×106)(0.40)(sin 30°). sin 30° = 0.5 exactly.
 - qvB = 1.28×10−13, times 0.5 gives F = 6.4×10−14 N.
 - (b)(ii) [2] Resolve the velocity. The perpendicular component v sin 30° feels a force always at right angles to the motion, producing circular motion. The parallel component v cos 30° feels no force, since sin 0 = 0, so it continues unchanged, carrying the proton steadily along the field. A circle plus a steady drift is a helix.
 - Radius uses only the perpendicular component: vperp = (2.0×106)(0.5) = 1.0×106 m/ s. Sine for the radius. Using the full v is the classic error.
 - r = m vperp/(qB) = (1.67×10−27)(1.0×106) / [(1.6×10−19)(0.40)] = (1.67×10−21)/ (6.4×10−20) = 2.61×10−2 m.
 - (b)(iii) [2] Period: T = 2πm/(qB) = 2π(1.67×10−27)/(6.4×10−20) = 1.64×10−7 s.
 - Parallel speed: vpar = (2.0×106)(cos 30°) = (2.0×106)(0.866) = 1.732×106 m/s. Cosine for the pitch.
 - Pitch = vpar × T = (1.732×106)(1.64×10−7) = 0.284 m.
(i) F = 6.4×10−14 N  ·  (ii) helical; r = 2.61×10−2 m  ·  (iii) pitch = 0.284 m  Method: §4.1
Independently recomputed: F = 6.400×10−14 N, r = 0.02610 m, pitch = 0.2840 m. Remember the

rule that unlocks every helix question: sin for the radius, cos for the pitch.

SPACED-REPETITION CHECKPOINT

Close the book. From memory:

 - Q2: when do fields at a midpoint cancel, and when do they add?
 - Q16: two forces at right angles. How do you combine them?
 - Q19: what is the first move when solving three simultaneous circuit equations?
 - Q20: sine or cosine for the radius of a helix? And for the pitch?

Answer

Fields cancel at the midpoint between like charges and add between opposite ones. Perpendicular forces combine by Pythagoras, never by adding. For simultaneous equations, make one unknown the subject and substitute to reduce to a single unknown. For a helix, sine gives the radius, cosine gives the pitch. QUESTION 11 · ELECTROSTATICS

(a) The force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them: F = kq1q2/r2, where F is force in N, q are the charges in C, r is the separation in m, and k = 8.99 × 109 N m2/C2.

 - (b) Convert: r = 20 cm = 0.20 m, q1 = 3.0 × 10−6 C, q2 = 5.0 × 10−6 C (magnitudes). The centimetre conversion. Always the first thing to do.
 - F = (8.99 × 109)(3.0 × 10−6)(5.0 × 10−6) / (0.20)2. Numerator: (8.99 × 109)(1.5 × 10−11) = 1.349 × 10−1. The two charges multiply to 1.5 × 10−11.
 - Denominator: (0.20)2 = 0.04. So F = 0.1349 / 0.04 = 3.37 N. 0.2 squared is 0.04, not 0.4.
 - The charges have opposite signs, so the force is attractive.
 - (c) Potential is a scalar, so simply add the two contributions with their signs. The midpoint is 0.10 m from each. No vectors here. This is why potential is easier than field.
 - V1 = kq1/r = (8.99 × 109)(+3.0 × 10−6)/0.10 = +2.697 × 105 V.
 - V2 = (8.99 × 109)(−5.0 × 10−6)/0.10 = −4.495 × 105 V. Keep the negative sign. This is where the marks are.
 - V = V1 + V2 = 2.697 × 105 − 4.495 × 105.
(b) F ≈ 3.37 N, attractive  ·  (c) V ≈ −1.80 × 105 V
Common error: using magnitudes in part (c). Potential carries a sign, and here the negative charge

dominates because it is larger, so the midpoint potential is negative. Field would need vector addition;

potential does not.

QUESTION 12 · CAPACITANCE

Recap. (a) Plates of area 0.02 m2, separated by 1.0 mm of air: find C. (b) A dielectric of εr = 5 fills the gap: find the new C. (c) Charged to 12 V: find the charge and the energy stored. (d) Why does a dielectric increase capacitance?

 - (a) C0 = ε0A/d, with d = 1.0 mm = 1.0 × 10−3 m. Millimetres to metres. A factor of 1000.
 - C0 = (8.85 × 10−12)(0.02) / (1.0 × 10−3) = (1.77 × 10−13) / (10−3) = 1.77 × 10−10 F.
 - (b) With the dielectric: C = εrC0 = 5 × 1.77 × 10−10 = 8.85 × 10−10 F. Bigger, as a dielectric must always make it.
 - (c) Charge: Q = CV = (8.85 × 10−10)(12) = 1.06 × 10−8 C.
 - Energy: W = ½CV2 = ½(8.85 × 10−10)(144) = 6.37 × 10−8 J. 122 = 144. Square the voltage before multiplying.
 - (d) The dielectric polarises: its molecular dipoles align and partially cancel the field between the plates. The voltage therefore falls for the same stored charge, and since C = Q/V, a smaller V means a larger C. (a) 1.77 × 10−10 F  ·  (b) 8.85 × 10−10 F  ·  (c) Q = 1.06 × 10−8 C, W = 6.37 × 10−8 J

Cross-check the energy using W = ½QV = ½(1.06 × 10−8)(12) = 6.37 × 10−8 J. The two forms

agree, as they must.

QUESTION 13 · CIRCUIT ANALYSIS

Recap. A 24 V battery drives a 6 Ω resistor in series with a parallel combination of 4 Ω and 12 Ω. Find (a) the total resistance, (b) the total current, (c) the current through the 4 Ω.
 - (a) Collapse the parallel pair first. Two items, so product over sum: Rp = (4 × 12)/(4 + 12) = 48/16 = 3 Ω. Check: 3 Ω is less than 4 Ω, the smaller of the pair. Correct.
 - That 3 Ω is in series with the 6 Ω, so they add: Rtotal = 6 + 3 = 9 Ω.
 - (b) Total current: I = V/R = 24/9 = 2.67 A.
 - (c) All 2.67 A passes through the 6 Ω resistor, so the voltage dropped across it is V = IR = (2.67)(6) = 16 V. The 6 Ω is in series, so it carries the whole current.
 - The voltage left for the parallel section is 24 − 16 = 8 V. Or directly: V = I × Rp = 2.67 × 3 = 8 V. Same answer.
 - Both parallel branches share that 8 V, so the current in the 4 Ω is I = V/R = 8/4 = 2.0 A.
(a) 9 Ω  ·  (b) 2.67 A  ·  (c) 2.0 A through the 4 Ω
Check: the 12 Ω branch carries 8/12 = 0.67 A. The two branch currents are 2.0 + 0.67 = 2.67 A, which

equals the total current. It balances, and the smaller resistor carries the larger current, exactly as it

must.

QUESTION 14 · MAGNETISM AND INDUCTION

Recap. (a) An electron enters a 0.20 T field at right angles at 3.0 × 106 m/s: find the force and radius. (b) Why does the field do no work? (c) A 150-turn coil: flux 0.05 to 0.01 Wb in 0.20 s. Find the induced emf.

 - (a) At right angles, sin 90° = 1, so F = qvB = (1.6 × 10−19)(3.0 × 106)(0.20). Fronts: 1.6 × 3 = 4.8, times 0.2 gives 0.96. Exponents: −19 + 6 = −13.
 - F = 9.6 × 10−14 N.
 - Radius: r = mv/(qB) = (9.11 × 10−31)(3.0 × 106) / [(1.6 × 10−19)(0.20)]. Electron mass here, not proton. Numerator: 2.733 × 10−24. Denominator: 3.2 × 10−20.
 - r = 8.54 × 10−5 m.
 - (b) The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity. Work is W = Fd cos  θ, and here θ = 90°, so cos 90° = 0 and W = 0. Therefore the kinetic energy, and hence the speed, remains constant. Only the direction changes.
 - (c) ΔΦ = 0.05 − 0.01 = 0.04 Wb. ε = N ΔΦ/Δt = (150)(0.04)/(0.20) = 6/0.20. The change in flux, not either value alone.
 - ε = 30 V in magnitude.
(a) F = 9.6 × 10−14 N, r ≈ 8.54 × 10−5 m  ·  (b) No work, so speed and kinetic energy are constant  ·  (c) 30 V
Note: in (c) the minus sign from Lenz's law is dropped because the question asks for the magnitude.

Mention that the sign indicates opposition to the change, and you will secure the mark.

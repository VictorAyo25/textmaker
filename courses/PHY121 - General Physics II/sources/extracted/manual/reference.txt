## The must-memorise list

If you memorise nothing else, memorise these. Each carries the hook that makes it stick.

ITEMTHE HOOK
 | Like repels, unlike attracts | Opposites attract. Everything electrostatic starts here.
 | q = ne | Charge comes in coins, not in liquid. Never a fraction of an electron.
 | F = kq1q2/r2 | Inverse square. Triple the distance, force falls to a ninth.
 | E = kQ/r2 but V = kQ/r | Field is squared, potential is not. Field is a vector, potential is a scalar.
 | Superposition | One at a time, then add. Works for forces, fields and potentials.
 | Flux out = charge in / ε0 | Gauss. External charges contribute nothing.
 | E = 0 inside a conductor | No charge inside means no field inside. The Faraday cage.
 | C = Q/V | Charge per volt. Fixed by geometry, not by Q or V.
 | W = ½CV2 | The half is there because voltage climbed from zero as you charged it.
 | Capacitors add in parallel | Backwards from resistors. Remember one, flip for the other.
 | V = IR | Cover the one you want and the formula rearranges itself.
 | R = ρL/A | Long and thin resists; short and fat does not.
 | Parallel R is less than the smallest | Free check on every parallel answer. If not, you forgot to flip the reciprocal.
 | KCL is charge, KVL is energy | Junctions conserve charge; loops conserve energy.
 | F = qvB sin θ | A stationary charge feels nothing: v = 0. Along the field, sin 0 = 0.
 | Magnetic fields do no work | They steer but never speed up. Kinetic energy is constant.
 | f = qB/2πm | No v in it. The cyclotron principle.
 | ε = −N dΦ/dt | CHANGE is the word. A steady flux induces nothing.
 | Lenz opposes | Conservation of energy. Nature always fights back.
 | V = E − Ir | Why headlights dim when you start the engine.

## A general problem-solving method

MUST-MEMORISE · THE SEVEN STEPS

 - Read the question twice. Underline what is asked for. Many marks are lost by answering a question that was not set.
 - List what you know, with symbols and units. Write the unknown with a question mark.
 - Convert everything to SI immediately. Centimetres to metres, milliamps to amps, microfarads to farads. Do this before anything else.
 - Choose the formula that contains what you know and what you want. If none does, you need an intermediate step.
 - Rearrange in symbols first, and substitute numbers only at the end.
 - Compute, keeping track of powers of ten separately from the front numbers.
 - Check. Is the size believable? Does it have the right unit? Does the sanity check apply (parallel less than smallest, voltages summing to the source)?

MEMORY HOOK: read, list, convert, choose, rearrange, compute, check. Step 3 and step 7 are the

two that save the most marks, and they are the two that are most often skipped.

## Glossary

TERMMEANING
 | Ampere (A) | Unit of current. One coulomb per second.
 | Amperian loop | An imaginary closed path chosen to make Ampere's law easy.
 | Branch | A single element in a circuit, such as a resistor or a source.
 | Capacitance | Charge stored per volt applied. Unit: farad.
 | Conductor | A material in which charge moves freely.
 | Coulomb (C) | Unit of electric charge.
 | Cyclotron frequency | Revolutions per second of a charge in a magnetic field. Independent of speed.
 | Dielectric | An insulator between capacitor plates. Increases capacitance.
 | Dipole | Two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.
 | Doping | Adding impurities to a semiconductor to make it conduct.
 | Eddy current | Loops of current induced inside a solid conductor by changing flux.
 | emf | Energy per unit charge given by a source. Measured in volts, despite the name.
 | Equipotential | A surface on which the potential is the same everywhere.
 | Farad (F) | Unit of capacitance. One coulomb per volt.
 | Gaussian surface | An imaginary closed surface chosen to make Gauss's law easy.
 | Insulator | A material in which charge is bound and cannot move freely.
 | Internal resistance | The resistance of a source itself. Causes terminal voltage to fall below emf.
 | Junction (node) | A point where two or more conductors meet.
 | Loop | A closed conducting path in a circuit.
 | Ohm (Ω) | Unit of resistance. One volt per amp.
 | Ohmic | Obeying Ohm's law, with resistance constant at fixed temperature.
 | Permittivity | How readily a material permits an electric field. Symbol ε.
 | Permeability | How readily a material permits a magnetic field. Symbol µ.
 | Polarisation | Creation of induced dipoles in an insulator by an applied field.
 | Quantisation | Charge exists only in whole multiples of e.
 | Resistivity | A material's intrinsic opposition to current. Symbol ρ. Unit Ω m.

 | Semiconductor | A material between conductor and insulator, whose resistance falls as it heats.
 | Short circuit | A path of zero resistance, which carries all the current.
 | Solenoid | A long coil of wire, producing a uniform field inside.
 | Superposition | Compute each contribution separately, then add.
 | Terminal voltage | The voltage across a source while current flows. Always less than the emf.
 | Tesla (T) | Unit of magnetic flux density.
 | Toroid | A solenoid bent into a ring.
 | Transformer | Two coils coupled by mutual induction. Changes voltage. AC only.
 | Weber (Wb) | Unit of magnetic flux. One tesla square metre.

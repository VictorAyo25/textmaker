Magnetic fields
A magnetic field is a vector field that describes the
magnetic influence on moving electric charges, electric
currents, and magnetic materials. A moving charge in a
magnetic field experiences a force perpendicular to both
its velocity and the magnetic field. it’s symbol “B”. Unit
= Tesla, 1T = 1 𝑁.𝑠
𝐶.𝑚

SLIDE 4
Properties
• Magnetic field lines form closed loops.
• Direction of field lines: from the North to the South pole outside a 
magnet.
• Field lines never intersect.
• Strength is denoted by the density of lines (closer = stronger field).
Sources of Magnetic Fields
• Moving charges (currents)
• Permanent magnets
• Changing electric fields (Maxwell’s extension)

SLIDE 5
Magnetic field B can be represented by field 
lines, and as with electric field lines, the 
direction of the field is indicated by the 
direction of the field line, and the magnitude 
of the field is indicated by the density of 
lines.
There are two important differences:
1. Electric field lines are in the direction of 
the electric force on a positive charge, but 
magnetic field lines are perpendicular to the 
magnetic force on a moving charge.
2. Electric field lines begin on positive 
charges and end on negative charges; 
magnetic field lines neither begin nor end.

SLIDE 18
Lorentz Force
A charged particle moving in a magnetic field is like a car
driving straight that suddenly hits a side wind pushing it off
course.
•The velocity of the particle is the car’s movement.
•The magnetic field is the side wind.
•The force from the magnetic field pushes the car sideways —
the path becomes circular or spiral, not straight!

SLIDE 20
Motion of charged particles in 
magnetic fields
• Magnetic fields influence only moving electric charges. Unlike 
electric fields, magnetic fields cannot change the speed (kinetic 
energy) of a charged particle because the magnetic force is always 
perpendicular to the direction of motion.
• Instead, the magnetic field changes the direction of motion, producing 
circular, helical, or spiral trajectories.
• This principle is the basis of many modern technologies, including:
• Particle accelerators 
• Cyclotrons 
• Mass spectrometers 
• Cathode ray tubes 
• Magnetic confinement fusion 
• Electron microscopes

Cyclotron Frequency
• Cyclotron frequency (also called the gyrofrequency) 
is the number of complete circular revolutions per 
second made by a charged particle moving 
perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field.
• When a charged particle (such as an electron or 
proton) enters a magnetic field at right angles, the 
magnetic force acts as the centripetal force, causing 
the particle to move in a circular path. The rate at 
which the particle completes one full revolution is 
called the cyclotron frequency.

Biot–Savart law
• Whenever an electric current flows through a 
conductor, it produces a magnetic field around it. This 
phenomenon was first discovered in 1820 by the Danish 
physicist Hans Christian Ørsted, who observed that a 
compass needle deflected when placed near a 
current-carrying wire.
• The quantitative relationship between a current 
element and the magnetic field it produces was later 
developed independently by the French physicists Jean-
Baptiste Biot and Félix Savart in 1820. This relationship 
is known as the Biot–Savart Law.
• The law is fundamental in electromagnetism because it 
allows us to calculate the magnetic field produced by 
any current distribution.
ccodel.covenantuniversity.edu.ng
43
============================================================
SLIDE 44
• The Biot–Savart Law states that the magnetic 
field at a point due to a small current element is 
directly proportional to the current and the 
length of the current element, directly 
proportional to the sine of the angle between 
the current element and the line joining the 
element to the point, and inversely proportional 
to the square of the distance between them.

Ampère's Law
• Whenever an electric current flows through a 
conductor, it creates a magnetic field around it. While 
the Biot–Savart Law can calculate the magnetic field 
produced by any current distribution, the calculations 
may become complicated for complex geometries.
• To simplify these calculations for systems with high 
symmetry, the French physicist André-Marie Ampère 
formulated Ampère's Circuital Law in 1826.
• Ampère's Law relates the circulation of the magnetic 
field around a closed path to the total electric current 
enclosed by that path.
• It is one of the four fundamental equations of classical 
electromagnetism known as Maxwell's Equations.

SLIDE 72
INTRODUCTION
• Electromagnetic induction is the process by which an electromotive 
force (emf) is generated in a conductor due to a change in the 
magnetic field surrounding it.
• This phenomenon was discovered in 1831 by the English scientist 
Michael Faraday, who found that a changing magnetic field could 
induce an electric current in a nearby conductor without direct 
electrical contact.
• Electromagnetic induction is the fundamental principle behind the 
operation of:
• Electric generators 
• Transformers 
• Induction motors 
• Wireless charging systems 
• Dynamic microphones 
• Metal detectors 
• Induction cookers

SLIDE 73
ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION
• Electromagnetic induction is the production of 
an emf (and, in a closed circuit, an electric 
current) whenever the magnetic flux linking a 
conductor changes.
A current is induced only if:
• The magnetic field changes. 
• The conductor moves relative to a magnetic 
field. 
• The area of the conducting loop changes. 
• The orientation of the conductor changes.

SLIDE 80
Electromotive force
Electromotive force, energy per unit electric charge that is 
imparted by an energy source, such as an electric generator or a 
battery. 
• Energy is converted from one form to another in the generator or 
battery as the device does work on the electric charge being 
transferred within itself. 
• One terminal of the device becomes positively charged, the other 
becomes negatively charged. 
• The work done on a unit of electric charge, or the energy thereby 
gained per unit electric charge, is the electromotive force. 
• Electromotive force is the characteristic of any energy source 
capable of driving electric charge around a circuit. It is 
abbreviated E in the international metric syste, but also popularly, 
as emf.
============================================================
SLIDE 81
Unit of electromotive force
The unit of electromotive force is the volt (V). If 
one coulomb of charge is driven through a 
potential difference of one volt, the work done is 
1 joule.
Electrical work in joules is the product of charge 
and voltage.

of energy.

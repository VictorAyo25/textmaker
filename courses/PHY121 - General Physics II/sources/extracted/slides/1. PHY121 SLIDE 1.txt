INTRODUCTION
• Everything around us, from the way a balloon sticks to a wall after
rubbing, to lightning flashing across the sky, is governed by a few
basic forces in nature.
• These forces are the “rules” that explain how objects in the
universe interact with each other. Scientists have identified four
fundamental forces: gravity, the electromagnetic force, the strong
nuclear force, and the weak nuclear force.
• Among these, the electromagnetic force is the one we encounter
most often in daily life, because it is responsible for electricity,
magnetism, and even light.

SLIDE 6
• Electrostatics is a branch of physics that focuses on electric charges at
rest. In simple terms, it studies what happens when electric charges are
at rest and interact with each other.
• Even though the charges are not moving, they can still exert a force on
each other through space. This invisible interaction is what we call the
electric force.
• At the heart of electrostatics is the idea of electric charge. Charge is a
basic property of matter, and it comes in two types: positive and
negative. Like charges repel, while opposite charges attract.
• One important fact about charge is that it cannot disappear or be
created from nothing; it can only move from one object to another.
Another key idea is that charge exists in small fixed amounts, rather
than any random value, which means it is “quantized.”

SLIDE 7
• Electrostatics also helps us understand how materials behave.
Conductors allow charges to move freely, while insulators hold
charges in place.
• This difference is very important in designing electrical devices,
protecting sensitive equipment, and even in technologies like
capacitors that store electrical energy.
• Beyond everyday experiences, electrostatics plays a major role in
modern science and technology.
• It is used in devices such as photocopiers, air purifiers, medical
imaging tools, and many electronic systems. It also forms the
foundation for more advanced topics in physics, including electric
circuits, magnetism, and electromagnetic waves.

ELECTRIC CHARGES and its properties 
• Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter responsible for 
electrical interactions.
• It is the basis of electricity, magnetism, and electrostatics.
• Electric charge is the property of matter that causes it to experience 
force in an electric field.
• Symbol: Q or q
• Unit: Coulomb (C)

Properties of charges 
1. additivity of charge
• Total charge is the algebraic sum of individual charges:
• Q_total = q1 + q2 + q3 + ...
2. conservation of charge
• Charge cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred.
• Total charge in an isolated system remains constant.

3. Quantization of Charge
• Charge exists in discrete units:
• q = ne, where e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
• 4. Invariance of Charge
• The magnitude of charge does not depend on:
i. Speed of the object ii. Reference frame 
iii. Physical conditions (temperature, pressure) 
• A charge remains the same whether it is moving or at rest.

• Electric charge is a scalar quantity:
• It has a magnitude only 
• It does not have a direction 
• Signs (+/−) indicate type, not direction
• 6. Transferability of Charge
• Charge can be transferred between bodies through:
• Conduction (direct contact) 
• Induction (without contact) 
• Friction (rubbing)

INTRODUCTION
•In electrostatics, electric charges exert forces on one 
another. When charges interact, work is done, and 
energy is either stored or transferred. 
•The concepts of electric potential and electric potential 
energy help explain how electrical energy behaves in 
electric fields.
•Electric potential is analogous to gravitational potential 
in mechanics. 
•Just as an object at a height possesses gravitational 
potential energy, a charge in an electric field possesses 
electric potential energy.

Equipotential Surfaces
• An equipotential surface is a surface where all points have the same 
electric potential.
• Properties of Equipotential surfaces
1. No work is done when moving a charge along an 
equipotential 
surface. 
2. Electric field lines are perpendicular to 
equipotential surfaces. 
3. Equipotential surfaces never intersect.

electrostatics in Electromagnetism. 
•It relates the electric field around a charged 
object to the total charge enclosed within a 
surface 
• It provides a powerful method for 
calculating electric fields, especially when 
there is symmetry (spherical, cylindrical, or 
planar).

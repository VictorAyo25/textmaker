Module Two · Unit 1

Fish Farming, Investment and Water Quality

What fish farming is, the parts of a fish farm, why it is worth investing in, and the single most tested topic in the course: the quality of the water.

What fish farming is

Must memoriseDefinitions

Aquaculture is the controlled cultivation or rearing of aquatic plants and animals in a water environment for commercial, recreational, and scientific purposes.

Fish farming is a branch of aquaculture, known as Pisciculture, that deals with the science and art of rearing fish.

Science and art: "art" is the fill-in answer (Test 2 asked "the science and ______ of rearing fish"). Pisciculture is the one-word name for fish farming.

PickPractice · cf. Test 1 Q13

What is the basic definition of fish farming given in the lecture note?

  • The science and art of rearing fishes
  • The study of high population growth in Nigeria
  • The importation of 1.9 million metric tons of seafood
  • The construction of car washes and restaurants

Answer a. Straight from the definition. The distractors are deliberately silly, the examiner's habit when the correct answer is a definition you either know or you do not.

The five departments of a fish farm

Must memoriseFive departments
  • Hatchery (the "labour room"): production of baby fish, the fingerlings and juveniles.
  • Grow-out: rearing fingerlings and juveniles to mature, marketable adult size.
  • Processing: adding value to the adult fish.
  • Feed mill (the "kitchen"): production of the fish feed.
  • Administrative: running the day-to-day operation of the farm.

The two nicknames are exam gold: Hatchery = labour room, Feed mill = kitchen. Both have been asked as fill-in questions.

Answers 1. hatchery   2. kitchen.

Why it pays: importance and investment

TeachText 2.1.2, 2.1.3

Fish gives high protein and low cholesterol (its fat is mostly heart-healthy Omega-3), and farming it makes fish seed available all year, easing the pressure on wild stocks. Beyond rearing, the value chain has many investment points: fish marketing, breeding (a hatchery), processing, fish feed production (feed is 60 to 70% of production cost), pond construction, consultancy, transport, training, aquaponic and integrated farming, and agri-tech.

Must memoriseIndices for exploitation

The reasons (indices) Nigeria's fish-farming landscape is worth exploiting:

  • High population
  • High demand for fish
  • Insufficient production of farmed fishes
  • Abundant water and land resources
  • Low supply of fishes

High population + high demand + insufficient production: the test asks which combination is the "primary driver", and pairs high population with insufficient production.

Fill in the gapPractice · cf. Test 2 Q13, Q14

1. The high demand for fish in Nigeria is partly driven by its population.

2. farming combines fish production with the cultivation of plants in a linked water-based system.

Answers 1. high   2. Aquaponic.

Water quality: the three parameters

Must memoriseDefinition and the big three

Water quality is the optimal physical, chemical, and biological parameters of water that influence fish health, growth, and productivity: the standard of water that lets the fish thrive.

There are exactly three groups of parameters. Knowing which group a measurement belongs to is asked directly, so learn the members of each group cold.

TeachThe numbers that get asked
  • pH: ideal 6.5 to 8.5 for most fish.
  • Dissolved oxygen (DO): minimum 5 to 8 mg/L. Low DO stresses and kills.
  • Ammonia: toxic above 0.05 mg/L. Nitrite: keep below 0.1 mg/L. Nitrate: above 50 mg/L affects health. Carbon dioxide: above 20 mg/L suffocates.
  • Iron: safe below 0.1 mg/L, moderate risk 0.1 to 0.5, high risk above 0.5 mg/L.
  • Total Bacterial Count (TBC): "high risk" is above 1.0x105 CFU/mL.
TrapPractice · cf. Test 1 Q1

The dissolved oxygen question offers 6.5 to 8.5 mg/L as an option. That is the pH range wearing a mg/L unit. The DO answer is 5 to 8 mg/L. Two ranges, two parameters: mixing them up is the whole trap. Learn the pair together so you can never be baited: pH 6.5 to 8.5, DO 5 to 8.

PickPractice · cf. Test 2 Q5, Q26

A technician measures ammonia, salinity, pH, hardness and dissolved minerals. Under which group do these fall?

  • Physical parameters
  • Chemical parameters
  • Operational parameters
  • Biological parameters

Answer b, chemical. Ammonia, pH and dissolved minerals are chemistry. Its partner question gives high temperature and cloudy water from suspended particles, which are physical (turbidity, colour, temperature, suspended solids). "Operational parameters" is not one of the three groups, so it is always wrong.

Answers 1. 8.5   2. water   3. poor. Note on 3: the test says "total plate count", but the course text only uses "Total Bacterial Count (TBC)". Same idea, different name; answer "poor" regardless, and do not go hunting for "plate count" in the text.

Aquarium management

Must memoriseThe maintenance schedule
  • Setup: substrate and decorations, condition the water, seal the glass, and know your fish species (do not stock an aggressive fish with a timid one).
  • Daily: feed small amounts one to two times, avoid overfeeding (it causes water pollution); observe the fish.
  • Weekly: partial water changes (20 to 30%), check parameters, clean the glass and decorations.
  • Monthly: filter maintenance, inspect equipment.
  • Long-term: quarantine new fish, trim or replace plants.
PickPractice · cf. Test 2 Q9

An aquarist wants to avoid stocking highly aggressive or incompatible species together. Which setup step directly addresses this?

  • Know the fish species
  • Condition the water
  • Seal the glass
  • Install substrate and decorations

Answer a. Compatibility is a species question. The others are real setup steps but address leaks, chemistry and habitat, not aggression.

Recall and check

Cover the answers. (1) Name the three water-quality parameter groups. (2) What is the DO minimum, and the pH range? (3) How often are partial water changes done? (4) What does overfeeding cause?

Answers (1) Physical, chemical, biological. (2) DO 5 to 8 mg/L; pH 6.5 to 8.5. (3) Weekly (20 to 30%). (4) Water pollution.

Module Two · Unit 2

Behaviour, the 3M Model and Marketing

Fish "talk" through behaviour; wealth is built through the 3M model; and none of it pays until the fish is marketed. All three are tested.

Reading fish behaviour

TeachText 2.1.6

Fish express themselves through behaviour, and the test gives you a described behaviour and asks for the condition or the remedy. There are four patterns. Learn the tell-tale sign and the first remedy for each.

PatternTell-tale signsFirst remedy
Inability to eatSlow, gasping at the surface, rejecting feedChange the polluted water; treat sickness; correct parameters
Hungry fishesBegging at the surface, lunging at food, swimming to youFeed to satiation to avoid cannibalism; grade by size
Slow movementSluggish, near the bottom, reduced response to stimuliIncrease aeration (air stones, filters, air pumps)
StressFaded colour, clamped fins, hiding in a secluded cornerMaintain water quality; stable temperature; acclimatise new fish
PickPractice · cf. Test 1 Q7, Q17

1. A fish holds its fins clamped tight, hovers in a secluded corner, and has a faded body colour. The most likely condition is:

  • Chronic hunger
  • Natural hibernation
  • Acute overfeeding
  • Stress

2. Fish move sluggishly and respond little to food or movement (the "slow movement" pattern). A recommended remedy is:

  • Increase netting to check for parasites
  • Discontinue all feeding for 48 hours
  • Increase aeration using air stones, filters, or air pumps
  • Move all fish to a smaller, more confined tank

Answers 1. d, Stress (clamped fins + faded colour + hiding is the signature). 2. c, aeration. Note the absolutes in the wrong options: "all feeding", "all fish", "a smaller, more confined tank" (which would worsen it).

The 3M of creating wealth

Must memoriseMake, Manage, Multiply

The 3M of creating wealth in fish farming is Make, Manage, and Multiply. Make is the science (the foundation); Manage and Multiply are the art (the business).

Three Ms. "Make" is the science; a question asks which of Make / Movement / Money / Market is one of the 3Ms, and the answer is Make.

Must memoriseThe ten MAKE principles

MAKE has ten principles, "like the fingers of your hand, working together":

  • Water, Land, Security, Location, Fisheries knowledge and manpower, Source of fingerlings/juveniles, Feed availability and quality, Market, Construction, Finance.

These ten are the confirmed answer to "which elements belong to the MAKE component": location, construction, feed, water, land, technical know-how and security.

TeachManage and Multiply

MANAGE keeps the money from shrinking: keeping records, financial prudence, communication skills, diligence, and effective human resources management (a code of conduct, policies, regulations, discipline, training).

MULTIPLY builds many streams: technological input, leverage and networking, the marketing mix (7Ps), the 4Cs of marketing, investment, reward mechanisms, and an integrated approach. Before all this comes research: a SWOT analysis and a SMART analysis.

Must memorise7Ps and 4Cs

7Ps of marketing: product, promotion, place, price, physical evidence (packaging), people, process (positioning).

4Cs of marketing: customer, cost, convenience, communication. (The customer is king.)

The "multiplying effect" needs the 7Ps and 4Cs together; SWOT and SMART are the research pair. Do not confuse the two pairs.

PickPractice · cf. Test 1 Q11, Q26 · Test 2 Q4, Q28

Marketing fresh and processed fish

Must memoriseMarket, brand, value

Marketing needs good product quality, strong branding, and smart sales strategies. The first step is to identify your target market:

  • Local consumers: households, restaurants, hotels, supermarkets.
  • Wholesale buyers, online shoppers, and the export market.

Build a strong brand with a Unique Selling Point (USP), attractive packaging, and Hygiene and Quality Assurance. Add value with smoked, spiced, fillet, frozen, or marinated (barbeque) fish.

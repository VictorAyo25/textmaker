Module Four · Unit 1

Identifying Agribusiness Opportunities

What makes an agribusiness idea worth pursuing, the chain that adds value from input to consumer, and the problems that get in the way.

Must memoriseSix evaluation criteria

An agribusiness opportunity is a potentially profitable business idea in agriculture. Judge one against six criteria:

  • Market Demand, Cost of Inputs, Profit Margin, Scalability, Environmental and Social Impact, Policy and Regulatory Compliance.
Must memoriseThe agricultural value chain

Each step adds value, from farm to consumer:

  • Input Supply (seeds, fertilisers, machinery)
  • Production (crop cultivation or livestock rearing)
  • Harvesting and Primary Processing (sorting, grading, drying)
  • Storage and Packaging (warehousing, cold storage)
  • Transportation and Logistics
  • Secondary Processing / Value Addition (milk into cheese, maize into flour)
  • Marketing and Sales, Retail and Consumption, Support Services
TeachProblems and trends

Common problems in agriculture: limited market access (reliance on middlemen), post-harvest losses (poor storage and cold chains), low productivity, input supply challenges, and poor extension services.

Current trends: precision agriculture (GPS, IoT, data analytics), urban and vertical farming, water-conservation and irrigation technology, and data-driven decision making.

A business model in disguise

PickPractice · cf. Test 2 Q30

A commercial farm wants to use its physical assets to provide educational weekend experiences for urban families. Which business model should it explore?

  • Agricultural waste management (composting, biofuel)
  • Agritech (software, databases, marketplaces)
  • Agritourism (visitors learn about farming, pick produce, or stay on a working farm)
  • Food processing (raw materials into processed goods)

Answer c, agritourism. "Educational experiences for families on the farm" is the definition of agritourism. The other three are real models, just not the one described. Match the scenario to the definition.

Module Four · Unit 2

Value Addition and Innovation

Value addition is the whole point of agripreneurship: change a product into a more valuable state. Learn the six types, the three forms, and the four kinds of innovation.

Must memoriseDefinition, types, forms

Value addition is changing or transforming a product from its original state to a more valuable one (longer shelf life, better quality, new uses), by altering its place, time, or form.

Six types: Form, Place, Time, Convenience, Packaging and Branding, Nutritional.

Three forms: Primary (cleaning, sorting, grading), Secondary (drying, milling, juicing), Tertiary (packaging, labelling, retailing).

TeachInnovation

Agribusiness innovation adopts new ideas, methods, or technologies. Four kinds: Product (fortified flour), Process (solar dryers, precision farming), Marketing (e-commerce), and Organisational (cooperatives, contract farming). Drivers: technology, changing consumer preferences, competition, and government policy.

PickPractice

Turning fresh tomatoes into paste, and milk into cheese, is which type of value addition?

  • Form value addition
  • Place value addition
  • Time value addition
  • Packaging value addition

Answer a. Changing the physical form is Form. Place = moving it somewhere more valuable; Time = storing for off-season; Packaging = presentation.

Module Four · Unit 3

Production and Operations Management

Turning inputs into valuable outputs efficiently. Learn the difference between production and operations, and the POCD scope, which is the most likely question here.

Must memoriseProduction, operations, POCD

Production is growing crops and rearing animals (the what and how of creating goods). Operations is broader: planning, input management, processing, logistics, and quality control, managing the whole path from input to customer.

Scope, by the POCD framework:

  • Planning: decide what, when, and how to produce.
  • Organising: structure operations, labour, and logistics.
  • Controlling: monitor performance, manage inventory, reduce waste, ensure quality.
  • Directing: supervise and guide workers and processes.
TeachAdvantages, limits, technology

Advantages: higher productivity and profit, better quality and consistency, better resource use, and the ability to scale. Disadvantages: unpredictable weather, labour shortages, price fluctuations, poor infrastructure, and post-harvest losses.

Technology in operations: precision agriculture (drones, sensors, GPS tractors), IoT and smart farming, farm-management software, and automated machinery.

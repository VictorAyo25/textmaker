DTS224 Data Management I. Time: 2 hours. Answer Question One (compulsory) and any three others. Each question carries 17½ marks.

Question OneCompulsory

(a) Explain, with symbolic examples: generalization, specialization, subtype discriminator, completeness constraint. (6)

(b) StudentGrade(matricNo, courseCode, sName, cTitle, grade) has matricNo → sName and courseCode → cTitle. Identify all determinants and the type of dependency each gives. (4)

(c) Over Sales(saleID, item, amount, staffID, region): write SQL to (i) list sales above 5000, largest first; (ii) total sales per region; (iii) average sale per staff; (iv) sales of the item 'Laptop'; (v) staff with sale over 20000; (vi) regions with no sales recorded. (7½)

Question Two

(a) Explain two limitations of the file-based approach. (4)

(b) What is a DBMS, and what are the three levels of data abstraction? (6½)

(c) A hospital EER has WARD (wardNo, name), PATIENT (patientNo, name, multivalued allergy), and an ADMISSION (date, bed) linking each patient to one ward. Transform it into relational schemas. (7)

Question Three

(a) Would you store data in a database rather than operating-system files? Justify with two points, and say when a database would not be worth it. (7½)

(b) A university library lends BOOKs to MEMBERs. A member may borrow many books over time; each loan records a date out and due date. A member is either a STUDENT (matricNo) or a STAFF (staffNo). Draw the EER diagram. (10)

Question Four

(a) Describe the relational data model. (2½)

(b) Explain six relational algebra operations with examples. (6)

(c) Over Emp(eNo, name, dNo), Dept(dNo, dName, loc): write relational algebra for (i) names of employees in the 'Research' department; (ii) departments in 'Abuja'; (iii) names and locations of all employees. (9)

Question Five

(a) Which of DDL, DML, or the data model represents real-world information? Explain each briefly. (6½)

(b) A bank has CUSTOMERs who hold ACCOUNTs. An account is either a SAVINGS account (interestRate) or a CURRENT account (overdraftLimit), never both. Draw the crow's-foot EER. (11)

Question Six

(a) Explain: relation, degree, cardinality. (3)

(b) Employee(empNo, projNo, eName, pName, hours, deptNo, deptName) has empNo → eName, deptNo; projNo → pName; deptNo → deptName. Draw the FD diagram and normalize to 3NF. (14½)

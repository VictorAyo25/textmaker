Before you start

How to use this manual

Read this page once. It is short, and it decides whether the rest of the manual works for you.

TeachThe bargain

This manual assumes you have never designed a database, never drawn an entity-relationship diagram, and never written a line of SQL. It carries you from there to a full mark in the examination. It is also meant to be enough on its own: you should never need a lecturer, a textbook or a website to follow it. Anything it uses, it teaches first, including its own notation.

In exchange it asks one thing: draw the diagrams and write the queries yourself. Reading a finished ER diagram or a solved normalization creates a feeling of understanding that evaporates in the examination hall, where you face a blank page and a business description. Every diagram in this book was drawn by hand first, and every query was checked against the schema it runs on. Do the same, and the difference between your answer and the one printed here is information.

The boxes

Every page is built from a small set of boxes. Each has one job, and the colour tells you which before you read a word.

Must memorise

Emerald green means learn this by heart. Definitions the examiner asks for word for word, the normal-form rules, the crow's-foot symbols, the six relational algebra operations. It is the only thing this colour is ever used for. If a page has green on it, there is something on that page you are expected to know cold.

WorkedSolved right where it is taught

A real question, solved in numbered steps, then a now you try at the end. Every worked box carries a small chip saying where the question comes from, so you know it is a question that was actually asked, not one invented to be easy:

PQ 25/26 Q6 a past paper (session and question); TEST 1 the class test; DECK the practice slides; TEXT M4U1 the course text's own exercise. Do the "now you try" before moving on.

SQL / Relational algebra

Indigo means a listing you would write on the answer sheet: an SQL statement or a relational algebra expression. It is always in a fixed-width font so the structure is exact. You are expected to be able to reproduce these, not just read them.

Trap

A mistake that is easy to make and costly in marks. Most are taken from the exact places the examiner sets a snare: a partial dependency mistaken for transitive, a NULL that breaks a count, a many-to-many relationship with an attribute stranded on the line.

Recall

Questions with the answers printed underneath, below a dashed line. Cover the answers. Say your answer out loud, or write it, and only then look. Checking whether you knew something is not the same as knowing it, and the difference is the whole point of this box.

Exam question

A question reproduced as it appeared on the paper, plain and uncoloured, so you meet it the way the examiner set it. Its worked answer follows in a teal Answer box.

Reading a figure

Must memoriseThe notation is the exam

Half of this course is drawn, not written. An entity is a rectangle, a relationship a line with a verb on it, and the small marks at the ends of that line, the crow's foot, a circle, a single bar, carry the whole meaning of a business rule. Get a symbol wrong and the answer is wrong, however good the prose around it. Every symbol used in this manual is defined the first time it appears, and the full legend sits in Module Two. Where a figure is a diagram you must be able to draw, redraw it yourself until it comes out without looking.

One promise, and one label

Teach

The promise. Every number, every normalized set of relations, and every query result in this manual was worked out independently, not copied from a source. The course's own materials contain arithmetic and design slips (you will meet one, a table whose printed candidate keys do not match its data), so nothing here is trusted just because a source printed it. Where a source is wrong, this manual says so and shows the correct working.

The label. A few questions in the past papers come from an older, wider syllabus (this course was once taught under a different code with extra "high performance" topics). Where a question or a piece of content is outside the current syllabus, it carries a Not in syllabus label. It is still taught and still solved in full, so that if it reappears you lose no marks, but the label tells you it is not core, so you spend your revision time accordingly.

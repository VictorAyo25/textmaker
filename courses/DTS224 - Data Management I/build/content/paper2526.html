AnswerQuestion One (a) and (b), compulsory

(a) EER terms. Super type: a generic entity type with one or more subtypes (EMPLOYEE over HOURLY and SALARIED). Specialisation: the top-down process of defining subtypes of a supertype. Total specialisation: every supertype instance must belong to at least one subtype (double line). Disjoint rule: a supertype instance may belong to at most one subtype (circle marked d). (Module Two, Unit 2.)

(b) Determinant, from EMPLOYEE2. A determinant is the attribute on the left of a functional dependency. In EMPLOYEE2(EmpID, CourseTitle, Name, DeptName, Salary, DateCompleted):

Determinants: EmpID determines Name, DeptName, Salary (a partial dependency, on part of the key); the composite (EmpID, CourseTitle) determines DateCompleted (the full dependency). This is a partial-dependency situation, so the relation is not in 2NF.

AnswerQuestion Two

(a) Two limitations of the file-based approach. Data redundancy and inconsistency (the same fact is stored and updated in several files); and program-data dependence (a file's structure is coded into every program). (Module One, Unit 2.1.)

(b) DBMS. The software that lets users define, create, maintain, and control access to a database (MySQL, Oracle). (Module One, Unit 2.2.)

(c) Data abstraction, three levels. Hiding storage detail, showing each user only what they need: the external level (per-user views), the conceptual level (all tables and relationships), and the internal level (physical storage). (Module One, Unit 2.5.)

(d) Transform the University Dining EER. Solved at Module Three, Unit 2.4: MENU, DISH, DISH_INGREDIENT, MENU_DISH, EVENT, MENU_EVENT, STAFF (with a SupervisorID foreign key), STAFF_SKILL, and WORK_SCHEDULE.

AnswerQuestion Three

(a) Database vs operating-system files. (i) Choose a database, because it gives one shared, consistent copy with concurrency control, and enforces integrity, security, and data independence centrally, none of which loose files provide. (ii) Not worth it for a small, single-user, single-application task with little data and a fixed structure, where a full DBMS is pure overhead. (Module One, Units 2.1 and 2.7.)

(b) EER for a law firm. Each CASE (caseNumber, dateOpened, dateClosed, judgment) is brought by one or more plaintiffs and is against one or more defendants. A plaintiff or defendant is a LEGAL ENTITY (entityNumber, name, netWorth), which is either a PERSON or an ORGANISATION. Since the same entity can be plaintiff in some cases and defendant in others, draw two many-to-many relationships between LEGAL ENTITY and CASE.

AnswerQuestion Four

(a) The relational data model. It represents all data as relations (tables of rows and columns), with relationships expressed by shared values rather than pointers. (Module Three, Unit 1.)

(b) Six relational algebra operations. Selection (σ, rows), projection (π, columns), union (∪), set difference (−), Cartesian product (×), and join (⋈). (Module Five, Unit 1.1.)

(c) Relational algebra over Emp/Dept/Project/Works_on. Solved at Module Five, Unit 1.4: (i) Headquarters employees by a selection and join; (ii) Abuja projects by joining Project, Dept, and Emp; (iii) employees on all A3/4 projects by division.

AnswerQuestion Five

(a) DDL vs DML vs data model. The one that represents real-world information is the data model: it is the set of concepts (entities, relationships, constraints) used to describe what the data means. DDL defines the structure (CREATE, ALTER, DROP); DML manipulates the data (INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE, SELECT). (Module One, Unit 2.3; Module Three, Unit 1.)

(b) Crow's-foot EER for the technology company. Solved at Module Two, Unit 2.6: OFFERING is total and disjoint over PRODUCT and SERVICE; SERVICE is partial and disjoint over REPAIR, MAINTENANCE, and others; a REPAIR repairs a PRODUCT.

AnswerQuestion Six

(a) Relational terms. Schema: a relation's name and its attributes. Alternate key: a candidate key not chosen as the primary key. Tuple: one row of a relation. (Module Three, Unit 1.)

(b) StaffPropertyInspection. (i) the FD diagram, (ii) transitive dependency, and (iii) the normalization to 3NF are worked in full at Module Four, Unit 1.4. The final 3NF answer: Property(propertyNo, pAddress), Staff(staffNo, sName), and PropertyInspection(propertyNo, iDate, iTime, comments, staffNo, carReg), with staffNo and propertyNo as foreign keys.

A transitive dependency here is staffNo → sName: sName depends on the key only through the non-key attribute staffNo, which is why 3NF moves it into its own Staff relation.

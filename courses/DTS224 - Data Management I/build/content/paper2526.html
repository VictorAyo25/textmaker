AnswerQuestion One

(a) EER terms, with symbolic examples. Super type: a generic entity type holding the attributes common to its subtypes, for example EMPLOYEE over HOURLY and SALARIED. Specialisation: the top-down process of defining subtypes of a supertype (EMPLOYEE specialised into HOURLY and SALARIED). Total specialisation: every supertype instance must belong to at least one subtype, drawn as a double line. Disjoint rule: an instance may belong to at most one subtype, drawn as a circle marked d. (Module Two, Unit 2.)

(b) Determinants in EMPLOYEE2. A determinant is the attribute, or set of attributes, on the left of a functional dependency. In EMPLOYEE2(EmpID, CourseTitle, Name, DeptName, Salary, DateCompleted):

EmpID determines Name, DeptName and Salary: a partial dependency, since EmpID is only part of the key. The composite (EmpID, CourseTitle) determines DateCompleted: a full functional dependency. Because a partial dependency exists, the relation is in 1NF but not 2NF.

(c) The six SQL queries. Solved at length in Module Five, Unit 2.4.

(i)   SELECT * FROM STAFF WHERE salary > 100000 ORDER BY salary DESC;
(ii)  SELECT branch_no, COUNT(*) FROM STAFF GROUP BY branch_no;
(iii) SELECT branch_no, AVG(salary) FROM STAFF GROUP BY branch_no;
(iv)  SELECT * FROM PROPERTYFORRENT WHERE rooms > 3 AND rent > 80000;
(v)   SELECT p.property_no, p.type, p.rent, s.fName, s.lName
      FROM PROPERTYFORRENT p JOIN STAFF s ON p.staff_no = s.staff_no;
(vi)  SELECT branch_no FROM BRANCH
      WHERE branch_no NOT IN (SELECT branch_no FROM STAFF);

The amounts are written 100000 and 80000 in SQL because the Naira sign and the thousands commas are presentation, not data; say so in one line under your answer and you have shown you read the question rather than ignored the currency.

AnswerQuestion Two

(a) Two limitations of the file-based approach. Data redundancy and inconsistency, since the same fact is stored and updated in several files; and program-data dependence, since a file's structure is coded into every program that reads it. (Module One, Unit 2.1.)

(b) A DBMS. The software that lets users define, create, maintain and control access to a database, for example MySQL or Oracle. (Module One, Unit 2.2.)

(c) Data abstraction, three levels. Hiding storage detail so each user sees only what they need: the external level (per-user views), the conceptual level (all tables and relationships), and the internal level (physical storage). (Module One, Unit 2.5.)

AnswerQuestion Three

(a) Database or operating-system files. (i) Choose a database: it gives one shared, consistent copy with concurrency control, and enforces integrity, security and data independence centrally, none of which loose files provide. (ii) It would not be worth it for a small, single-user, single-application task with little data and a fixed structure, where a full DBMS is pure overhead. (Module One, Units 2.1 and 2.7.)

(b) EER for the law firm. Each CASE (caseNumber, dateOpened, dateClosed, judgmentDescription) is brought by one or more plaintiffs and is against one or more defendants. A plaintiff or defendant is a LEGAL ENTITY (entityNumber, name, netWorth), which is either a PERSON or an ORGANISATION. Because the same entity can be plaintiff in some cases and defendant in others, draw two many-to-many relationships between LEGAL ENTITY and CASE. requestedJudgment is a property of the plaintiff relationship, not of the entity, so it sits on that relationship.

AnswerQuestion Five

(a) The choice and the three concepts. (i) The data model. (ii) A data model is the set of concepts (entities, relationships, attributes and constraints) used to describe the structure and meaning of data, so it is the one that represents information about the real world. The data definition language defines that structure to the DBMS (CREATE, ALTER, DROP), and the data manipulation language operates on the data held in it (SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE). Neither language models reality; they realise and query a model. (Module One, Unit 2.3.)

(b) Crow's-foot EER for the technology company. Worked in full at Module Two, Unit 2.6. OFFERING (offeringID, description) is total and disjoint over PRODUCT (productName, standardPrice, dateOfFirstRelease) and SERVICE (unitName, conditionsOfService). SERVICE is partial and disjoint over REPAIR (cost) and MAINTENANCE (hourlyRate), because the paper allows "other types of services". A REPAIR repairs a PRODUCT: optional on the product side (some products never require repair), many on the repair side (many potential repair services for a product). CUSTOMER (customerID, name, address, phoneNumber) purchases OFFERING, an optional many-to-many carrying purchaseDate and contactPerson; and a SERVICE performed is billed to a CUSTOMER, carrying datePerformed, dateDue and amountDue, which is a separate relationship from the purchase because a customer may be billed for a service they did not purchase.

AnswerQuestion Six

(a) The three terms. Schema: the structure of a relation, its name together with its attributes, for example STAFF(staffNo, fName, lName, branchNo). Alternate key: a candidate key that was not chosen as the primary key. Tuple: one row of a relation, one complete record. (Module Three, Unit 1.)

(b)(i) and (iii) are worked step by step at Module Four, Unit 1.4. Taking the attribute as sName throughout, and the key as (propertyNo, iDate):

(ii) Transitive dependency. A non-key attribute depends on the key only through another non-key attribute. Here staffNo → sName: sName is determined by staffNo, and staffNo is itself determined by the key, so sName reaches the key only via staffNo. That is what 3NF removes, by moving sName into a relation of its own keyed on staffNo.

The 3NF result:

Property(propertyNo, pAddress)                   PK propertyNo
Staff(staffNo, sName)                            PK staffNo
PropertyInspection(propertyNo, iDate, iTime, comments, staffNo, carReg)
   PK (propertyNo, iDate)   FK propertyNo -> Property, FK staffNo -> Staff

Steps to state: 1NF, the table already holds single values, so it qualifies once the repeating property address is understood as belonging to the property. 2NF, remove the partial dependency fd2 (propertyNo → pAddress) into Property. 3NF, remove the transitive dependency fd3 (staffNo → sName) into Staff. carReg is left on the inspection because fd4 makes it depend on staffNo together with iDate, which is not a determinant available in either split relation.

TeachHow to spend the three hours

Question One is compulsory and mixes EER terms, a functional-dependency reading and SQL, so it rewards broad preparation. For your three chosen questions, pick the ones whose long parts you are surest of: the transforms (Q2d, Q5b), the algebra (Q4c) and the normalisation (Q6b) each carry the most marks and are the most mechanical once learned. Draw every diagram large and label every cardinality; an unlabelled line scores little.

Watch the mark text as you triage. This paper does not write marks the same way twice: most parts read "mks", Question Three alone reads "marks" and prints 2.5 as a decimal, and Question Six drops to the singular "mk". None of that changes what a part is worth, but a candidate skimming for the big numbers can misread 2.5 as 25.

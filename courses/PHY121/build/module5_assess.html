Exercise 1 · Displacement current

Question. The electric field between two parallel plates of area 2.0 × 10⁻² m² is increasing at a steady rate of 5.0 × 10¹² V/(m·s). Find the displacement current between the plates.

  1. Between plates ΦE = EA, so Id = ε₀ dΦE/dt = ε₀ A (dE/dt).
  2. Substitute: Id = (8.85 × 10⁻¹²)(2.0 × 10⁻²)(5.0 × 10¹²).
  3. Multiply the fronts: 8.85 × 2.0 × 5.0 = 88.5. Add exponents: −12 − 2 + 12 = −2.
I_d = 88.5 × 10⁻² = 0.885 A

Now redo it: a well-under-one-amp displacement current from a very fast-changing field. Sanity: the units cancel to (F/m)(m²)(V/(m·s)) = (C/V·m)(m²)(V/m·s) = C/s = A. Always let the units confirm the shape.

Exercise 2 · Wavelength from frequency

Question. A microwave oven works at 2.45 GHz. What is the wavelength of its radiation in vacuum?

  1. Use c = fλ, rearranged to λ = c/f.
  2. Convert: 2.45 GHz = 2.45 × 10⁹ Hz. Substitute: λ = (3.0 × 10⁸)/(2.45 × 10⁹).
λ = 0.122 m ≈ 12 cm

Now redo it: 3.0/2.45 = 1.224, and 10⁸/10⁹ = 10⁻¹, giving 0.1224 m. A ~12 cm wave, comparable to the spacing of the metal grid in the oven door, which is what keeps the microwaves inside.

Exercise 3 · Amplitudes and energy

Question. An electromagnetic wave has an electric field amplitude E₀ = 60 V/m. Find (a) the magnetic field amplitude, (b) the intensity, and (c) the energy density (using u = ε₀E²).

  1. (a) B₀ = E₀/c = 60 / (3.0 × 10⁸) = 2.0 × 10⁻⁷ T.
  2. (b) I = ½ε₀cE₀² = ½(8.85 × 10⁻¹²)(3.0 × 10⁸)(60)².
  3. Compute: (60)² = 3600; ½ × 8.85 × 3.0 = 13.275; 13.275 × 10⁻⁴ × 3600 = 4.78.
  4. (c) u = ε₀E₀² = (8.85 × 10⁻¹²)(3600) = 3.19 × 10⁻⁸ J/m³.
B_0 = 2.0 × 10⁻⁷ T I = 4.78 W/m² u = 3.19 × 10⁻⁸ J/m³

Now redo it: all three confirmed independently. Note B₀ is ten-million times smaller than E₀. The factor of c again.

Exercise 4 · From intensity back to the field

Question. Sunlight at the Earth's surface has an intensity of about 1000 W/m². Estimate the electric field amplitude E₀ and magnetic field amplitude B₀ of sunlight.

  1. Rearrange I = ½ε₀cE₀² for E₀ = √( 2I/(ε₀c) ).
  2. Substitute: E₀ = √( 2(1000) / [(8.85 × 10⁻¹²)(3.0 × 10⁸)] ) = √( 2000 / 2.655 × 10⁻³ ).
  3. = √(7.53 × 10⁵) = 868 V/m. Then B₀ = E₀/c = 868/(3.0 × 10⁸) = 2.89 × 10⁻⁶ T.
E_0 ≈ 868 V/m B_0 ≈ 2.89 × 10⁻⁶ T

Now redo it: E₀ = 867.9 V/m confirmed. A few hundred volts per metre and a few microtesla. Sunlight is a surprisingly strong field, spread over a whole hemisphere of sky.

Exercise 5 · Field rate in a charging capacitor

Question. A capacitor with circular plates of radius 5.0 cm is charged by a steady current of 3.0 A. At what rate is the electric field between the plates changing?

  1. The displacement current equals the conduction current: Id = 3.0 A.
  2. Plate area: r = 5.0 cm = 0.050 m, so A = πr² = π(0.050)² = 7.85 × 10⁻³ m².
  3. From Id = ε₀A(dE/dt), rearrange: dE/dt = Id/(ε₀A) = 3.0 / [(8.85 × 10⁻¹²)(7.85 × 10⁻³)].
dE/dt = 4.32 × 10¹³ V/(m·s)

Now redo it: ε₀A = 6.95 × 10⁻¹⁴, and 3.0/6.95 × 10⁻¹⁴ = 4.32 × 10¹³. Smaller plates than the 10 cm example, so a larger dE/dt is needed for the same current, since the flux is spread over less area.

A general problem-solving method

Must-memorise · the seven steps
  1. Read the question twice. Underline what is asked for. Many marks are lost by answering a question that was not set.
  2. List what you know, with symbols and units. Write the unknown with a question mark.
  3. Convert everything to SI immediately. Centimetres to metres, milliamps to amps, microfarads to farads. Do this before anything else.
  4. Choose the formula that contains what you know and what you want. If none does, you need an intermediate step.
  5. Rearrange in symbols first, and substitute numbers only at the end.
  6. Compute, keeping track of powers of ten separately from the front numbers.
  7. Check. Is the size believable? Does it have the right unit? Does the sanity check apply (parallel less than smallest, voltages summing to the source)?

MEMORY HOOK: read, list, convert, choose, rearrange, compute, check. Step 3 and step 7 are the

two that save the most marks, and they are the two that are most often skipped.

Glossary

TERMMEANING
Ampere (A)Unit of current. One coulomb per second.
Amperian loopAn imaginary closed path chosen to make Ampere's law easy.
BranchA single element in a circuit, such as a resistor or a source.
CapacitanceCharge stored per volt applied. Unit: farad.
ConductorA material in which charge moves freely.
Coulomb (C)Unit of electric charge.
CyclotronRevolutions per second of a charge in a magnetic field. Independent of
frequencyspeed.
DielectricAn insulator between capacitor plates. Increases capacitance.
DipoleTwo equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.
DopingAdding impurities to a semiconductor to make it conduct.
Eddy currentLoops of current induced inside a solid conductor by changing flux.
emfEnergy per unit charge given by a source. Measured in volts, despite the name.
EquipotentialA surface on which the potential is the same everywhere.
Farad (F)Unit of capacitance. One coulomb per volt.
Gaussian surfaceAn imaginary closed surface chosen to make Gauss's law easy.
InsulatorA material in which charge is bound and cannot move freely.
Internal resistanceThe resistance of a source itself. Causes terminal voltage to fall below emf.
Junction (node)A point where two or more conductors meet.
LoopA closed conducting path in a circuit.
Ohm (Ω)Unit of resistance. One volt per amp.
OhmicObeying Ohm's law, with resistance constant at fixed temperature.
PermittivityHow readily a material permits an electric field. Symbol ε.
PermeabilityHow readily a material permits a magnetic field. Symbol µ.
PolarisationCreation of induced dipoles in an insulator by an applied field.
QuantisationCharge exists only in whole multiples of e.
ResistivityA material's intrinsic opposition to current. Symbol ρ. Unit Ω m.
SemiconductorA material between conductor and insulator, whose resistance falls as it heats.
Short circuitA path of zero resistance, which carries all the current.
SolenoidA long coil of wire, producing a uniform field inside.
SuperpositionCompute each contribution separately, then add.
Terminal voltageThe voltage across a source while current flows. Always less than the emf.
Tesla (T)Unit of magnetic flux density.
ToroidA solenoid bent into a ring.
TransformerTwo coils coupled by mutual induction. Changes voltage. AC only.
Weber (Wb)Unit of magnetic flux. One tesla square metre.
